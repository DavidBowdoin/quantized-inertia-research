# Causal restriction is not spectral truncation: a no-go result for the Rindler–Casimir mechanism of Quantised Inertia (QI)

**Citation:** Junghanns, J. (2026, 13 August). *Causal restriction is not spectral truncation: a no-go result for the Rindler–Casimir mechanism of quantised inertia*. Zenodo preprint, version 2 ("Heavily revised and expanded"), https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21923680. Concept DOI covering all versions: https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21895660. CC BY 4.0 (from the Zenodo and DataCite records). The stored PDF is the Zenodo file named "… submission to MNRAS.pdf" (10 pages, MNRAS LaTeX style, "Preprint 13 August 2026", "Accepted XXX. Received YYY"). Page numbers below refer to it. Version 1 (https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21895661, 12 August 2026) had the title "Why Quantised Inertia is Dead On Arrival".

**TL;DR:** Junghanns is an independent researcher based in Milan. He argues that the 2013 "asymmetric Casimir" mechanism of QI fails at the quantum-field-theory step it depends on. A Rindler horizon only restricts which observables an accelerated observer can access (the algebra of the Rindler wedge). It does not remove field modes or act as a reflecting boundary. Restricting the Minkowski vacuum to that algebra leaves every local expectation value unchanged, so the renormalised vacuum stress tensor stays isotropic in every frame. The one-sided Unruh-pressure deficit that QI needs therefore does not arise. He also argues that Θ stands for several geometrically different quantities in the QI literature. An appendix corrects the mode count in [Renda 2019](../2019-renda-sceptical/README.md). With the correct count, Renda's F ≈ 2.17 peak disappears, though Renda's two main criticisms still stand. The paper is a preprint that has not been peer-reviewed.

## Key claims
- **QI's versions are logically separate (Sect. 2).** The paper distinguishes three versions. (i) The 2007 cosmological mode restriction, in which Unruh wavelengths too long for the cosmic scale are excluded. (ii) The 2013 asymmetric Rindler suppression, in which the horizon behind an accelerating body reduces the Unruh radiation from that side and creates a pressure difference. (iii) The 2016 uncertainty-principle reformulation. Junghanns says these should "not be treated as a single target". Only version (ii) is ruled out by the no-go result.
- **What a Rindler horizon does (Sect. 3).** By the Bisognano–Wichmann theorem, the Minkowski vacuum restricted to the wedge is a KMS (thermal) state under boosts, with the Unruh temperature. The thermal structure comes from evaluating the same global state on a smaller algebra. It does not come from a reflecting condition, a change of vacuum, or the removal of field solutions.
- **The no-go result (Sect. 4, Proposition 1 and Corollary 1).** Restricting a state to a subalgebra does not change the expectation value of any observable in that subalgebra. Poincaré invariance fixes the renormalised vacuum stress tensor to C·η_µν, which is isotropic whatever the value of C. Because that is a tensor statement, it holds in the accelerated frame as well. The forward and rearward pressures are therefore equal, and the "asymmetric mean momentum flux" required by the 2013 mechanism vanishes.
- **A horizon is not a Casimir plate (Sect. 5).** A real boundary, compactification or topological identification can change the stress tensor. An observer-dependent horizon imposes no Dirichlet, Neumann, conducting or similar condition, and the field is regular across it.
- **Possible rescues considered (Sects. 6–8).** (a) The Fulling–Rindler vacuum is a *different state*. It is singular at the horizon and is not produced just by accelerating a body through the Minkowski vacuum. Using it would need an extra "state-preparation law". (b) Unruh thermality is a detector response, not a pre-existing radiation bath that could be blocked on one side. (c) Real detector–field back-reaction exists (Unruh & Wald 1984; Raine, Sciama & Grove 1991). It depends on a specific coupling and vanishes as the coupling goes to zero. It does not give a universal law F = −m·a for arbitrary matter.
- **The cosmological version and the "moving target" Θ (Sect. 9).** A horizon length does not by itself imply a Casimir spectrum. Separately, Θ has meant different things in different papers:
  - twice the Hubble distance, 2c/H (McCulloch 2007);
  - the present "co-moving cosmic diameter", 8.8 × 10²⁶ m, as opposed to the earlier 2.6 × 10²⁶ m (McCulloch 2017). Junghanns says 2c/H₀ and 8.8 × 10²⁶ m differ by "roughly 3.4", and that the 2017 paper itself uses both the Hubble-diameter and co-moving-diameter wording;
  - Θ₀/(1+z) at earlier epochs (McCulloch 2017). Junghanns says the stated "linear expansion" justification does not single this out: a genuinely co-moving diameter does not evolve at all.
  - Later papers (McCulloch & Lucio 2019; McCulloch 2024) use the fixed present-epoch value with no redshift correction.

  These choices give different histories of a₀(t) = 2c²/Θ(t), for example c·H(t) or a₀,₀(1+z). They have not been "tested in common" against the same data. Junghanns presents this as an open empirical programme rather than a refutation.
- **Interaction-dependent inertia (Sect. 9.2).** McCulloch (2024) says each interaction's inertial mass depends on the mutual acceleration. Taken literally, this gives each body a family of masses m_I^(ij)(t), and the equations of motion become coupled and implicit. With a time-dependent Θ, mechanical energy is not conserved by Noether's theorem. A cosmological completion would need its own covariant bookkeeping. QI does not supply the many-body or perturbation theory needed to predict high-z rotation curves, structure growth or the CMB.
- **The uncertainty-principle formulation (Sect. 10).** McCulloch 2016 is not directly excluded. It needs separate assumptions: that the horizon distance c²/a equals a quantum position uncertainty Δx of a specified state, and that a change in uncertainty produces a mechanical force. An uncertainty relation constrains dispersions and "does not by itself constitute an equation of motion".
- **What the paper does not claim (Sect. 12).** It does not show that every QI empirical formula fails, that inertia cannot have a vacuum, causal or informational origin, or that MOND is wrong.
- **What survives (Sect. 13).** Acceleration does change an observer's causal structure, and modular theory describes it. Junghanns suggests looking at modular transport and holonomies (Czech et al. 2018, 2023) as a mechanism independent of the one ruled out.

## Results & predictions
- **Analytic result:** within standard QFT in Minkowski spacetime, an acceleration horizon does not create the asymmetric vacuum stress that the Rindler–Casimir mechanism needs (statement (41), p. 7).
- **Renda's appendix, re-evaluated (App. A, table on p. 10):**
  - With OEIS A002102 (Renda's count), F_max ≈ 2.17 is reproduced to four or five significant figures. The maximum then sits at a ≈ 2.44 × 10⁻⁹ m s⁻², not at Renda's 1.20 × 10⁻⁹ m s⁻².
  - With the Maxwell (conducting-cavity) multiplicities, F(a) rises monotonically towards 1, for example 0.083 at 1.2 × 10⁻⁹, 0.50 at 2.42 × 10⁻⁹ and 0.95 at 1 × 10⁻⁸ m s⁻². There is no overshoot, so there is no "stable acceleration".
  - Taking the largest admissible wavelength as twice the Hubble diameter (λ_p = 4Θ/p) halves the exponent and moves the A002102 peak to a = 1.220 × 10⁻⁹ m s⁻². Junghanns says this reproduces Renda's published abscissa, so the offset "is consistent with a factor of two" between Renda's printed equation and the one he evaluated.
  - Junghanns frames the appendix as an internal-consistency check done "arguendo". The main text argues that the cavity does not exist.
- No new observational prediction. Junghanns says testing consistently evolved Θ(t) definitions against the same high-z data would be "more informative than leaving the alternatives untested" (p. 6).

## Criticisms & responses
- **Status:** a self-archived preprint by an independent researcher. The stored file is a manuscript prepared for MNRAS. No acceptance or journal publication was found as of 2026-10-19.
- **Disclosures in the paper (p. 8):** the acknowledgements thank "M. E. McCulloch for comments on an earlier draft". They also state that ChatGPT and Claude were used and that "their use was not confined to language editing". The listed contributions include parts of the argument's framing, the normalisation-independent statement in §4.1, the scaling variable (A9), the origin of the abscissa offset (§A4), and an independent reproduction of the mathematics and the Appendix A numbers. The author says the mode-counting correction is his own and takes "sole responsibility" for the content. Mathematica's notebook assistant was used for code.
- **McCulloch's response:** none specific to this paper was found. His blog post "Responding to Criticisms" (30 Aug 2026) answers Renda's peak-wavelength point by pointing to the uncertainty-principle and information-erasure derivations ([blog](https://physicsfromtheedge.blogspot.com/2026/08/responding-to-criticisms.html)). Those are the routes that Junghanns, in Sect. 10, says are not excluded but need independent assumptions.
- **Relation to other critiques:** this paper attacks an earlier step than [Renda 2019](../2019-renda-sceptical/README.md) and [Dickmann & Dickmann 2023](../2023-dickmann-casimir-contradicts/README.md). Those papers accept a cavity-like spectral cut-off and then dispute the resulting sum. Junghanns argues that no such cut-off follows from QFT at all. His Sect. 7 point, that Unruh thermality is a detector response rather than a bath, is close to the isotropy objection summarised in [2017-koberlein-doing-it-wrong](../../07-media/2017-koberlein-doing-it-wrong/README.md). The [Kholupenko](../../01-papers/2019-kholupenko-unruh-anisotropy-1/README.md) papers argue that Unruh radiation can be anisotropic for finite or screened detectors. Junghanns's Sect. 8 accepts that interacting detectors show real anisotropic response, but denies that this gives a universal inertial law. He does not cite Kholupenko.
- **Editorial check:** the Θ values Junghanns quotes (2.6 × 10²⁶ m in 2007/2012 and 8.8 × 10²⁶ m from 2017) and the 2017 Θ₀/(1+z) prescription match what this archive records in [equations](../../00-overview/equations.md) and [2017-mcculloch-galaxy-rotations-sparc](../../01-papers/2017-mcculloch-galaxy-rotations-sparc/README.md). The ratio 8.8/2.6 ≈ 3.4 checks out. This archive has not independently checked the Appendix A numbers. Note that they contradict the F ≈ 2.17 peak at 1.2 × 10⁻⁹ m s⁻² that our Renda README and [galaxy-rotation](../../02-evidence/galaxy-rotation.md) note report from Renda.
