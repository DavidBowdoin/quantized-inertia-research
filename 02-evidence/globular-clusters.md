# Globular clusters

> **Scope note.** None of the QI papers in this repository analyses globular clusters. Web searches for this note (October 2026) also found no McCulloch paper that does. This note records the observational debate that QI-style low-acceleration theories would have to face. The external sources below were checked against their arXiv abstract pages.

## The phenomenon and the data
- Globular clusters are thought to contain little dark matter, so their outskirts, where internal accelerations fall below a₀, have been proposed as a test of gravity at low acceleration.
- **Scarpa, Marconi & Gilmozzi (2006)**, "Globular Clusters as a Test for Gravity in the Weak Acceleration Regime" (AIP Conf. Proc. 822, [arXiv:astro-ph/0601581](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0601581)):
  - In three clusters, "the velocity dispersion is found to remain constant at large radii rather than follow the Keplerian falloff".
  - The flattening sets in at an internal acceleration of about 1.8 ± 0.4 × 10⁻⁸ cm s⁻², which the authors call "fully consistent with MOND predictions".
- **Hernandez & Jiménez (2012)**, "The outskirts of globular clusters as modified gravity probes" (ApJ 750, 9, [arXiv:1108.4021](https://arxiv.org/abs/1108.4021)): MOND-like equilibrium models with an inner Newtonian region reproduce the surface-brightness and dispersion profiles. Asymptotic dispersions scale as M^¼.
- **Hernandez, Jiménez & Allen (arXiv 2012, MNRAS)**, "Flattened velocity dispersion profiles in Globular Clusters: Newtonian tides or modified gravity?" (MNRAS, doi:10.1093/mnras/sts263, [arXiv:1206.5024](https://arxiv.org/abs/1206.5024)): for 16 clusters, Newtonian tidal radii at perigalacticon are typically about 4× larger than the radii where the flattening occurs. The flattening radii track the a₀ crossing, and the authors argue this disfavours a tidal explanation.
- The same Hernandez group later produced the wide-binary analyses that QI relies on ([2012-hernandez-wide-binaries](../01-papers/2012-hernandez-wide-binaries/README.md), [2019-hernandez-gaia-wide-binaries](../01-papers/2019-hernandez-gaia-wide-binaries/README.md)).

## Mainstream explanation(s)
- **Energetically unbound "potential escapers" and tidal effects.** Claydon, Gieles & Zocchi (2017, MNRAS 466, 3937, [arXiv:1612.02253](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.02253)) show with N-body models that such stars, which can remain inside clusters indefinitely on circular orbits, raise the velocity dispersion near the Jacobi surface. They list dark matter, modified gravity and potential escapers as the three candidate explanations of flat outer profiles. They report a tentative observational signal matching the dependences predicted for potential escapers.
- Contamination by field stars is also commonly cited (as summarised in the literature found in the search for this note).
- No consensus on a single explanation was established from the sources consulted.

## QI's prediction
- **No published QI globular-cluster prediction was found.** In principle the QI outer-edge formula used for dwarfs and galaxies, v⁴ = 2GMc²/Θ with 2c²/Θ ≈ 2 × 10⁻¹⁰ m s⁻², would apply ([2017-mcculloch-dwarf-galaxies](../01-papers/2017-mcculloch-dwarf-galaxies/README.md), [2012-mcculloch-galactic-scales](../01-papers/2012-mcculloch-galactic-scales/README.md)). That would give a deep-MOND-like M^¼ scaling with an a₀ larger than MOND's.
- QI claims to have no external field effect ([2025-mcculloch-jupiter-mass-binaries](../01-papers/2025-mcculloch-jupiter-mass-binaries/README.md)). For clusters inside the Milky Way's field, it would therefore predict a stronger flattening than MOND with EFE. This is an inference from the general QI formula, not a calculation published by McCulloch.

## Independent tests, critiques and null results
- No test of QI on globular clusters exists in the repository or was found online.
- The MOND-oriented globular-cluster literature is itself disputed. Potential escapers offer a Newtonian route to flat outer dispersions. Whether that route fully accounts for the observed profiles was not settled in the sources consulted.

## Assessment
Flattened outer velocity-dispersion profiles in some globular clusters have been presented as MOND-like evidence (Scarpa et al. 2006; Hernandez and collaborators 2012). Newtonian N-body work attributes such flattening to energetically unbound stars and tides (Claydon et al. 2017). QI has made no specific, published prediction for these systems, so they provide neither support for nor a test of QI at present. They are a natural future test, because QI's lack of an external field effect gives a definite expectation.
