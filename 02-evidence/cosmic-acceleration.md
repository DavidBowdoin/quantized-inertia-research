# Cosmic acceleration and the minimum acceleration

## The phenomenon and the data
- Type Ia supernova observations (Perlmutter et al. 1999; Riess et al. 1998) show that the expansion of the universe is accelerating. The mainstream description is a cosmological constant or dark energy, with Ω_Λ ≈ 0.7 in the ΛCDM comparison used by [Hernandez et al. 2019](../01-papers/2018-hernandez-bounding-curvature/README.md).
- QI papers use these data only qualitatively ([2010-mcculloch-minimum-accelerations](../01-papers/2010-mcculloch-minimum-accelerations/README.md)). None of the QI sources in this repository fits supernova distance–redshift data.
- A well-known numerical coincidence is that the galactic acceleration scale a₀ ≈ 1.2 × 10⁻¹⁰ m s⁻² is of order cH₀. Various expressions are used:
  - a₀ ≈ cH₀/2π (Milgrom);
  - cH₇₀ = 5.83 a₀ (Milgrom 2002, quoted in [2018-hernandez-bounding-curvature](../01-papers/2018-hernandez-bounding-curvature/README.md));
  - a₀ ≈ a_Λ/8.3 with a_Λ = c²√Λ ([Smolin 2017](../01-papers/2017-smolin-mond-quantum-gravity/README.md));
  - a₀ = H/2π ([Cadoni & Tuveri 2019](../01-papers/2019-cadoni-long-range-qg/README.md)).

## Mainstream explanation(s)
- **ΛCDM:** a cosmological constant (vacuum energy) drives late-time acceleration. Its value is fitted, not derived, so the physical origin of Λ is an open problem in mainstream physics.
- **Horizon- and entropy-based alternatives** by other authors link Λ or H to a₀ without QI:
  - Smolin 2017;
  - Cadoni & Tuveri 2019;
  - Hernandez et al. 2019's "bounding curvature", which gives an H(z) within 5% of ΛCDM for z < 1 with one fitted parameter, γ = 0.2935.

## QI's prediction
- **Minimum acceleration.** [McCulloch 2007](../01-papers/2007-mcculloch-pioneer-anomaly/README.md) found it as ≈ 0.99 cH. [McCulloch 2010, EPL](../01-papers/2010-mcculloch-minimum-accelerations/README.md) wrote it as 2c²/Θ:

  $$ m_I = m_g\left(1 - \frac{2c^2}{|a|\Theta}\right) \;\Rightarrow\; a_{min} = \frac{2c^2}{\Theta} $$

  - "MiHsC ... predicts that an object must still accelerate: there is a minimum acceleration in nature." The paper says this is close to the acceleration attributed to dark energy.
  - Numerically, 2c²/Θ is 6.7 × 10⁻¹⁰ m s⁻² with Θ = 2.7 × 10²⁶ m ([2012](../01-papers/2012-mcculloch-galactic-scales/README.md)). It is ≈ 2 × 10⁻¹⁰ m s⁻² with Θ = 8.8 × 10²⁶ m (2017 onward), and 4.18 × 10⁻¹⁰ m s⁻² with Pickering's Θ = 4.3 × 10²⁶ m ([2017-pickering-resonant-cavity](../01-papers/2017-pickering-resonant-cavity/README.md)).
- **Rindler-horizon route.** [McCulloch 2019, Progress in Physics](../01-papers/2019-mcculloch-rindler-horizons/README.md) argues that a photon moving through the Hubble sphere stops feeling the mass behind its Rindler horizon. The imbalance gives a = 2.45 × 10^(−11±1) m s⁻², said to agree with the cosmic acceleration and the galactic critical acceleration of ~2 × 10⁻¹⁰ m s⁻². Note the ±1 uncertainty in the exponent. The paper's text also contains a typo for the cosmic radius.
- **Other derivations.** QI papers state that the Hubble-horizon part of QI gives "cosmic acceleration without dark energy" ([2017-mcculloch-information-loss](../01-papers/2017-mcculloch-information-loss/README.md)). Giné (2012) claims that a holographic version gives late-time acceleration "obviating the dark energy" ([2012-gine-holographic-modified-inertia](../01-papers/2012-gine-holographic-modified-inertia/README.md), abstract only; full text not accessible). Giné & Luciano (2024) use the same Hubble-scale damping to estimate Λ ≈ 2H₀²/c² ([2024-gine-luciano-discretization-lambda](../01-papers/2024-gine-luciano-discretization-lambda/README.md)).

## Independent tests, critiques and null results
- **No expansion-history test.** No QI paper in the sources computes an expansion history H(z) or a supernova Hubble diagram. The claim is an order-of-magnitude match between a_min and "the cosmic acceleration".
- **Coincidence warning.** Turyshev & Toth (2010) warn that a_P ≃ cH₀ is misleading in the Pioneer context ([2010-turyshev-pioneer-review](../01-papers/2010-turyshev-pioneer-review/README.md)). The same caution applies to any argument that rests on a cH match.
- **Unstable value of Θ.** Θ has taken values from 2.6 to 8.8 × 10²⁶ m (see [galaxy-rotation](galaxy-rotation.md)), so the "predicted" minimum acceleration has varied by a factor of ~3.4. The Rindler-horizon estimate is uncertain by a factor of 10 either way.
- **Renda 2019.** Re-computing the Unruh spectrum allowed by the cavity gives a non-linear F(a), which is 0 below a₀ and peaks at F ≈ 2.17 near 1.2 × 10⁻⁹ m s⁻². That undermines the simple linear form from which a_min is read ([2019-renda-sceptical](../03-criticism/2019-renda-sceptical/README.md)).
- **Hernandez et al. 2019** mention McCulloch's route, in which dark-energy-like vacuum effects set the galactic scale, and call it "unsatisfactory".
- No independent cosmological fit of QI was found in the sources.

## Assessment
QI ties its minimum acceleration to the size of the cosmic horizon, so it lands, by construction, at the order of magnitude of cH. That is also the scale of the observed cosmic acceleration and of MOND's a₀. Several independent horizon-based approaches note the same coincidence. QI has not turned it into a quantitative cosmological prediction: no expansion history, no supernova fit, and a horizon length Θ whose adopted value has changed between papers. The match is therefore an order-of-magnitude statement supported only in McCulloch's own work. ΛCDM fits the expansion data with a fitted Λ whose origin is unexplained.
