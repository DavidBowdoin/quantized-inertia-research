# The EPR paradox and the uncertainty principle

**Citation:** McCulloch, M. E. & Giné, J. (2021). *The EPR paradox and the uncertainty principle*. Modern Physics Letters B, 35(04), 2150072 (published online 18 January 2021). https://doi.org/10.1142/s021798492150072x

Paywalled, so no PDF is stored. This README is based on the publisher abstract (via Crossref). No open preprint was found on arXiv.

**TL;DR:** The authors propose a "simple resolution" of the EPR paradox. Because two entangled particles do not interact while they separate, the uncertainty in the system's energy shrinks, so the uncertainty in time grows beyond the time they have been separating. Present and past then become "indistinguishable": a measurement gives the same value as if the particles were still together. They argue that the information destroyed when present and past become identical should release heat by Landauer's principle, which might make the proposal testable.

## Key claims
From the abstract:
- Explanations of EPR correlations from quantum mechanics "fail to explain the EPR … paradox totally in a way than can be accepted by the whole community."
- Lack of interaction during the journey reduces the energy uncertainty ΔE, so the time uncertainty Δt exceeds the elapsed separation time.
- "the present and past become indistinguishable because when we measure an observable in the system its value is the same as if the two particle were still together or very close."
- Destroying information in this way should release heat (Landauer's principle), offering a possible test.
