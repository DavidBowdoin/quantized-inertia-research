**TL;DR:** A single-author, highly speculative theory proposal. Soli defines inertia as an "entropic 4-force" that emerges from the IKKT (type IIB) matrix model through "mild" UV/IR mixing, and identifies it with QI acting at the Planck scale. Dark-energy density is said to supply the underlying quantum-statistical fluctuations. He then reinterprets data from two existing experiments as Unruh effects. One is a Thorlabs datasheet for a silica optical fibre, whose Rayleigh-scattering attenuation he reads as doubled by "superradiant" photon-pair production. The other is the CERN positron-channelling experiment, as analysed by Lynch et al. He proposes that the Bose-Einstein vs Fermi-Dirac statistics in the two cases could be combined to measure an IKKT "supersymmetric mixed state". No new experiment is reported.

## Key claims
- Unruh-radiation effects "have been discovered in two experiments": a fermionic positron-in-silicon channelling experiment at CERN and a bosonic photon-in-silica fibre experiment "preformed at ThorLabs", in both cases recognised only after the experiments were done (Introduction, p. 1).
- Inertia is "an entropic 4-force that is emergent from the IKKT matrix model and caused by 'mild' UV/IR mixing" (Abstract, p. 1). It is "equivalent to Quantised Inertia (QI) interacting at the Planck scale" (p. 1). It resists only non-gravitational acceleration, because there is no heat flow in free fall (p. 2; Eq. 2, p. 4).
- The paper admits that "mild" UV/IR mixing does not exist in the 4-dimensional IKKT model without added soft NCSUSY-breaking terms. These terms are not added; they are assumed to be "some unknown" terms (p. 1). The conclusion repeats that "the theory awaits soft NCSUSY breaking" (p. 14).
- The minimum acceleration a₀ ≈ 2 × 10⁻¹⁰ m/s² corresponds to r = c²/a₀ = 4.4 × 10²⁶ m, which Soli equates with the distance to the edge of "our small causal patch". It "produces most of the effect of dark matter" (p. 5).
- The universe contains about 2.8 × 10⁶¹ causal patches, which is said to solve the flatness problem (pp. 1, 5).
- **Fibre-optic superradiance:** laser photons reflecting inside a fibre see a hot Unruh bath, and virtual Unruh photons stimulate emission of extra "superradiant" photons. These become real by pair production with Rayleigh-scattered laser photons, drawing their energy from fibre heat. This is presented by analogy with tidal friction (Sects. 4–6, pp. 8–11).
- The Thorlabs fibre's measured attenuation is about twice normal Rayleigh scattering, which is attributed to this pair production (Fig. 3, p. 11).
- The photon absorption and emission forces are symmetric around the fibre loop, and "anomalous acceleration is constrained by emdrive IR-laser balance beam experiments", citing Neunzig, Weikert & Tajmar (p. 6).
- An appendix proposes a "superradiant-photon rocket-motor" based on an EmDrive cavity, with energy coming from cavity heat (p. 17).

## Key equations
Unruh energy-density integrand in units ħ = c = 1, with ω = E/a (Eq. 1, p. 3):
$$ \sigma(\omega) = \frac{\omega}{e^{2\pi\omega} - 1} $$
Thermodynamic equivalence principle and work term, with K the modular Hamiltonian (Eqs. 2–3, p. 4):
$$ \delta Q = T\,\delta S = 0, \qquad dW = -d\langle K_1 - K_0 \rangle $$
Thermodynamic Einstein equation, and dark energy as a geometric mean of IR and UV densities (Eqs. 4–5, pp. 5–6):
$$ T_U\,dS = dE + P_U\,dV, \qquad \rho_{DE} = \sqrt{\rho_{IR}\,\rho_{UV}} $$
Reflection force, photon-mass force and its Tolman form, with θ the fibre's reflection (critical) angle (Eqs. 6–10, pp. 6–7):
$$ F = \frac{2L}{c}, \qquad F = c\frac{\partial m_i}{\partial t} = \frac{\partial U}{\partial r}, \qquad F = \frac{U}{r} = m_i\,a\cos\theta, \qquad F = \frac{2L\cos\theta}{c} $$
Unruh temperature for the fibre (Eq. 11, p. 7):
$$ T_U = a\cos\theta\,\frac{\hbar}{2\pi c k_B} $$
Statistical factor plotted in Fig. 4, and the IKKT average (Eqs. 26–27, p. 13):
$$ f_{\pm} = \frac{1}{1 \pm e^{-2\pi E/a}}, \qquad f_{IKKT} = \tfrac{1}{2}\left(f_{\rm Fermionic} + f_{\rm Bosonic}\right) $$

## Data used
- No new measurements.
- **Thorlabs fibre datasheet** (FG910LEC, 0.22 NA, 910 µm core, low-OH silica): attenuation versus wavelength, refitted and compared with the Rayleigh curve (Fig. 3, p. 11; ref. [14]). The scenario uses a 1064 nm Nd:YAG laser at 5 kW continuous inside the fibre, with reflection angle θ = 77.3° (p. 7).
- **CERN SPS positron channelling** (Wistisen et al. 2018: 178.2 GeV positrons on silicon along the ⟨111⟩ axis) as reanalysed by Lynch et al., who report a Rindler temperature T_R = T_U(1.09 ± 0.41). Lynch et al.'s polynomial coefficients for ΔE are quoted (Sect. 7, pp. 11–12; refs. [20], [21]).

## Results & predictions
- Fibre numbers (Eqs. 12–14, p. 7): for λ = 1064 nm, U = 1.1665 eV, r = λ/2π = 0.169 µm and a = cω = 5.31 × 10²³ m/s². With cos 77.3° the normal acceleration is 1.17 × 10²³ m/s², giving an Unruh temperature T_U = 473.5 K.
  - Editorial check: ħa/(2πck_B) with a = 1.1684 × 10²³ m/s² gives about 474 K, which matches.
- For an elastic collision with no angle factor, T_U = 2,153.8 K, which the paper notes is above the melting point of silica (p. 10).
- Proposed set-up: 3,000 fibre loops of 0.1 m radius (1.885 km of fibre), each carrying 5 kW, i.e. 15 MW circulating. This would give "0.1 [N] of solar-sail like force" from F = 2L/c (p. 6; Sect. 5, p. 8).
  - Editorial check: 2 × 15 MW / c = 0.1 N, which matches. This is ordinary radiation pressure on the reflecting half-loop. The paper itself says the forces are symmetric around the loop (p. 6), so it does not claim a net QI thrust from it.
- Radiated blackbody power from the inside cladding area (1.715 m²): 178.3 W/m², or 305.8 W, doubled to P = 0.6116 kW for pair production (Eqs. 15–16, p. 8).
- **Prediction (Fig. 4, p. 13):** the bosonic and fermionic statistical factors f diverge as a/2πE grows, and an IKKT "mixed state" would show up as their average. No numerical prediction for a specific measurement, and no thrust prediction, is given.
- No follow-up publication reporting a measurement was found in a brief search.

## Criticisms & responses
- **Status:** a single-author APS meeting paper and ResearchGate preprint, not peer-reviewed. It has received little attention: no citing works were found via OpenAlex or Semantic Scholar. The acknowledgements thank McCulloch "for pointing out the need for this Planck scale interaction in Quantised Inertia" (p. 14).
- **Self-acknowledged gaps:** the key ingredient, "mild" UV/IR mixing in 4 dimensions, requires soft NCSUSY-breaking terms that the paper assumes but does not supply. The Planck-scale photon condensate is likewise assumed "from thermodynamic considerations" (p. 1).
- **Reinterpreted data (editorial reading):** the "Unruh effects" are readings of existing data, not tests designed for them. The fibre case rests on a manufacturer's attenuation curve sitting about twice above a plain Rayleigh curve. Ordinary fibre-loss mechanisms are not discussed. The CERN case is Lynch et al.'s published reanalysis.
- **Mainstream reading of the CERN result:** this repository's notes on Lynch's later work say the authors describe their results as classical ("thermal Larmor") radiation tied to acceleration, which does not test QI's horizon cut-off ([lab-tests](../../02-evidence/lab-tests.md), [2023-lynch-accelerated-electron-thermometer](../2023-lynch-accelerated-electron-thermometer/README.md)).
- **Related laboratory tests:** [Neunzig et al. 2022](../../03-criticism/2022-neunzig-laser-resonators/README.md) tested asymmetric laser resonators and fibre loops for QI-type thrust and found nothing above photon thrust. This paper cites the conference version of that work (ref. [12], p. 6) as a constraint on anomalous acceleration in fibre loops. Its own proposal is about heat and statistics, not thrust, so those nulls do not test it directly.
- A web search also turned up a 2025 APS abstract by the same presenter, on a different topic (a "U(1)-gravity boson" connected to a UAP detector). It is mentioned only for context.
