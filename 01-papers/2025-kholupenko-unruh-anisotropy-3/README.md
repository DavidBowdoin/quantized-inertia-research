# On the Possible Anisotropy of the Unruh Radiation. Part III: A Screened Detector

**Citation:** Kholupenko, E. E. (2025). *On the Possible Anisotropy of the Unruh Radiation. Part III: A Screened Detector*. Gravitation and Cosmology, 31(3), 353–365 (September 2025). https://doi.org/10.1134/S0202289325700239

Paywalled, so no PDF is stored. This README is based on the abstract (via the OpenAlex record) and the reference list.

**TL;DR:** The paper considers detection of Unruh radiation by a screened, point-like monopole detector with a narrow directivity pattern, for massive and massless scalar fields in (3+1)D. The detector's response is found to be significantly anisotropic in all cases, and its energy profile depends strongly on the screening function. For a narrow enough directivity pattern, the brightness temperature "may exceed the Unruh temperature by orders of magnitude". The paper concludes that Unruh radiation is not thermal equilibrium radiation. It also argues that the interaction of Unruh radiation with an accelerated screened detector "should lead to the emergence of a reaction force".

## Key claims
From the abstract:
- The stationary energy–angular responses of a screened detector are calculated numerically for massive and massless particles, and estimated analytically for massless ones.
- "in all cases the response is significantly anisotropic, and its energy profile strongly depends on the shape of the screening function."
- The brightness temperature can exceed T_U by orders of magnitude for a narrow directivity pattern.
- Unruh-radiation generation is a symmetric pair-production process, implying "counterpart radiation going to infinity". Its spectrum depends on the detector's properties, and it might in principle be observed by inertial detectors, for example in particle accelerators.
- "The interaction of the Unruh radiation with an accelerated screened detector should lead to the emergence of a reaction force acting on this detector."

## Results & predictions
- Anisotropic, screening-dependent response.
- Brightness temperature ≫ T_U for narrow directivity (no specific number in the abstract).
- A predicted outgoing counterpart radiation, potentially observable at accelerators.
- A predicted reaction force on a screened accelerated detector. Its magnitude is not checked here.
