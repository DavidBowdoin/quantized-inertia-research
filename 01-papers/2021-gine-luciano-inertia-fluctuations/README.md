# Modeling inertia through the interaction with quantum fluctuations

**Citation:** Giné, J. & Luciano, G. G. (2021). *Modeling inertia through the interaction with quantum fluctuations*. Results in Physics, 28, 104543 (September 2021). https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rinp.2021.104543 (gold open access, CC BY).

This README is based on the abstract and the reference list (via Semantic Scholar and OpenAlex). It does not draw on the full text.

**TL;DR:** The paper models inertia as arising from interactions between a body's Planck-size "resonant parts" and quantum fluctuations. It proposes two frameworks. The first applies the Heisenberg uncertainty principle to fluctuations near a body. The second uses the asymmetric, Casimir-like damping of radiation seen by an accelerated object because of its Rindler horizon, which is essentially McCulloch's QI mechanism. The paper then discusses whether the two approaches are consistent.

## Key claims
From the abstract:
- "The origin of inertia of macroscopic bodies has never been thoroughly elucidated."
- Assumptions: (i) any body can be thought of as composed of resonant parts of Planck size; (ii) inertia arises from the interaction between these constituents and quantum fluctuations.
- Model 1: a direct application of the Heisenberg uncertainty principle to the fluctuations near a body.
- Model 2: "the asymmetric (Casimir-like) damping of the radiation experienced by an accelerated object due to the appearance of a Rindler horizon".
- The paper discusses whether the two models are consistent.
