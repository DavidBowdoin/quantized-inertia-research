# The Proton Radius Anomaly from the Sheltering of Unruh Radiation

<!-- local-only-note -->
> 📁 **Local only:** the figures rendered from this paper are kept out of the public repository (`CC-BY-NC-ND-2.5` does not permit derivatives). The PDF itself is included; each figure caption below links to its page in the PDF.

**Citation:** McCulloch, M. E. (2017). *The Proton Radius Anomaly from the Sheltering of Unruh Radiation*. Progress in Physics 13(2), 100–101 (April 2017; submitted 5 Jan 2017, accepted 19 Jan 2017). https://progress-in-physics.com/2017/PP-49-05.PDF. Progress in Physics does not assign DOIs.

**TL;DR:** In this two-page note, the muon in muonic hydrogen sees Unruh radiation because of its orbital acceleration. The proton blocks part of that radiation (a solid-angle fraction of ~5.7×10⁻⁶), which gives a net push towards the proton. The estimated extra binding energy is ~180 µeV, about 55% of the 320 µeV needed to explain the "proton radius puzzle". For ordinary hydrogen the effect is ~5 orders of magnitude smaller.

## Key claims
- The muonic-hydrogen Lamb shift gives a proton radius of 0.84 fm, against 0.88 fm from electron methods: 4% smaller, a 7σ discrepancy (Pohl et al. 2010; Antognini et al. 2013; muonic deuterium, Pohl et al. 2016). Equivalently, there is an unexplained extra binding energy of 320 µeV.
- Unruh radiation is "taken to exist and to be able to push on particles". This is the QI premise that inertia is an Unruh-radiation pressure imbalance.
- The proton shelters the muon from Unruh radiation on the side facing it, giving an extra attraction. The electron orbits ~200 times further out, so the proton subtends a much smaller solid angle there and the effect is negligible.

## Key equations
Muon orbital acceleration with v ~ αc (Eq. 1):
$$a = \frac{v^2}{R} = \frac{(\alpha c)^2}{R}$$

Unruh wavelength via Wien's law, β = 0.2 (Eq. 2), and its energy (Eqs. 3–5):
$$\lambda_U \sim \frac{8c^2}{a}, \qquad E = \frac{hc}{\lambda_U} \sim \frac{ha}{8c} \sim \frac{h\alpha^2 c}{8R}$$

Sheltered energy (Eq. 6):
$$E \sim \frac{h\alpha^2 c}{8R} \times \frac{\pi r_P^2}{2\pi R^2} = 2.8\times10^{-23}\ \text{J}$$

## Data used
- Literature values only: proton radius 0.88 fm (electron) and 0.84 fm (muon), and the 320 µeV (5.1×10⁻²³ J) energy discrepancy. Sources: Carlson 2015 review, Pohl et al. 2010 and 2016, Antognini et al. 2013.

## Results & predictions
- Solid-angle fraction of the proton as seen from the muon: **5.7 × 10⁻⁶**.
- Extra muon–proton binding energy: **2.8 × 10⁻²³ J ≈ 180 µeV**, about **55%** of the 320 µeV anomaly.
- Electron case: **7.1 × 10⁻²⁸ J**, about 5 orders of magnitude smaller, so no anomaly is expected in ordinary hydrogen.
- The paper proposes no new experiment and gives no explanation for the remaining ~45%.

## Criticisms & responses
- **The anomaly has largely gone away.** Later electronic-hydrogen measurements gave proton radii close to the muonic value, about 0.83–0.84 fm. Examples: the Lamb shift (Bezginov et al. 2019, *Science* 365, 1007) and the PRad electron-scattering experiment (Xiong et al. 2019, *Nature* 575, 147). CODATA 2018 adopted r_p = 0.8414 fm. Most physicists now attribute the puzzle mainly to problems with the older electron-based measurements, not to new physics in muonic hydrogen. That removes the anomaly this paper sets out to explain. See [other-anomalies](../../02-evidence/other-anomalies.md).
- **Heuristic method.** The calculation is order-of-magnitude. It assigns a classical orbital speed and radius to a quantum bound state (the paper notes this caveat itself), uses Wien's law for Unruh radiation, and treats the proton as an opaque disc. The orbital radius R is not stated numerically.
- **Venue.** Progress in Physics is a low-impact, non-mainstream journal. This paper was accepted two weeks after submission.
