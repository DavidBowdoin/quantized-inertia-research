# On the Possible Anisotropy of the Unruh Radiation. Part I: Massless Scalar Field in (1+1)D Space-Time

**Citation:** Kholupenko, E. E. (2019). *On the Possible Anisotropy of the Unruh Radiation. Part I: Massless Scalar Field in (1+1)D Space-Time*. Gravitation and Cosmology, 25(3), 213–225. https://doi.org/10.1134/S0202289319030071

**TL;DR:** For a massless scalar field in (1+1)D, the paper argues that under "natural assumptions like finiteness of the integration volume or finiteness of the interaction (propagation of information) speed", the Unruh effect should be **anisotropic**. It presents this as a fundamental property, not a detector artefact. QI's mechanism depends on Unruh radiation being non-uniform around an accelerating body. The abstract names "motion dynamics of accelerated bodies" as a possible application, and the reference list includes Giné & McCulloch (2016). Parts II (2022) and III (2025) extend the work.

> Paywalled. No PDF is stored. This summary is based on the abstract (OpenAlex / publisher metadata) and the Crossref reference list.

## Key claims
- From the abstract: "It is shown that, under some natural assumptions like finiteness of the integration volume or finiteness of the interaction (propagation of information) speed, the Unruh effect should be anisotropic."
- "This property is not connected with a particular detector design but is fundamental like the Unruh effect itself."
- "This consideration can be generalized to the case of massless and massive particles in (3 + 1)D space-time."
- Possible applications: "the description of black hole evaporation and motion dynamics of accelerated bodies."

## Results & predictions
- Qualitative result: the Unruh spectrum seen by a uniformly accelerated observer should depend on direction once the integration volume or the propagation speed is finite. > [!UNVERIFIED] No quantitative anisotropy figures from Part I were checked.
- Per a later abstract (Ioffe seminar page for Part II), the (3+1)D massive case is "significantly anisotropic", so the radiation "cannot be considered as thermal (equilibrium) radiation". See [Part II](../2022-kholupenko-unruh-anisotropy-2/README.md).

## Criticisms & responses
- > [!UNVERIFIED] A brief search found no published critique or reply that addresses this paper specifically.
- Context: the standard result for a uniformly accelerated detector in Minkowski vacuum is an *isotropic* thermal bath in the detector's frame (Unruh 1976; Crispino, Higuchi & Matsas 2008, both cited here). This paper's conclusion depends on adding finite-volume or finite-speed assumptions to that idealised case. Milgrom (1999) had argued that isotropy prevents Unruh radiation from causing inertia (as noted in [McCulloch & Giné 2020](../2020-mcculloch-horizon-widths/README.md)). Kholupenko's anisotropy is relevant to that objection.
- The reference list (38 entries, Crossref) includes Giné & McCulloch, *Mod. Phys. Lett. A* 31, 1650107 (2016) ("Inertial mass from Unruh temperatures") and the author's own 2018 EPJ Web of Conferences 191, 07006 conference paper.
