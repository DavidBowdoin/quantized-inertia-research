# Galaxy rotations from quantised inertia and visible matter only

<!-- local-only-note -->
> 📁 **Local only:** the stored PDF and its rendered figures are kept out of the public repository because this copy's licence (`arXiv-nonexclusive`) does not permit redistribution. Read the paper at [arXiv:1709.04918](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.04918) · [doi:10.1007/s10509-017-3128-6](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10509-017-3128-6).

**Citation:** McCulloch, M. E. (2017). *Galaxy rotations from quantised inertia and visible matter only*. Astrophysics and Space Science 362(9), 149. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10509-017-3128-6. Preprint: [arXiv:1709.04918](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.04918) (`paper.pdf` is the arXiv version).

**TL;DR:** McCulloch derives a QI form of the radial acceleration relation (RAR), a_pred = a_bar·√(1 + 2c²/(a_bar Θ)), with no free parameter. He overlays it on the binned SPARC RAR data (McGaugh et al. 2016; 153 galaxies, ~3300 points). Because Θ (the cosmic diameter) was smaller in the past, QI predicts that the rotation anomaly grows with redshift as (1+z). He compares this with Übler et al. (2017) Tully–Fisher evolution and Genzel et al. (2017) high-z galaxies.

## Key claims
- QI fits the z ≈ 0 SPARC RAR "within the error bars" using only visible matter, c and Θ. MoND also fits, but with a fitted a₀ (Sec. 3, Fig. 1).
- The paper takes the McGaugh et al. (2016) RAR result to argue "there is no dark matter" (Introduction).
- The critical acceleration 2c²/Θ scales with cosmic size, so QI "uniquely" predicts faster rotation for equal-mass galaxies at high z (Sec. 4).
- QI baryonic Tully–Fisher relation: v⁴ = 2GMc²(1+z)/Θ_now (Eq. 12).
- Θ is set to the present co-moving diameter, 8.8×10²⁶ m (93 billion light years). This differs from the Θ = 2.6×10²⁶ m used in McCulloch (2007, 2012). The paper says that value "represents the cosmic diameter at the epoch when the light from the galaxies was emitted" (Sec. 3).
- No effect on equivalence-principle tests (torsion balance or free fall), because the inertial change does not depend on mass.

## Key equations
McGaugh et al. (2016) empirical RAR fit (Eq. 2):
$$a_{obs} = \frac{a_{bar}}{1 - e^{-\sqrt{a_{bar}/a_0}}}$$

QI force balance at a galaxy's edge (Eq. 6, from McCulloch 2012):
$$a^2 = \frac{GM(|a| + |a'|)}{r^2}, \qquad |a'| = \frac{2c^2}{\Theta}$$

QI radial acceleration relation (Eqs. 7–8):
$$a_{pred}^2 = a_{bar}^2 + a_{bar}\frac{2c^2}{\Theta}, \qquad a_{pred} = a_{bar}\sqrt{1 + \frac{2c^2}{a_{bar}\Theta}}$$

Redshift scaling and Tully–Fisher relation (Eqs. 9, 12, 13):
$$\Theta_{z} = \frac{\Theta_{now}}{1+z}, \qquad v^4 = \frac{2GMc^2(1+z)}{\Theta_{now}}, \qquad M = \frac{v^4\,\Theta_{now}}{2Gc^2(1+z)}$$

## Data used
- SPARC radial acceleration relation, binned (McGaugh, Lelli & Schombert 2016, PRL 117, 201101). The binned data were supplied by McGaugh by personal communication.
- Übler et al. (2017, ApJ 842, 121): KMOS Tully–Fisher evolution at z ≈ 0.9 and 2.3.
- Genzel et al. (2017, Nature 543, 397): six massive star-forming galaxies at z = 0.854–2.383.
- Mentioned only: Thomas et al. (2013) SDSS/BOSS velocity dispersions at z = 0–2.

## Results & predictions
- At z = 0 the QI curve lies on the binned SPARC data "within the error bars". The paper gives no χ² or RMS statistic.
- Mass at fixed rotation speed, change relative to z = 0:

| Redshift | Übler et al. (2017) observed | QI prediction (Eq. 13) |
|---|---|---|
| z = 0.9 | −0.38 to −0.47 dex | −0.28 dex |
| z = 2.3 | −0.2 to −0.47 dex | −0.52 dex |

- Genzel et al. (2017) galaxies: the QI minimum acceleration (2c²/Θ at each z) rises with z, as the observed accelerations do. For the four lower-z galaxies the QI values are **9–19% higher** than observed. The paper says agreement "is worse" for the two highest-z galaxies.
- The paper claims the data support the redshift prediction "at least up to Z=2.2".

## Criticisms & responses
- **Which Θ to use.** The "no adjustable parameter" claim depends on the choice of Θ. This paper uses 8.8×10²⁶ m; earlier QI papers used 2.6×10²⁶ m, a factor of ~3.4 different. The paper explicitly motivates its value using the present co-moving cosmic diameter from Bars and Terning (2009). Since the predicted a₀-like scale is 2c²/Θ, the definition matters at the factor-of-a-few level; a change between papers does not by itself establish fitting to galaxy data. Renda (2019) raised general objections to the arbitrariness of QI's horizon scale: [2019-renda-sceptical](../../03-criticism/2019-renda-sceptical/README.md).
- **Low-acceleration fit.** Eq. 8 has the "simple"-MOND-like form with an effective a₀ = 2c²/Θ ≈ 2.0×10⁻¹⁰ m/s², versus the fitted 1.2×10⁻¹⁰ m/s². In the deep regime QI therefore predicts accelerations ~30% higher than the MoND curve. Fig. 1 shows the z = 0 QI curve slightly above the lowest-acceleration bins. (Editorial observation from the equations; no published statistic.) Editorial check: our refit of the full 2,693-point SPARC RAR ([A1](../../09-analysis/a1-sparc-rar-refit/README.md)) measures the offset as 17–32% below g_bar ≈ 10⁻¹¹ m s⁻², with point-level ΔBIC +490 against MOND simple with a fitted a₀. That likelihood approximates within-galaxy correlations; the score and its galaxy bootstrap are not a calibrated exclusion significance. The paper's §3 describes RMS scatter in the bins, whereas A1 studies offsets from means and reports point-based standard errors. A1's separate public-bin reconstruction places the published curve within one tabulated scatter of all 14 means (largest absolute offset/scatter = 0.741), consistent with the author's visual agreement claim. Identity with the original privately supplied bins remains unverified.
- **"Unique" redshift prediction.** Milgrom (1983 onward) noted that a₀ ≈ cH₀/2π and discussed a possibly time-varying a₀. Whether an evolving critical acceleration is unique to QI is therefore debatable.
- **How the data authors read their results.** Übler et al. (2017) and Genzel et al. (2017) interpreted their high-z results within ΛCDM, in terms of high baryon/gas fractions and pressure support. Genzel et al. is titled "Strongly baryon-dominated disk galaxies at the peak of galaxy formation ten billion years ago".
- **Wide-binary tests.** Later wide-binary tests of low-acceleration gravity reached conflicting conclusions. See [wide-binaries](../../02-evidence/wide-binaries.md) and [2023-banik-gaia-dr3-wide-binaries](../../03-criticism/2023-banik-gaia-dr3-wide-binaries/README.md).
