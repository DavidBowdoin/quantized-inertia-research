# Inertial mass from Unruh temperatures

**Citation:** Giné, J. & McCulloch, M. E. (2016). *Inertial mass from Unruh temperatures*. Modern Physics Letters A 31(17), 1650107 (published online 7 June 2016). https://doi.org/10.1142/S0217732316501078

> [!NOTE]
> Paywalled. No PDF is stored. This summary is from the abstract only (Crossref metadata / World Scientific). No arXiv preprint was found.

**TL;DR:** QI's mechanism says that when an object accelerates, a Rindler horizon forms behind it and suppresses Unruh radiation on that side. The resulting net radiation pressure opposes the acceleration, like inertia. The earlier version of this model (McCulloch 2013, EPL 101, 59001) predicted inertial masses over twice the expected value. This paper corrects an error in that model, bringing the prediction to within 29% of the Planck mass. It also gives a qualitative thermodynamic picture: the work behind inertia comes from the difference between the Unruh temperatures seen by the accelerated object and by the cosmos.

## Key claims
(From the abstract.)
- Inertia is explained as a net Unruh radiation pressure caused by a Rindler horizon forming opposite to the acceleration.
- "So far, this model has predicted masses over twice those expected. In this paper, an error in this model is corrected so that its prediction improves to within 29% of the expected Planck mass."
- Applying Carnot's principle and entropy to Unruh temperatures means "the work needed for inertia comes from the difference in the Unruh temperatures seen by the accelerated object and the cosmos".
- "This implies that highly-accelerated systems may emit heat in a new way."

## Results & predictions
- Predicted mass is within **29%** of the Planck mass, improved from "over twice" in the earlier model.
- Qualitative prediction: new heat emission from highly accelerated systems.
- Later citations of this result give different figures. Giné (2017, IJMPA) describes the corrected prediction as "within 26% of the Planck mass", and McCulloch (2014, Galaxies) had cited the earlier Giné & McCulloch work as explaining standard inertia "to within 26%". The 29% figure is from this paper's own abstract.
