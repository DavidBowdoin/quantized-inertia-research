**TL;DR (from the abstract):** McCulloch restates QI (inertia caused by the quantum vacuum being damped behind an accelerating body by a relativistic horizon, giving a gradient and a force; slightly amended, it predicts galaxy rotation without dark matter) and says it implies a way to move objects by "harvesting the quantum background". He then argues that electrons quantum-tunnelling across a capacitor are confined, which reduces their position uncertainty and so increases their momentum uncertainty ("quantum jitter"). This extra momentum is passed to the anode as a new quantum force. The abstract says the force exactly predicts recent capacitor-thrust experiments and is large and scalable enough to replace satellite ion drives.

## Key claims
- QI model of inertia as above; the abstract says the model "suggests a new way to move objects by harvesting the quantum background" (Crossref abstract).
- Tunnelling electrons confined between capacitor plates have reduced position uncertainty and so, by the uncertainty principle, increased momentum uncertainty, which is transferred to the anode as thrust (Crossref abstract).
- The resulting force "exactly predicts recent experimental evidence of thrust from capacitors" (Crossref abstract). Which experiments are meant is not stated in the abstract.
- The propellant-less thrust is said to be large enough to replace satellite ion drives and scalable enough to make deep-space exploration much easier (Crossref abstract).

## Criticisms & responses
- **Status:** a single-author, non-peer-reviewed SSRN preprint. No published response was found (Crossref records 0 citations, 2026-10).
- **Independent capacitor test:** [Tajmar & Schreiber 2020](../../03-criticism/2020-tajmar-capacitor-thrust/README.md) replicated a Becker & Bhatt-type field-emission (leakage-current) capacitor and found < 0.3 mg, saying the claimed effect is "ruled out by an order of magnitude". That paper does not appear in the reference list SSRN deposited with Crossref.
  > [!UNVERIFIED] Whether the full text discusses Tajmar & Schreiber (2020) is unknown.
- **Momentum conservation:** a closed capacitor driven by its own power supply that produces net thrust with no exhaust would violate momentum conservation in standard physics (see [propulsion](../../02-evidence/propulsion.md)). McCulloch's general reply (blog post "Responding to Criticisms", 2026-08-30) is that damping the Unruh waves on one side shifts the centre of mass of the Unruh field the other way, restoring momentum conservation. Whether this paper's tunnelling/"jitter" version addresses this is unknown.
- **In-space tests:** IVO Ltd's QI-thruster flights have produced no published thrust result: Barry-1 (2023–24) lost contact before its drive was activated ([2024-debrief-ivo-failure](../../07-media/2024-debrief-ivo-failure/README.md)), and McCulloch himself calls the second flight (Rogue OTP-2, 2025) inconclusive ([2026-nextbigfuture-ivo-review](../../07-media/2026-nextbigfuture-ivo-review/README.md)).
- Editorial note: the uncertainty principle bounds the spread of momentum, not its mean. A larger momentum *uncertainty* for confined electrons does not by itself give a net *directed* momentum transfer to one plate; the abstract does not say how a direction is chosen. This is our reading of the abstract only.
