# Testing quantized inertia on Proxima Centauri

**Citation:** McCulloch, M. E. (2024). *Testing quantized inertia on Proxima Centauri*. Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society: Letters, 532(1), L67–L69 (received 19 Sept 2023; accepted 20 May 2024; Advance Access 25 May 2024). https://doi.org/10.1093/mnrasl/slae047 (open access, CC BY 4.0).

**About the stored PDF:** `paper.pdf` is the published OUP/MNRAS Letters version (3 pages, journal pp. L67–L69 = PDF pp. 1–3). The CC BY 4.0 licence is stated on pp. L67 and L69. Page references below are journal pages. The letter has no figures or tables.

**TL;DR:** Proxima Centauri appears to orbit α Cen A+B too fast to be bound by their visible mass alone: 0.53 ± 0.14 km/s observed (as cited) against 0.344 ± 0.018 km/s Newtonian. Yet its co-motion and chemistry suggest it is bound. McCulloch applies QI's modified inertia. In the low-acceleration limit this gives v⁴ = 2GMc²/Θ, so v = 0.483 ± 0.01 km/s, within the observed error bars. He argues that dark matter, adding mass to A and B, and MOND (because of its external field effect) do not fix the problem, and that QI has no external field effect.

## Key claims
- From the abstract: "the fast orbit of Proxima implies it is gravitationally unbound given the visible mass of A and B." This "cannot be fixed with the addition of dark matter, which must be uniform on such scales, or adding mass to A and B (their mass is well constrained) or by Modified Newtonian Dynamics" (p. L67).
- The three stars share age, chemistry and motion; the chance of them being unbound is cited as one in a million (Matthews & Gilmore 1993). Matthews & Gilmore suggested a 3σ increase in the A+B mass, which the letter calls much larger than the mass uncertainty (p. L67).
- Wertheimer & Laughlin (2006) are cited as finding that even near apastron, "with extreme assumptions", Proxima was more likely unbound than bound (pp. L67, L69).
- MOND's predictions here are "very similar to the Newtonian" because of its external field effect; MOND gives 0.424 ± 0.001 km/s (Beech 2009) with a₀ = 1.2 × 10⁻¹⁰ m/s², which the letter calls "artificially low" (p. L67).
- QI has "no External Field Effect" because the inertial mass in each interaction depends only on the mutual acceleration, here relative to the A+B barycentre (p. L68).
- Inertial mass cannot become negative: as a → 2c²/Θ the inertial mass collapses and the acceleration rises again, implying a minimum acceleration 2c²/Θ ∼ 2 × 10⁻¹⁰ m/s². Proxima's maximum observed speed, 0.67 km/s, implies a ≈ 2 × 10⁻¹⁰ m/s², "consistent with the predicted minimum acceleration" (p. L68).
- The effect is a "subtle violation of the equivalence principle" that cannot be seen in torsion-balance tests, because the inertial change is independent of the mass (pp. L68–L69).
- The QI formula (eq. 5) is said to be identical to the one used by McCulloch (2012, 2017) for dwarf galaxies, galaxies and clusters (p. L68).
- Future Gaia data on Proxima's orbit may compare Newton, MOND and QI "conclusively" (p. L69).

## Key equations
Transcribed from the PDF (p. L68). Θ = 8.8 × 10²⁶ m is "the co-moving cosmic diameter".

$$m_i = m\left(1 - \frac{2c^2}{|a|\Theta}\right) \qquad (1)$$
$$m_i = m\left(1 - \frac{2c^2r^2}{GM\Theta}\right) \qquad (2)$$
$$\frac{GMm}{r^2} = \frac{mv^2}{r} \;\;(3), \qquad v = \sqrt{\frac{GM}{r}} \;\;(4)\ \text{(Newtonian)}$$
$$v^4 = \frac{2GMc^2}{\Theta} \;\;(5), \qquad v = \left(\frac{2GMc^2}{\Theta}\right)^{1/4} = 0.483 \pm 0.01\ \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}} \;\;(6)\ \text{(QI)}$$

The letter does not derive eq. (5) here; it cites McCulloch (2012).

## Results & predictions
- Newtonian: 0.344 ± 0.018 km/s (eq. 4). Matching the observed speed would need an increase in the A+B mass "about three times larger than the uncertainty in that mass" (p. L68).
- MOND (a₀ = 1.2 × 10⁻¹⁰ m/s², Beech 2009): 0.424 ± 0.001 km/s.
- QI: 0.483 ± 0.01 km/s (eq. 6), against 0.53 ± 0.14 km/s observed; the letter notes QI is closer than MOND.
- Editorial check: with GM = 2.00 × 1.327 × 10²⁰ m³/s² and r = 15 000 AU = 2.24 × 10¹⁵ m, eq. (4) gives 0.344 km/s ✓; eq. (5) with Θ = 8.8 × 10²⁶ m gives 0.483 km/s ✓; (670 m/s)²/r ≈ 2.0 × 10⁻¹⁰ m/s² ✓ (the letter prints this as "a = v²r", evidently meaning v²/r); 2c²/Θ ≈ 2.04 × 10⁻¹⁰ m/s² ✓.
- Editorial check (MOND comparison): Beech's 0.424 km/s equals the deep-MOND result v⁴ = GMa₀ with a₀ = 1.2 × 10⁻¹⁰ m/s² and no external field ((2.65 × 10²⁰ × 1.2 × 10⁻¹⁰)^{1/4} ≈ 0.422 km/s). QI's eq. (5) has the same form with a₀ replaced by 2c²/Θ ≈ 2.0 × 10⁻¹⁰ m/s². So the QI–MOND difference quoted here is essentially the value of the acceleration scale, plus QI's assumption of no external field effect. The letter both quotes this no-EFE MOND number and says MOND fails because of its EFE.
- Editorial check (external field): the Galaxy's acceleration at the Sun is about v²/R ≈ (230 km/s)² / 8.2 kpc ≈ 2 × 10⁻¹⁰ m/s², comparable to the internal acceleration of Proxima's orbit. Whether such an external field matters is exactly where QI's "no EFE" assumption and MOND differ.
- The letter acknowledges simplifications: "nothing has been specified about the orbit of Proxima", only that it stays above QI's minimum acceleration (p. L69).

## Criticisms & responses
- **Bound Newtonian orbit already reported:** [Kervella, Thévenin & Lovis (2017), A&A 598, L7](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/201629930) used HARPS absolute radial velocities and revised α Cen parameters. They concluded that "Proxima and α Cen are gravitationally bound with a high degree of confidence". They derived an orbital period of about 550,000 yr, eccentricity 0.50 (+0.08/−0.09), and apastron at about 13.0 kau.
  - That result conflicts with this letter's premise that Proxima is unbound under Newtonian gravity.
  - The letter does **not** cite or discuss Kervella et al. (2017) (checked against its reference list, p. L69). Its most recent cited analysis of whether Proxima is bound is Wertheimer & Laughlin (2006).
- **Proposed decisive test:** [Banik & Kroupa (2019), MNRAS 487, 1653](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.08264) showed that Proxima's orbit differs significantly between Newton and MOND. They proposed a test with about 10 years of 0.5 μas-precision astrometry (Theia-class). So MOND is not ruled out by existing data on this system. The letter does not cite Banik & Kroupa either.
- **Single-system caveats (factual):** the letter tests one system, with a 26% uncertainty on the observed velocity. It assumes a circular-orbit balance (eq. 3) at the current separation, while Kervella et al. find e ≈ 0.5.
- **MOND comparison (editorial):** see Results; the QI and deep-MOND formulas share the same form, and the letter's MOND number does not include the EFE it invokes against MOND.
- The letter thanks "R. Tattersall and a reviewer for constructive criticism" and DARPA grant HR001118C0125 (p. L69).
- General QI critique: [Renda 2019](../../03-criticism/2019-renda-sceptical/README.md).
- Earlier history: McCulloch's blog references list a 2015 "Testing quantised inertia on the Alpha Centauri system (to be submitted)" alongside Wertheimer & Laughlin (2006, AJ 132, 1995), "Are Proxima and Alpha Centauri gravitationally bound?".
