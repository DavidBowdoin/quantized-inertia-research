# Modified inertial mass from information loss

**Citation:** McCulloch, M. E. & Giné, J. (2017). *Modified inertial mass from information loss*. Modern Physics Letters A 32(28), 1750148 (published online 4 Sept 2017). https://doi.org/10.1142/S0217732317501486

> **PDF not retrievable.** A green-OA accepted manuscript is listed in the University of Plymouth PEARL repository (`pearl.plymouth.ac.uk/context/bms-research/article/2851/viewcontent/papinform5.pdf`), but the server is bot-protected and returned HTML. The publisher version is paywalled. No arXiv preprint was found. This summary is from the abstract (Crossref metadata).

**TL;DR:** QI says inertia comes from Unruh radiation made inhomogeneous by Rindler horizons (giving standard inertia) or the Hubble horizon (giving galaxy rotation without dark matter and cosmic acceleration without dark energy). This paper proposes an alternative derivation from conservation of mass + energy + information ("EMI"). When the area of a Rindler horizon shrinks, information is lost, and mass–energy is released in discrete amounts. Suggested tests: quantisation of inertial mass and acceleration at very high accelerations. The authors also suggest a possible route to the cosmological constant problem.

## Key claims
(From the abstract.)
- MiHsC/QI assumes inertia is caused by Unruh radiation made inhomogeneous by Rindler horizons (from acceleration) or by the Hubble horizon. "The former predicts the standard inertial mass, and the latter predicts galaxy rotation without dark matter and cosmic acceleration without dark energy."
- New proposal: "the sum of mass (M), energy (E) and the information content of horizons (I) is conserved (EMI) so that mass–energy is released in a discrete manner when the area of a Rindler horizon reduces."
- Testable by "looking for the quantization of inertial mass and acceleration at very high accelerations".
- "may provide an explanation for the cosmological constant problem".
