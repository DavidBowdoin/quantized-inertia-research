# Propulsive Forces using High-Q Asymmetric High Energy Laser Resonators

**Citation:** Taylor, T. S. (2017). *Propulsive Forces using High-Q Asymmetric High Energy Laser Resonators*. Journal of the British Interplanetary Society (JBIS), 70, 238–243. BIS refcode 2017.70.238. https://bis-space.com/shop/?p=9802 (the shop listing spells the title "Aysmmetric").

**TL;DR:** The paper applies McCulloch's quantised inertia (described as Unruh radiation modified by a Hubble-scale Casimir effect) to optical cavities. In QI the predicted net thrust scales with cavity Q and input power. Taylor therefore proposes that a high-Q, asymmetric but stable, Fabry–Perot *laser* resonator "should produce very large propulsive forces if the theory is correct", and gives an experiment design. Neunzig, Weikert & Tajmar (2021) later tested this prediction and found no net thrust at the nanonewton level.

> Paywalled. No PDF is stored. This summary is based on the publisher's abstract (BIS shop listing) and on how later papers describe it.

## Key claims
- Spacecraft flyby anomalies (Galileo, NEAR, Cassini, Rosetta) suggest "unusual short-lived changes in their inertia", which one theory (QI) attributes to Unruh radiation modified by a Hubble-scale Casimir effect.
- The EmDrive (an asymmetric microwave resonator) is the laboratory form of that test. At the time, no tests had been reported in the optical regime.
- In QI the net propulsive force is "a function of the cavity Q and input power". Optical resonators reach far higher Q than microwave cavities, so they should give far larger thrust.
- The paper describes an experiment design for the concept.
- Keywords listed by BIS: Casimir Effect; Modified inertia.

## Key equations

## Results & predictions
- Qualitative prediction: "very large propulsive forces" from high-Q asymmetric laser resonators. > [!UNVERIFIED] The specific thrust numbers in the paper were not checked.
- **Test outcome:** Neunzig, Weikert & Tajmar (2021, CEAS Space Journal 14, 45–62; https://doi.org/10.1007/s12567-021-00366-4) tested asymmetric laser resonators, reflective cavities and fibre-optic loops on a dedicated thrust balance. From their abstract: "all devices showed no net thrust within our resolution at the Nanonewton range", which "puts strong limits on all proposed theories like quantised inertia by at least 4 orders of magnitude for the laboratory-scale geometries and power levels used".

## Criticisms & responses
- **Direct experimental test (null result):** [Neunzig, Weikert & Tajmar 2021/2022](../../03-criticism/2022-neunzig-laser-resonators/README.md). They found no thrust at the nN level, with limits on QI "by at least 4 orders of magnitude".
- The EmDrive results that motivated the paper were later attributed to experimental artefacts by the same Dresden group: [2021-tajmar-emdrive-false-positive](../../03-criticism/2021-tajmar-emdrive-false-positive/README.md), [2022-neunzig-emdrive-thrust](../../03-criticism/2022-neunzig-emdrive-thrust/README.md).
- The flyby anomalies cited as motivation remain unexplained but contested; see [flyby-anomaly](../../02-evidence/flyby-anomaly.md).
