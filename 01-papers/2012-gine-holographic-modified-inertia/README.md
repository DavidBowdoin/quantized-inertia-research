# The holographic scenario, the modified inertia and the dynamics of the universe

<!-- local-only-note -->
> 📁 **Local only:** the stored PDF and its rendered figures are kept out of the public repository because this copy's licence (`all-rights-reserved`) does not permit redistribution. Read the paper at [doi:10.1142/S0217732312502082](https://doi.org/10.1142/S0217732312502082).

**Citation:** Giné, J. (2012). *The holographic scenario, the modified inertia and the dynamics of the universe*. Modern Physics Letters A 27(34), 1250208 (13 pp.). Received 3 Sep 2012, accepted 9 Oct 2012, published 25 Oct 2012. [doi:10.1142/S0217732312502082](https://doi.org/10.1142/S0217732312502082)

**About the stored PDF:** `paper.pdf` is the published World Scientific version (owner-supplied, local only; copyrighted). Article pages 1250208-1 to -13 are PDF pp. 1–13. Page references below use the printed article page numbers.

**Authorship:** sole author Jaume Giné (Lleida), confirmed on the PDF. This predates his co-authorship with McCulloch (from 2016), but he later became a regular QI co-author.

**TL;DR:** A largely review-style paper that sets Verlinde's entropic gravity next to McCulloch's MiHsC. It summarises how a holographic screen at the Hubble horizon (Easson, Frampton & Smoot) gives a modified Friedmann acceleration equation, and how Cai, Liu & Li's second "Schwarzschild" screen restores thermal equilibrium. Giné's own contribution is a justification of that second screen as the Gauss's-law mass between the test particle and the observer (Sec. 5.1). He also shows that Funkhouser's de Sitter version of Verlinde's second law reproduces Milgrom's MOND. The "first connection" with MiHsC at the end is the rearrangement of a = a_L + a_H into m_I = m_g(1 − a_H/|a|). No data are fitted.

## Key claims
- Verlinde's entropic derivation of F = ma and Newtonian gravity is summarised (Sec. 2). Giné notes Visser's constraints and says "there are also serious concerns regarding the theoretical viability of Verlinde's model" (p. 1250208-2).
- MiHsC is summarised (Sec. 3) with its Pioneer formula a = GM☉/r² + 0.99 cH₀ and its Tully–Fisher relation v⁴ = GM·2c²/Θ = GMcH₀ (pp. 1250208-4, -5).
- The horizon temperature T_H = ħH/(2πk_B) ≈ 3 × 10⁻³⁰ K corresponds, through the Unruh relation, to a_H = cH ≈ 10⁻⁹ m/s². The entropic force toward the horizon mimics dark energy, with pressure P = −(2/3)ρ_c c² (Sec. 4, after Easson, Frampton & Smoot).
- Giné's justification of the second screen (Sec. 5.1): the mass M_S inside radius R around the observer acts, by Gauss's law, like a black hole at the observer, with a Schwarzschild horizon and temperature T_S. A test particle then feels a_e = a_H − a_S, and a_S falls with distance, "which is what is observed in the universe" (p. 1250208-8).
- With a screen of radius 1/(βH), the modified Friedmann acceleration equation is Eq. 29. β = √2 gives thermal equilibrium (T_H = T_S) and the standard Friedmann equation. Late-time acceleration and inflation then need quantum corrections to the entropy, with fine-tuned higher-order coefficients (pp. 1250208-8, -9; from Cai, Liu & Li).
- Following Cai, Cao & Ohta, the screen around the bulk gives the standard FRW acceleration equation via the Tolman–Komar mass. Adding the Hubble-horizon screen gives Easson et al.'s modified equation, Eq. 23 (Sec. 5.2).
- In de Sitter space Verlinde's model gives m[(a² + k²)^{1/2} − k] = F with k = √(Λ/3), identical to a Milgrom formulation of MOND. So MOND follows from "both models the holographic scenario and the modified inertia" (pp. 1250208-10, -11). But "a simple derivation of MOND from both models does not save MOND from its inherent problems" (p. 1250208-12).
- The "first connection" (Sec. 6, Eqs. 40–44): both models rest on Unruh radiation from acceleration relative to surrounding matter. Writing the measured acceleration as a = a_L + a_H with a_L = GM☉/r² gives a MiHsC-like modified inertia.

## Key equations
Transcribed from the PDF (units restored except in Sec. 5, which sets c = k_B = ħ = 1).

Verlinde relations (Eqs. 1–4):
$$ \Delta S = 2\pi k_B\frac{mc}{\hbar}\Delta x \;\;(1), \qquad F\Delta x = T\Delta S \;\;(2), \qquad T = \frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{\hbar a}{k_B c} \;\;(3), \qquad N = \frac{Ac^3}{G\hbar} = \frac{4\pi R^2c^3}{G\hbar} \;\;(4) $$

MiHsC (Eqs. 7, 10, 11), with β = 0.2 and Θ = 2c/H₀:
$$ m_I = m_i\left(1 - \frac{\beta\pi^2c^2}{|a|\Theta}\right) \sim m_i\left(1 - \frac{2c^2}{|a|\Theta}\right) \;\;(7), \qquad a = \frac{GM_\odot}{r^2} + \tfrac12\beta\pi^2 cH_0 \sim \frac{GM_\odot}{r^2} + 0.99\,cH_0 \;\;(10), \qquad v^4 = GM\frac{2c^2}{\Theta} = GMcH_0 \;\;(11) $$

Horizon temperature and acceleration (Eqs. 12–13):
$$ T_H = \frac{\hbar H}{2\pi k_B} \sim 3\times10^{-30}\ \text{K} \;\;(12), \qquad a_H = \frac{2\pi c k_B T_H}{\hbar} \;\;(13) $$

Friedmann acceleration with a boundary term (Eqs. 22–23), the second screen (Eqs. 24–28) and the result (Eq. 29), in c = k_B = ħ = 1 units:
$$ \frac{\ddot a}{a} = -\frac{4\pi G}{3}(\rho + 3p) + \frac{a_H}{L_b} \;\;(22), \qquad \frac{\ddot a}{a} = -\frac{4\pi G}{3}(\rho + 3p) + H^2 \;\;(23) $$
$$ T_S = \frac{1}{8\pi G M_S} \;\;(24), \qquad a_S = 2\pi T_S = \frac{1}{4GM_S} \;\;(25), \qquad r_S = \frac{8\pi G\rho}{3\beta^3H^3} \;\;(26), \qquad a_e = \beta H - \frac{3\beta^3H^3}{16\pi G\rho} \;\;(28) $$
$$ \frac{\ddot a}{a} = -\frac{4\pi G}{3}(\rho + 3p) + \beta^2H^2 - \frac{3\beta^4H^4}{16\pi G\rho} \;\;(29) $$

MOND from Verlinde in de Sitter space (Eqs. 36, 39):
$$ m\left[(a^2 + k^2)^{1/2} - k\right] = F, \quad k = \sqrt{\Lambda/3} \;\;(36), \qquad m\frac{a^2}{2k} = F \;\;(a/k \ll 1) \;\;(39) $$

The "first connection" (Eqs. 41–44):
$$ a = a_L + a_H \;\;(41), \qquad a\left(1 - \frac{a_H}{|a|}\right) = \frac{GM_\odot}{r^2} \;\;(43), \qquad m_I = m_g\left(1 - \frac{a_H}{|a|}\right) = m_g\left(1 - \frac{2\pi c k_B T_H}{\hbar|a|}\right) \;\;(44) $$

Editorial checks:
- ½βπ² = 0.987 ✓ (Eq. 10), and βπ² = 1.97 ≈ 2 ✓ (Eq. 7).
- From r_S = 2GM_S, T_S = 1/(4πr_S) = 3β³H³/(32π²Gρ) (Eq. 27) and a_S = 2πT_S ✓ (Eq. 28).
- Substituting a_e for a_H with L_b = 1/(βH) in Eq. 22 gives Eq. 29 ✓.
- Setting the last two terms of Eq. 29 equal gives H² = 16πGρ/(3β²), which is 8πGρ/3 for β = √2 ✓.
- In Sec. 5, β is Cai, Liu & Li's screen coefficient. It is unrelated to MiHsC's Wien-law β = 0.2 in Eq. 7.
- Eq. 44 is an algebraic rearrangement of the assumed split a = a_L + a_H (Eq. 41), not a derivation from holography. It is written with m_g, not m_i.

## Data used
- None. The paper is theoretical and partly a review. It cites agreement with SN Ia data for Eq. 29 from Cai, Liu & Li, and says MiHsC's Tully–Fisher relation agrees "with the observed data taking into account the errors bars" (p. 1250208-5). Neither claim is tested here.

## Results & predictions
- No new quantitative results or predictions. Giné's original content is the Gauss's-law justification of the second screen (Sec. 5.1), the identification Λ/3 = H² from comparing Eqs. 23 and 38, and the MiHsC-like Eq. 44.
- Late-time acceleration and inflation are not derived here. The paper defers them to the quantum-corrected entropy of Cai, Liu & Li and Easson et al., which needs fine-tuned coefficients (p. 1250208-9).
- Much of the paper (MOND via Funkhouser, the Pioneer formula, the Tully–Fisher relation, the Easson et al. pressure) overlaps with the companion IJTP paper ([2012-gine-cosmological-holographic](../2012-gine-cosmological-holographic/README.md)), which is cited here as Ref. 11.

## Criticisms & responses
- **Acknowledged in the paper:**
  - Visser's constraints on Verlinde's entropy and temperature functions (p. 1250208-2).
  - The unjustified second screen in Cai, Liu & Li, which Sec. 5.1 aims to fix.
  - The fine-tuning needed for late-time acceleration (p. 1250208-9).
  - The general problems of MOND (p. 1250208-12).
- Editorial notes:
  - The "first connection" between Verlinde and MiHsC (Eq. 44) is a rewriting of the assumed a = a_L + a_H. It does not derive MiHsC's Hubble-scale Casimir mechanism, and Eq. 44 uses m_g where MiHsC modifies m_i.
  - The Pioneer formula (Eq. 10) is presented as an MiHsC success. The Pioneer anomaly has since been attributed to anisotropic thermal recoil ([2012-turyshev-pioneer-thermal](../../03-criticism/2012-turyshev-pioneer-thermal/README.md); see [pioneer-anomaly](../../02-evidence/pioneer-anomaly.md)). That paper was published earlier the same year, and this paper does not cite it.
- On the MiHsC side generally: [Renda (2019)](../../03-criticism/2019-renda-sceptical/README.md) criticises the linear Unruh-spectrum factor behind Eq. 7. Giné's own later work replaces the MiHsC mechanism with other derivations ([2017-gine-inertial-mass-holographic](../2017-gine-inertial-mass-holographic/README.md); [Giné & Luciano 2021](../2021-gine-luciano-inertia-fluctuations/README.md)).
- No published response specific to this paper was found in a brief search.
