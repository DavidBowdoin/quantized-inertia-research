# On the Possible Anisotropy of the Unruh Radiation. Part II: Massive Scalar Field in (3+1)D Space-Time

**Citation:** Kholupenko, E. E. (2022). *On the Possible Anisotropy of the Unruh Radiation. Part II: Massive Scalar Field in (3+1)D Space-Time*. Gravitation and Cosmology, 28(2), 139–152. https://doi.org/10.1134/S0202289322020074

Paywalled, so no PDF is stored. This README is based on the author's abstract, as posted for an Ioffe Institute seminar on 15 March 2022 ([ioffe.ru/astro/Seminars/220315.html](https://www.ioffe.ru/astro/Seminars/220315.html)), and on the reference list via OpenAlex.

**TL;DR:** The paper studies the Unruh effect for a massive scalar field in (3+1)D, for acceleration that starts at a definite moment ("semi-eternal" Rindler motion). It derives the stationary energy–angular distribution of Unruh radiation that a two-opposite-horn antenna would observe. The distribution is found to be significantly anisotropic, so the Unruh radiation "cannot be considered as thermal (equilibrium) radiation". The paper also argues that Fulling modes are not fully appropriate wave-functions for this case, and suggests alternatives.

## Key claims
From the abstract:
- For accelerated motion "with certain beginning moment", the Unruh radiation seen by a two-opposite-horn antenna has a "significantly anisotropic" energy–angular distribution.
- The Unruh radiation then "cannot be considered as thermal (equilibrium) radiation".
- "Fulling modes are not completely relevant wave-functions for description of particles in semi-eternal Rindler space-time. More appropriate candidates are suggested".

## Criticisms & responses
- No critique of this specific paper was found in a brief search.
- **Relevance to QI:**
  - QI assumes that Unruh radiation seen by an accelerated body is made anisotropic by its Rindler horizon. This paper studies anisotropy in standard QFT for a finite-start acceleration.
  - It does not test QI. Its reference list (via OpenAlex) includes Giné & McCulloch (2016) *Inertial mass from Unruh temperatures*, but how that paper is discussed was not checked.
  - The anisotropy of acceleration radiation is an older topic in the literature, which this paper also cites: Israel & Nester 1983, Hinton, Davies & Pfautsch 1983, Grove & Ottewill 1985, and Takagi 1984.
