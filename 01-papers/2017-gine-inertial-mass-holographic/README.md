# Inertial mass of an elementary particle from the holographic scenario

Received 26 Oct 2016, revised 17 Jan 2017, accepted 20 Feb 2017, published 8 Mar 2017. https://doi.org/10.1142/S0217751X17500439

**About the stored PDF:** `paper.pdf` is the published World Scientific version (owner-supplied, local only; copyrighted). Article pages 1750043-1 to -11 are PDF pp. 1–11. Page references below use the printed article page numbers. The PDF prints the issue as "Nos. 6 & 7"; Crossref gives "06n07".

**Authorship:** sole author Jaume Giné (Lleida), confirmed on the PDF. Giné co-authored the 2016 paper this one builds on and many later QI papers with McCulloch, so this is sympathetic rather than independent work.

**TL;DR:** Giné treats an elementary particle as a tiny black hole and applies Verlinde-style entropic-force reasoning to its accelerating Rindler horizon, taken as a half-sphere. The entropic force gives F = M_i a with M_i = c²r/(2G). Giné notes that this is just the Schwarzschild relation r = 2GM/c² for the assumed black hole. Inserting the radius of a "Planck particle", r_p = √(2Gh/c³), gives M_i = √(hc/2G), which the paper says "coincides with the value of the mass of a Planck particle". That mass is √π ≈ 1.77 times the Planck mass. The paper also concludes that the Giné & McCulloch (2016) radiation-pressure derivation "does not give correct results for an elementary particle". It re-derives the MiHsC/MOND-like inertia law and the Easson–Frampton–Smoot entropic dark-energy pressure.

## Key claims
- A "holographic equivalence principle": a particle accelerated toward an event horizon is equivalent to one attracted by a central mass. The acceleration toward the horizon can be read either as Verlinde's entropic force or as the QI radiation imbalance (pp. 1750043-1, -2). "Both arguments are claims because they are based on the existence of effects not universally accepted" (p. 1750043-2).
- With total acceleration a = a_L + a_H and a_H = 2πck_BT_H/ħ = cH, the inertial mass becomes m_I = m_i(1 − a_H/a), which is McCulloch's m_I ≈ m_i(1 − 2c²/aΘ) because 2c²/Θ = cH (pp. 1750043-4, -5). In the deep-MOND regime this gives flat rotation curves.
- Elementary particles are treated as black holes, following the "strong gravity" programme (Caldirola, Salam & Strathdee, Sivaram & Sinha). The particle event horizon is at the Schwarzschild radius (pp. 1750043-5, -6).
- The Rindler horizon at c²/a is approximated by a half-sphere, so A = 4πr²/2 and S_R = k_Bc³πr²/(2Għ) (p. 1750043-7).
- Result: M_i = c²r/(2G). For a Planck particle this "coincides with the value of the mass of a Planck particle m_p", which "is √π times larger than the Planck mass" (p. 1750043-8).
- Applied to the whole universe, the same reasoning gives GM_U = c²R_U and an entropic pressure P = −(2/3)ρ_c c², equal to the measured dark-energy value. The paper credits this to Easson, Frampton & Smoot (p. 1750043-9).
- Limits stated by the paper: the argument does not apply to masses below m_p or to macroscopic bodies, which are not black holes. The elementary-particles-as-black-holes assumption "has several problems not solved up to now"; an electron would be a naked singularity (pp. 1750043-9, -10).

## Key equations
Transcribed from the PDF.

The 2016 result as restated here (Eq. 1, p. 1750043-3):
$$ F_x = -\frac{\pi^2 h a}{48 c\, l_p} \qquad (1) $$
followed in the text by "m_i ∼ π²h/(48c l_p) ∼ 2.75 × 10⁻⁸ kg which is 26% greater than the Planck mass".

Horizon temperature and acceleration (Eqs. 2–3); modified inertia (Eqs. 4–6, 8):
$$ T_H = \frac{\hbar H}{2\pi k_B} \sim 3\times10^{-30}\ \text{K} \;\;(2), \qquad a_H = \frac{2\pi c k_B T_H}{\hbar} \;\;(3) $$
$$ a - a_H = a_L = \frac{GM}{r^2} \;\;(4), \qquad m_I = m_i\left(1 - \frac{a_H}{a}\right) = m_i\left(1 - \frac{2\pi c k_B T_H}{\hbar a}\right) \;\;(6), \qquad m_I \sim m_i\left(1 - \frac{2c^2}{a\Theta}\right) \;\;(8) $$

Entropic-force chain for the particle (Eqs. 15–23, pp. 1750043-6 to -8):
$$ S = \frac{k_B c^3 A}{4G\hbar} = \frac{k_B c^3 \pi r_s^2}{G\hbar} \;\;(15), \qquad F = \frac{dE}{dr} = T\frac{dS}{dr} \;\;(17), \qquad T = \frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{\hbar a}{k_B c} \;\;(19) $$
$$ S_R = \frac{k_B c^3 A}{4G\hbar} = \frac{k_B c^3 \pi r^2}{2G\hbar} \;\;(20), \qquad \frac{dS_R}{dr} = \frac{k_B c^3 \pi r}{G\hbar} \;\;(21) $$
$$ F = M_i a = \frac{c^2 r a}{2G} \;\;(22), \qquad M_i = \frac{c^2 r}{2G} \;\;(23), \qquad M_i = \frac{c^2 r_p}{2G} = \frac{c^2\sqrt{2Gh/c^3}}{2G} = \sqrt{\frac{hc}{2G}} \;\;(24) $$

Cosmic case (Eqs. 27–30):
$$ M_U = \frac{c^2 R_U}{G} \;\;(27), \qquad F = -T_H\frac{dS}{dr} = -\frac{c^4}{G} \;\;(29), \qquad P = \frac{F}{A} = -\frac{c^4}{4\pi G R_U^2} = -\frac{c^2H^2}{4\pi G} = -\frac{2}{3}\rho_c c^2 \;\;(30) $$

Editorial checks:
- Eqs. 19 and 21 multiply to c²ra/(2G); ħ and k_B cancel ✓. Eq. 23 is therefore r = 2GM_i/c², the Schwarzschild radius already assumed, as the paper itself says.
- √(hc/2G) = 3.86 × 10⁻⁸ kg = 1.773 m_P, matching the stated √π ≈ 1.772 ✓.
- The paper writes the de Broglie wavelength as λ = ħ/(mc) but the Planck-particle length as l_p = √(2Gh/c³), with h. Equating ħ/(mc) with 2Gm/c² actually gives m = √(ħc/2G) ≈ 0.71 m_P. The √π factor comes from using h (the Compton wavelength h/mc) in l_p, so the "exact" match depends on which convention is chosen for the target length.
- Eq. 30: with ρ_c = 3H²/(8πG), (2/3)ρ_c c² = c²H²/(4πG) ✓. T_H with H = 2.27 × 10⁻¹⁸ s⁻¹ is 2.8 × 10⁻³⁰ K ✓ (Eq. 2).

## Results & predictions
- M_i = √(hc/2G) ≈ 3.86 × 10⁻⁸ kg for a Planck particle, i.e. √π m_P ≈ 1.77 m_P. The abstract's "exact value of the mass of a Planck particle" refers to this Planck *particle* mass, not the Planck mass m_P = 2.176 × 10⁻⁸ kg that the 2016 result was compared with.
- Editorial check: measured against m_P, the 2016 result (1.29 m_P) is closer than this paper's 1.77 m_P. The "exact" agreement comes from changing the target, not from a better number.
- **26% vs 29%:** the 26% figure in this abstract and on p. 1750043-3 comes from quoting the 2016 mass as 2.75 × 10⁻⁸ kg. The 2016 paper prints 2.799 × 10⁻⁸ kg and 29%; editorial check: π²h/(48c l_P) = 2.81 × 10⁻⁸ kg = 1.29 m_P, so 29% is right ([2016-gine-unruh-temperatures](../2016-gine-unruh-temperatures/README.md)).
- No observational predictions. The MOND-like inertia law (Eq. 6) and the dark-energy pressure (Eq. 30) restate earlier results (Giné 2012; Easson, Frampton & Smoot 2011).
- Editorial check of the paper's electron figures: the classical Schwarzschild radius is 1.35 × 10⁻⁵⁷ m ✓ ("about 10⁻⁵⁷ m"). The Reissner–Nordström charge length √(Ge²/4πε₀c⁴) is 1.4 × 10⁻³⁶ m, about ten times the paper's "about 10⁻³⁷ m". This does not affect the paper's point.

## Criticisms & responses
- **Self-assessment in the paper:** Giné says the result is "not surprising", because Eq. 23 is the Schwarzschild relation of the assumed black-hole particle (p. 1750043-8). He also restricts the result to Planck-mass particles and concedes that the black-hole-particle assumption has unsolved problems (pp. 1750043-9, -10).
- **On the radiation-pressure derivation:** the paper's own conclusion that Refs. 10 and 17 (Giné & McCulloch 2016; McCulloch 2013) "do not give correct results for an elementary particle" is a criticism, from a QI co-author, of QI's main inertia mechanism.
- Editorial notes:
  - Any dimensionally consistent combination of h, c and G gives a mass of order m_P. Recovering √(hc/2G) from a Planck-particle radius defined as √(2Gh/c³) is a consistency check, not a prediction. The paper does not derive the mass of any observed particle.
  - The half-sphere Rindler-horizon area (factor ½) is assumed. Without it Eq. 22 would carry a factor 2, and the result would not match.
  - The ħ/h mismatch noted above means the "exact" match depends on a convention.
- **Later work:** [Renda (2019)](../../03-criticism/2019-renda-sceptical/README.md) criticises the 2013/2016 peak-wavelength derivation that this paper also sets aside. He does not address the holographic route. [Giné & Luciano (2021)](../2021-gine-luciano-inertia-fluctuations/README.md) return to Planck-size constituents ("resonant parts") and a Schwarzschild-radius count n ≃ r_s/ℓ_p = 2M/m_p, extending this paper's black-hole-particle picture to macroscopic bodies.
- No published critique specific to this paper was found.
