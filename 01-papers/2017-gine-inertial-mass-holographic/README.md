# Inertial mass of an elementary particle from the holographic scenario

**Citation:** Giné, J. (2017). *Inertial mass of an elementary particle from the holographic scenario*. International Journal of Modern Physics A 32(06n07), 1750043 (published online 8 March 2017). https://doi.org/10.1142/S0217751X17500439

> [!NOTE]
> Paywalled. No PDF is stored. This summary is from the abstract only (Crossref metadata). Crossref gives the issue as "06n07", not 1 as in the source list.

**TL;DR:** Giné, McCulloch's frequent co-author, starts from the QI picture of inertia. An accelerating object forms a dynamical Rindler horizon behind it, which suppresses Unruh radiation on that side (a "Rindler-scale Casimir effect"). The other side is only slightly reduced by the Hubble-scale Casimir effect, and the net pressure acts as inertia. Earlier versions of this mechanism gave masses twice too large, then within 26% after a correction. Giné instead derives the inertial mass of an elementary particle from a holographic argument and says it gives "the exact value of the mass of a Planck particle" when applied to a Planck particle.

## Key claims
(From the abstract.)
- Recaps the QI inertia mechanism: a Rindler horizon and Rindler-scale Casimir effect on one side, and the Hubble-scale Casimir effect on the other.
- The original model gave masses "twice those expected" (McCulloch 2013). After correction, the prediction "improves to within 26% of the Planck mass" (citing Giné & McCulloch 2016, whose own abstract says 29%).
- Here the inertial-mass expression is "derived from the holographic scenario giving the exact value of the mass of a Planck particle when it is applied to a Planck particle".

## Criticisms & responses
- Recovering the Planck mass exactly from Planck-scale inputs (ħ, c, G) is largely a consistency check: any dimensionally correct combination of these constants gives a mass of order m_P. Whether the derivation adds predictive content cannot be judged from the abstract. (Editorial note.)
- Inconsistency in the record: this abstract says the corrected Giné & McCulloch (2016) prediction is within 26%, while that paper's own abstract says 29%.
