**TL;DR:** Millis, former head of NASA's Breakthrough Propulsion Physics project, now at the Tau Zero Foundation, reviews "space drives" (propulsion by interacting with surrounding space, without propellant). He says the field is at steps 1–3 of the scientific method. Its key issues are conservation of momentum, the lack of an obvious reaction mass, and the net-external-thrust requirement. He then focuses on the open question of what creates inertial frames. Taking a Machian view (inertia due to distant matter), set in Euclidean geometry with the optical–mechanical analogy for gravity and light, he offers a sequence of "what-if" thought experiments in which an inertial frame has a variable "strength" set by surrounding matter. **How QI is treated (settled):** McCulloch appears only once, as an "Author Suggestion" (with Haisch) in the Table 1 row "Inertia by Vacuum or Unruh Effect", classed as *Speculation* with the next step "Derive testable equations", and as reference [61] (McCulloch 2010, EPL). QI is not mentioned, described or assessed anywhere in the text. That is a neutral listing, neither endorsement nor critique.

## Key claims
- **Benefit (Sect. 2, pp. 85–87):** an ideal space drive needs energy quadratic in Δv, a rocket exponential. For a 10³ kg probe, Isp ≈ 10⁴ s and a 100-year rendezvous with Alpha Centauri (Δv ≈ 10⁷ m/s each way), the space drive needs ≈ 10¹⁷ J against ≈ 10¹¹¹ J for the rocket, "a difference of 94 orders of magnitude" (p. 87). The introduction rounds this to "about 100 orders of magnitude" for interstellar missions (p. 85). "These comparisons are only to illustrate the gains sought, not an assertion of definitive results."
- **Problem statement (Sect. 3, p. 87):** a space drive must satisfy conservation of momentum and energy, satisfy the net-external-force requirement, and be controllable. Exceptions he notes: Riemannian-geometry transport (warp drives, wormholes), Wanser's analysis of the Mach Effect Thruster, and negative-mass propulsion (Bondi 1957, Forward 1990).
- **Indigenous reaction mass (Sect. 5, p. 88):** candidate mass densities: CMB ~10⁻³¹ kg/m³, dark matter ~10⁻²⁷, dark energy ~10⁻²⁶, interstellar hydrogen ~10⁻²¹, and quantum vacuum / "spacetime itself" 10⁻²⁶ to 10⁹⁸ kg/m³ depending on cut-off. (The text layer drops the minus signs; the signs here follow the dark-energy and Planck-cut-off values stated in Sect. 5.3.) Casimir forces are measured but small (~10⁻⁹ N) and symmetric. Millis also says a mass-density estimate in his own earlier book "was found to be in error" (p. 88).
- **Riemannian vs Machian frames (Sect. 6, pp. 88–89):** Riemannian geometry "assumes that inertial frames are an intrinsic, unalterable property of spacetime". Within it, "there is no means in this formalism to explicitly demonstrate that the motion of an object, using warp drives or wormholes, will satisfy global conservation of momentum over its initial and final locations" (p. 89). Mach's principle has many formulations (21 listed by Barbour & Pfister), "some of which have been dismissed"; its viability, and attempts to link inertia or gravity to the quantum vacuum, "remain open" (pp. 89–90).
- **Quantum-vacuum inertia (Sect. 6.5, p. 90):** names Sakharov 1968, Rueda & Haisch 1998, Puthoff 2002 and Maclay 2010; "This is a topic of continued study." McCulloch is not named here.
- **Thought experiments (Sect. 8, pp. 90–93):** empty space without inertial frames; a mass shell creating an inertial frame inside it; two nested shells whose frames superpose; a scalar "inertial frame strength" (Eq. 6); weaker frames giving lower effective inertia (Fig. 3) and higher lightspeed (Fig. 4); and a possibly finite propagation speed for frame changes. Sect. 8.8 lists open alternatives at each step. "There is no assertion at this time that these exercises provide accurate representations of the physical universe" (p. 90).
- **Conclusion (Sect. 9, p. 93):** "At this stage, the physics from which to engineer a space drive does not exist". Few concepts have testable hypotheses.

## Key equations
Energy for an ideal space drive vs a rocket, rendezvous mission (Eqs. 1–2; the factor 2 for accelerate-plus-decelerate):
$$ E_D = (2)\,\tfrac{1}{2} m\,\Delta v^2, \qquad E_R = \tfrac{1}{2} m v_{ex}^2\left(e^{(2)\Delta v/v_{ex}} - 1\right) $$
Optical–mechanical analogy: refractive index of space near mass M (Eq. 4, de Felice; Eq. 5, Puthoff):
$$ n = \frac{\left(1 + \frac{GM}{2rc^2}\right)^3}{1 - \frac{GM}{2rc^2}}, \qquad n = e^{2GM/rc^2} = 1 + \frac{2GM}{rc^2} + \cdots $$
Provisional scalar "inertial frame strength" inside a shell of source matter M_F and radius R (Eq. 6; S_F a scaling factor):
$$ \Phi_F = \frac{S_F M_F}{4\pi R} $$

## Criticisms & responses
- **Treatment of QI (previously UNVERIFIED, now settled):** QI/MiHsC is only listed in Table 1 under "Inertia by Vacuum or Unruh Effect" with Haisch, as *Speculation* whose next step is to "Derive testable equations", citing McCulloch 2010. There is no discussion, endorsement or critique of QI in the text. Citing it this way is not endorsement (see the editorial rules in `AGENTS.md`).
- **Earlier quotes corrected:** this entry previously quoted the IAC-16 abstract ("it is unknown if any of these lines of inquiry will be fruitful"). The journal abstract reads "It is unknown if this line of inquiry will be fruitful" (p. 85).
- **Limits of the paper (editorial):** the thought experiments are explicitly unformalised and untested; Millis gives no criterion that would separate his Machian "frame strength" from standard general relativity in an experiment. No published response to this paper was found.
- In later commentary (e.g. Centauri Dreams, 2018) Millis discussed EmDrive and Mach-effect thruster test results. See [07-media](../../07-media/) and [2018-tajmar-spacedrive](../../04-experiments/2018-tajmar-spacedrive/README.md).
