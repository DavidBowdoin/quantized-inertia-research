# Inertial frames and breakthrough propulsion physics

**Citation:** Millis, M. G. (2017). *Inertial frames and breakthrough propulsion physics*. Acta Astronautica 138, 85–94 (September 2017). https://doi.org/10.1016/j.actaastro.2017.05.028. Based on the IAC-16 paper of the same title (IAC-16,C4,8,10,x33973; Tau Zero Foundation): [IAF listing](https://iafastro.directory/iac/archive/browse/IAC-16/C4/8/33973/).

> [!NOTE]
> Paywalled. No PDF is stored. ScienceDirect blocked automated access. This summary is from the IAC-16 conference abstract and Crossref reference metadata.

**TL;DR:** Millis is former head of NASA's Breakthrough Propulsion Physics project, now at the Tau Zero Foundation. He argues that a propellantless space drive would need new physics, and he picks the unknown *origin of inertial frames* as a key open question. He discusses Mach's principle, whose "final viability remains unknown", and argues that the Riemannian-geometry formalism behind warp-drive and wormhole ideas cannot handle momentum conservation for such transport. His reference list includes McCulloch (2010), "Minimum accelerations from quantized inertia" (EPL 90, 29001), alongside Sciama (1953) and Rueda & Haisch (1998) on the origin of inertia. QI therefore appears as one of the open inertia hypotheses he cites, not as an endorsed mechanism.

## Key claims
(From the IAC-16 abstract.)
- "further advances in physics are required" to achieve a propellantless space drive. Past space-drive concepts were compared with known physics to identify next-step research questions.
- "Inertial frames are the reference frames upon which the laws of motion and the conservation laws are defined", yet "it is still unknown what causes inertial frames to exist."
- Mach's principle, "that an inertial frame, here, is created by the surrounding distribution of mass", is discussed. Some variants have been dismissed, but its final viability is unknown.
- The predominant Riemannian formalism of general relativity, the basis of wormhole and warp-drive theories, "has been found to be unable to address conservation of momentum for transport based on those methods."
- The work is "one step in a process to seek spaceflight physics breakthroughs", not a device proposal, and "it is unknown if any of these lines of inquiry will be fruitful."
