# A Toy Cosmology Using a Hubble-Scale Casimir Effect

**Citation:** McCulloch, M. E. (2014). *A Toy Cosmology Using a Hubble-Scale Casimir Effect*. Galaxies 2(1), 81–88. https://doi.org/10.3390/galaxies2010081 (CC BY 3.0)

**TL;DR:** McCulloch treats the Hubble volume as a black hole turned inside out. Its edge emits Hawking radiation inwards, and only wavelengths that fit exactly into the Hubble scale are allowed (a "Hubble-scale Casimir effect", or "horizon wave censorship"). From this he gets an equilibrium mass for the observable universe (~4.6×10^52 kg), which he compares with the observed visible mass (~1.8×10^52±1 kg) as a possible answer to the flatness problem. He also claims the model accounts for the ~5% suppression of low-l CMB power. The paper calls itself an "incomplete toy model".

## Key claims
- The visible mass of the observable universe, ~1.8×10^52±1 kg, is indistinguishable within its error bars from the critical (flat-universe) value. The paper calls the reason for this "not known" and offers an alternative to inflation (pp. 81–82).
- Recaps MiHsC/quantised inertia (QI): inertia comes from Unruh radiation, and only Unruh wavelengths that fit into the Hubble scale are allowed. This gives a modified inertial mass (Eq. 4–5) and a minimum acceleration (pp. 82–83).
- The Hubble volume's boundary is likened to a black-hole event horizon that emits Hawking radiation inwards. Disallowing wavelengths that do not fit gives an equilibrium mass $M_{eq}$ (Sec. 3).
- In the model, the mass of the observable universe grows linearly with its diameter, so it increases with time (similar to Steady State theory). Unlike Steady State, the model also says the universe was hotter in the past (Sec. 4).
- Applying the same suppression to CMB "patterns of variation" predicts a 5.5% drop in variation for l < 40. The paper compares this with the 5–10% deficit reported by Planck (pp. 85–86).
- Rearranging the mass formula makes the speed of light equal to twice the escape velocity for $M_{eq}$ at the Hubble scale (Eq. 12).
- Says the earlier calculation in McCulloch (2007) should have set λ = 2Θ, not λ = Θ (p. 84).

## Key equations
Unruh temperature and Wien wavelength (Eqs. 1–3):
$$T = \frac{\hbar a}{2\pi c k}, \qquad \lambda = \frac{\beta h c}{kT} = \frac{4\pi^2 \beta c^2}{a}$$

MiHsC modified inertial mass (Eqs. 4–5), with Θ the Hubble diameter:
$$m_i = m_g\left(1 - \frac{\lambda}{4\Theta}\right) \approx m_g\left(1 - \frac{2c^2}{|a|\Theta}\right)$$

Hawking temperature and the matching wavelength (Eqs. 6–7):
$$T \sim \frac{\hbar c^3}{8\pi G M k}, \qquad \lambda \sim \frac{16\pi^2 G M \beta}{c^2}$$

Modified inward Hawking emission (Eq. 10) and equilibrium mass (Eq. 11):
$$E' = \sigma T^4\left(1 - \frac{4\pi^2 G M \beta}{\Theta c^2}\right), \qquad M_{eq} = \frac{\Theta c^2}{4\pi^2 G\beta} \sim \frac{\Theta c^2}{8G} \sim \frac{c^3}{4GH}$$

Speed of light as an escape velocity (Eq. 12), and horizon temperature (Eq. 14):
$$c = \sqrt{\frac{8GM_{eq}}{\Theta}}, \qquad T = \frac{hc\beta}{2k\Theta}$$

CMB suppression by multipole (Eq. 16):
$$E' = E\left(1 - \frac{1}{2L}\right)$$

## Data used
- Estimated number of stars in the observable universe (~9×10^21), from Hubble Space Telescope results, combined with a solar-mass average. The paper also cites Funkhouser (2006), whose estimate is 2.4×10^52 kg.
- Hubble constant with an assumed 9% uncertainty (Freedman 2001 HST Key Project).
- Planck 2013 CMB power spectra (Planck Collaboration XV) for the low-l anomaly.

## Results & predictions
- Equilibrium (minimum) mass of the observable universe: **$M_{eq}$ ≈ 4.6 ± 0.4 × 10^52 kg**. Observed baryonic estimate: **1.8 × 10^52±1 kg**. The paper says these agree within the stated error bars.
- Earlier crude estimate (abrupt cutoff, from McCulloch 2007): M = 1.165 × 10^52 kg.
- $M_{eq}$ is half the Steady State Theory mass, $M_{SST} = c^3/2GH$.
- Predicted reduction of CMB variation for l < 40: **5.5%**. Planck reported a 5–10% deficit at 2.5–3σ significance.
- Qualitative predictions: the observable universe gains mass as it expands, and it was hotter in the past.

## Criticisms & responses
- None found that address this paper specifically.
- General critiques of MiHsC/QI also cover the ingredients used here: applying Wien's law and Unruh/Hawking radiation at Hubble scales, and the "Hubble-scale Casimir effect" assumption. See Renda (2019), [../../03-criticism/2019-renda-sceptical/README.md](../../03-criticism/2019-renda-sceptical/README.md).
- Read the observed-mass comparison with care. The paper's observed value carries an error of ±1 in the exponent. The predicted 4.6×10^52 kg is about 2.5 times the central estimate, and the agreement claim rests on that wide uncertainty.
- > [!UNVERIFIED] No independent check of the CMB-suppression claim (Eq. 16) against later Planck releases was found.
