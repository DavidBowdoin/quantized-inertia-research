# On the Apparent Discretization of Spacetime and Its Connection with the Cosmological Constant

**Citation:** Giné, J. & Luciano, G. G. (2024). *On the Apparent Discretization of Spacetime and Its Connection with the Cosmological Constant*. Universe, 10(3), 142 (11 pp.). Received 18 Dec 2023, revised 30 Jan 2024, accepted 12 Mar 2024, published 14 Mar 2024 (dates as printed on the PDF). https://doi.org/10.3390/universe10030142

**About the stored PDF:** `paper.pdf` is the published MDPI version, downloaded from mdpi-res.com. The PDF and Crossref both state CC BY 4.0. Page references below are the printed "n of 11" page numbers.

**Authorship:** Jaume Giné (Departament de Matemàtica) and Giuseppe Gaetano Luciano (corresponding author), both at the Universitat de Lleida. McCulloch is not a co-author. Giné is a regular McCulloch co-author, and this paper reuses the QI horizon picture, so it is sympathetic rather than independent work.

**TL;DR:** Section 2 gives a heuristic measurement argument that the smallest measurable length is the Planck length, so spacetime behaves *as if* it were discrete, and vacuum fluctuations arise only at "resonant points" a Planck length apart. Section 3 then estimates the cosmological constant. Following McCulloch (2007), Giné & McCulloch (2016) and Giné & Luciano (2021), the paper treats the Hubble horizon as damping Unruh-like radiation (a "Hubble-scale Casimir-like effect"). It applies the uncertainty principle to fluctuations from that horizon with Δx ≃ 2πr_u, where r_u = c/H₀, and shares the energy among the N₀ = r_u/ℓ_P resonant points along a radius. It sets this equal to the cosmological-constant energy per Planck cell inside the horizon and gets Λ = 2H₀²/c². With Ω_Λ ≃ 0.69 the observed value is 3Ω_Λ H₀²/c² ≃ 2.07 H₀²/c², which the paper calls "good agreement". The authors stress that the argument is heuristic.

## Key claims
- Measuring an object needs probe photons with wavelength no larger than the object and energy no larger than the object's. Applying this to the smallest black hole gives a minimum mass of about the Planck mass and a minimum measurable distance of about the Planck length (Eqs. 5–11, pp. 3–4). The paper says this is "a well-known result" but derived in a new way (p. 4).
- Spacetime is therefore *apparently* discrete: whether or not it is genuinely discrete, quantum fluctuations behave as if created at points a Planck length apart. Only fluctuations with λ ≳ ℓ_P count (p. 4). The time–energy uncertainty relation then gives the Planck time as the lifetime of such fluctuations (Eqs. 14–16, p. 5).
- The paper recalls Giné's heuristic derivation of the Unruh temperature from the uncertainty principle, with Δx ≃ πd and d = c²/a the distance to the Rindler horizon (Eqs. 19–21, pp. 6–7, citing Giné 2018).
- **QI ingredient (Sec. 3.3, p. 7):** citing McCulloch 2007, Giné & McCulloch 2016 and Giné & Luciano 2021 (Refs. 50–52), the Hubble horizon is said to damp Unruh radiation, giving a Hubble-scale Casimir-like effect for every observer. The same uncertainty-principle reasoning is applied to fluctuations from the horizon at r_u = c/H₀, with Δx ≃ 2πr_u; the factor 2 is attributed to the horizon appearing isotropically (Ref. 53).
- Every Planck-size point resonates with the same energy, because whether a point lies on the horizon or inside it depends only on where the observer is (p. 8). Equating the two energy estimates gives Λ = 2H₀²/c² (Eq. 29).
- The paper says that this proposal is "not directly connected with any modification of the Heisenberg relation". It relies instead on the conjecture that spacetime is apparently granulated at the Planck scale (p. 6). The GUP, EUP and GEUP forms are reviewed (Eq. 18) but not used in the Λ estimate.
- The paper presents the result as offering "some hints" towards solving the cosmological-constant problem (abstract).

## Key equations
Transcribed from the PDF.

Minimal measurable mass and length (Eqs. 10, 11):
$$ m_s \gtrsim \sqrt{\frac{hc}{2G}} \gtrsim \sqrt{\frac{\hbar c}{G}} = m_P, \qquad r_s \gtrsim \frac{h}{m_P c} = \sqrt{\frac{hG}{c^3}} = \ell_P $$

Unruh temperature from the uncertainty principle (Eqs. 20, 21), with Δx ≃ πd and d = c²/a:
$$ \Delta x\,\Delta E \simeq \frac{\hbar c}{2}, \qquad \Delta E \simeq \frac{\hbar c}{2\pi d} = \frac{\hbar a}{2\pi c} $$

Hubble-horizon fluctuations (Eqs. 23–25), with Δx ≃ 2πr_u = 2πN₀ℓ_P and N₀ ≃ r_u/ℓ_P:
$$ \Delta E \simeq \frac{\hbar c}{2\Delta x} \simeq \frac{\hbar c}{4\pi N_0 \ell_P}, \qquad \Delta E_i = \frac{\Delta E}{N_0} \simeq \frac{\hbar c\,\ell_P}{4\pi r_u^2} $$

Bulk estimate (Eqs. 26–28) and result (Eq. 29):
$$ \rho_{vac} = \frac{\Lambda c^4}{8\pi G}, \qquad \frac{E_T}{N} = \rho_{vac}\,\ell_P^3, \qquad \Lambda = \frac{2G\hbar}{c^3\ell_P^2 r_u^2} = \frac{2}{r_u^2} = \frac{2H_0^2}{c^2} $$

Editorial checks:
- Equating Eq. 25 and Eq. 28 gives Λ = 2Għ/(c³ℓ_P²r_u²). With ℓ_P² = ħG/c³ this is 2/r_u² ✓, and with r_u = c/H₀ it is 2H₀²/c² ✓.
- 3 × 0.69 = 2.07 ✓.
- Eq. 11 writes ℓ_P = √(hG/c³) with h, while Eq. 10 uses ħ for m_P. The paper says numerical factors are irrelevant for its order-of-magnitude purpose (p. 4).

## Data used
- None (theoretical). The only observational input is Ω_Λ ≃ 0.69 (Particle Data Group 2020, Ref. 54).

## Results & predictions
- Λ = 2H₀²/c², compared with 3Ω_Λ H₀²/c² ≃ 2.07 H₀²/c² (Eqs. 29–30, p. 8).
- No new testable prediction is made. The outlook proposes studying links between minimal length and non-extensive (Tsallis-like) statistics (p. 9).
- Editorial notes:
  - The Planck length cancels from the final result. Once the horizon energy and the bulk energy are both divided into Planck cells, what remains is Λ ∝ 1/r_u² = H₀²/c². That scaling follows from dimensional analysis whenever the only large scale is the Hubble radius. The content of the claim is the O(1) prefactor 2. That prefactor comes from the choices Δx ≃ 2πr_u (with the "isotropic" factor 2) and ΔE ≃ ħc/2Δx, which the paper motivates heuristically rather than derives.
  - The horizon distance used is r_u = c/H₀, the Hubble radius. It is not QI's Θ (2.6–2.7 × 10²⁶ m in early papers, 8.8 × 10²⁶ m from 2017). QI enters only as the qualitative "Hubble-scale Casimir-like" damping picture.
  - Because the result is tied to the present H₀, it gives Λ ∝ H(t)² if applied at other epochs. The paper does not discuss this, and a Λ that varies with H is no longer a constant.
  - Giné's sole-author follow-up, [Symmetry 17, 634 (2025)](https://doi.org/10.3390/sym17050634) (not archived here; it does not use the QI horizon-damping ingredient), redoes the estimate with Boyer's spherical-shell Casimir energy. It gets Λ ≃ 1.16 H₀²/c², about 56% of the observed value. The O(1) prefactor therefore depends on which heuristic is used.

## Criticisms & responses
- No published critique of this specific paper was found in a brief search.
- The paper cites Bishop et al. (2022, Ref. 45), who argue that GUP models in their current form cannot address the cosmological-constant problem. It replies that its own estimate does not depend on modifying the Heisenberg relation (p. 6).
- The QI horizon-damping picture it relies on is disputed. Renda (2019) argues that the Hubble-cavity Unruh calculation does not give QI's assumed form ([2019-renda-sceptical](../../03-criticism/2019-renda-sceptical/README.md)). Dickmann et al. (2023) argue that QI's use of the Casimir effect contradicts standard Casimir physics ([2023-dickmann-casimir-contradicts](../../03-criticism/2023-dickmann-casimir-contradicts/README.md)). Neither critique addresses this paper directly.
