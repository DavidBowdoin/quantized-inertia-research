**TL;DR:** A two-page proposal, not a QI paper. McCulloch suggests that a rotor shaped like a swastika, exposed to waves arriving from all directions, will turn towards its arm-tips because the inner faces of the arms are sheltered, by analogy with the Casimir effect and Boersma's "maritime Casimir effect" between ships. He derives a maximum-force estimate from an empirical wave-slamming pressure, proposes the rotor as a simple one-moving-part wave-energy converter, and speculates that smaller versions could extract energy from sound, Brownian motion and "potentially the zero point field". No experiment or calculation for a specific device is reported. **Neither MiHsC nor quantised inertia is mentioned anywhere in the paper**, so `status` is `neutral`.

## Key claims
- A swastika-shaped rotor in an isotropic wave field rotates "in the direction its arms are pointing (towards the arm-tips)" because of a sheltering effect (abstract; p. 140).
- The inspiration is Boersma's proposal that two ships at sea shelter each other from waves and are pushed together, a maritime analogue of the Casimir effect; the paper notes the water-wave analogue has been measured (Denardo et al. 2009) (p. 139).
- Mechanism (Fig. 1, p. 139): the corner areas A and B enclosed by an arm are "sheltered zones rather like harbours" where only waves with nodes at the walls can exist. If the ocean wavelength does not fit, fewer waves exist there, while the open area C still has waves. The outer half of each arm is then pushed towards the sheltered side; the inner half feels no net force. The total is four times the force on one arm.
- Advantages claimed over existing wave-energy converters: one moving part, no preferred wave direction, works with isotropic random waves, and it would also rotate (the opposite way, like an anemometer) in a surface current (p. 140).
- Speculation: at smaller scales the same shape could be spun by sound waves, Brownian motion, or "on the nanoscale by the zero-point field" (p. 140).
- The paper suggests the sheltering idea "might explain" the reported motion of boomerang-shaped colloidal particles under Brownian motion (Chakrabarty et al. 2013) towards their concave side (p. 140).
- It "now needs to be tested experimentally" (p. 140).

## Key equations
Wave impact (slamming) pressure, quoted from refs [7, 8], with K an empirical constant between 3 and 10, ρ the water density and C the wave phase speed (Eq. 1, p. 139):
$$ P = \frac{F}{A} = K\rho C^2 $$
Force on the outer half of one arm, with D the half-length of the arm and h the wave height (Eqs. 2–3, p. 140):
$$ F = KA\rho C^2 = KDh\rho C^2 $$
Maximum acceleration of the arm, neglecting friction, for rotor mass m (Eq. 4, p. 140):
$$ a = \frac{KDh\rho C^2}{m} $$
- Editorial check: Eq. 3 is dimensionally a force (Pa × m²). Eq. 4 treats the arm's motion as linear (F = ma); no torque, moment of inertia, rotation rate or extractable power is derived, so the paper gives no energy or power estimate despite its title.
- Editorial check: Eq. 4 is an upper bound that assumes the waves in A and B are cancelled completely (p. 140). No estimate of how much they are actually reduced is given.

## Data used
- None. The only numbers are background figures: 7–10 GW of UK wave energy potentially extractable against 65 GW peak demand (citing Drew et al. 2009), and an 81% conversion efficiency for Salter's Duck (citing Falnes 2007) (p. 139).

## Results & predictions
- Prediction: the rotor turns towards its arm-tips in isotropic waves, and the opposite way in a steady surface current (p. 140).
- Test conditions proposed: a wave tank large enough that wall interactions are reduced and area C is not damped; waves similar to or shorter than the arm width (longer waves "will not be able to resolve the shape of the arms so there will be no rotation"); a light rotor with arms projecting well above the water; high waves with large phase speed; care to reduce residual rotational flows (p. 140).
- No test result has been found in this repository's sources.
  > [!UNVERIFIED] Whether McCulloch or anyone else later built and tested a swastika rotor in a wave tank was not checked. OpenAlex records 0 citations of this paper (checked 2026-10).

## Criticisms & responses
- No published criticism of this paper was found.
- **Relation to QI:** the paper does not mention MiHsC, quantised inertia, Unruh radiation or horizons. Its link to McCulloch's QI work is conceptual only: the same "waves that do not fit between boundaries are suppressed" Casimir-style reasoning underlies MiHsC (see [2013-mcculloch-asymmetric-casimir](../2013-mcculloch-asymmetric-casimir/README.md) and the [Casimir effect](../../05-context/casimir-effect.md) context note). It is therefore not evidence for or against QI.
- Editorial note: extracting net work from Brownian motion (thermal equilibrium) with a passive rotor would contradict the second law of thermodynamics; this is the point of Feynman's well-known ratchet-and-pawl analysis. Extracting usable energy from the quantum vacuum ground state raises the same kind of objection. The paper does not discuss either issue. Ocean waves and sound are not in equilibrium, so the wave-energy proposal itself is not affected by this objection.
- Editorial note: the paper calls the shape "a swastika, or Greek letter Chi"; the figure shows a four-armed hooked cross (a gammadion), not the letter χ.
- Editorial note: the sheltering argument is qualitative. It assumes the sheltered corners suppress the incident waves, but gives no wave-mechanical calculation (diffraction, reflection from the arm faces, or which wavelengths "fit") to show that a net torque survives.
  > [!UNVERIFIED] The paper's reading of Chakrabarty et al. (2013), that boomerang colloids move "towards their concave sides", has not been checked against that paper.
- Venue: Progress in Physics is a low-impact, rapid-turnaround journal (this paper was accepted three days after submission, p. 140).
