# Cosmological Consequences of the Holographic Scenario

**Citation:** Giné, J. (2012). *Cosmological Consequences of the Holographic Scenario*. International Journal of Theoretical Physics 52(1), 53–61. Received 16 Apr 2012, accepted 3 Aug 2012, published online 19 Aug 2012 (dates as printed on the PDF); issue dated 2013. [doi:10.1007/s10773-012-1298-0](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10773-012-1298-0)

**About the stored PDF:** `paper.pdf` is the published Springer version (owner-supplied, local only; © Springer Science+Business Media 2012). Journal pages 53–61 are PDF pp. 1–9. Page references below use the printed journal page numbers.

**Authorship:** sole author Jaume Giné (Lleida), confirmed on the PDF. This predates his co-authorship with McCulloch (from 2016), but he later became a regular QI co-author.

**TL;DR:** A short theoretical paper arguing that Verlinde's holographic (entropic) gravity and McCulloch's MiHsC lead to the same MOND-like phenomenology. Giné expands his own MOND interpolation function for a ≫ a₀ to get a "modified inertia" m_i(1 − a₀/|a|), with a₀ ~ cH₀. Plugging it into McCulloch's arguments reproduces his treatments of the Pioneer anomaly, the flyby anomalies, the Tajmar effect and the minimum disc-galaxy mass (~10⁹ M☉ within 500 pc). The paper concludes that Verlinde's model therefore "also gives a promising possible explanation" of these anomalies. It also acknowledges that "there is nothing yet to prove experimentally" that either model is correct.

## Key claims
- Verlinde's entropic gravity, in an asymptotically de Sitter universe, gives Funkhouser's modified second law m[(a² + k²)^{1/2} − k] = F with k = √(Λ/3). This is identical to a Milgrom MOND formulation, with 2k playing the role of a₀, so "the MOND can be derived from the Verlinde's theory" (pp. 54–55).
- Giné's own interpolation function (Ref. 27, Giné 2009), with an effective acceleration a_e = a₀(1 − R_obs/R_U), recovers McCulloch's Pioneer formula a = GM/r² + 0.99 cH₀ in the limit a ≫ a₀. Here a₀ ~ cH₀ is justified from GM_U = c²R_U and Mach's principle (p. 55).
- The paper acknowledges the size mismatch: cH₀ = 6.9 × 10⁻¹⁰ m/s², while the a₀ "empirically derived from the galaxy data is typically 2 × 10⁻¹⁰ m/s²" (p. 55).
- It also acknowledges that the bound/unbound problem is open. Any gravitational explanation must say why the planets do not show the Pioneer anomaly, and conventional thermal explanations of the anomaly have appeared, including Turyshev et al. 2012 (p. 56).
- Defining m_i,m = m_i(1 − a₀/|a|) = m_g(1 − a₀/|a|) and substituting it into McCulloch's momentum-conservation arguments gives the same flyby and Tajmar-effect results as McCulloch (pp. 56–57). The paper notes that this "violates the equivalence principle", citing McGaugh that the principle is untested at very low accelerations (p. 56).
- Minimum disc-galaxy mass: attributing the extra acceleration c²/R_U to apparent dark mass gives M_dark = c²r²/(GR_U) = 2.3 × 10³⁹ kg = 1.1 × 10⁹ M☉ within 500 pc. This is close to the observed cutoff reported by McGaugh et al. (2010) (p. 57). Applied to the whole universe, M_U,dark = c²R_U/G = 2 × 10⁵³ kg, which equals M_U by Eq. 13. Giné takes this as an explanation of the flatness problem (pp. 57–58).
- Conclusion: the two mechanisms "must be put to the test on as many different and independent phenomena as possible, in order to discriminate between them". Suggested benchmarks are the Moon's eccentricity anomaly, Saturn's perihelion, solar mass variation and the secular increase of the AU (p. 58).

## Key equations
Transcribed from the PDF.

Giné's interpolation function, with a_e = a₀(1 − R_obs/R_U)R̂_U (Eq. 1, p. 54), and Funkhouser's de Sitter second law (Eq. 2):
$$ m_i\frac{|\mathbf a|}{|\mathbf a + \mathbf a_e|}\,\mathbf a = \mathbf F \;\;(1), \qquad m\left[(a^2 + k^2)^{1/2} - k\right] = F, \quad k = \sqrt{\Lambda/3} \;\;(2) $$

McCulloch's Pioneer acceleration and its MOND counterpart (Eqs. 3–4, p. 55):
$$ a = \frac{GM}{r^2} + \tfrac12\beta\pi^2cH_0 \sim \frac{GM}{r^2} + 0.99\,cH_0 \;\;(3), \qquad a = \frac{GM}{r^2} + a_0 \;\;(4) $$

Machian a₀ argument and MiHsC's Tully–Fisher relation (Eqs. 5–6):
$$ m_i a_0 = G\frac{m_g M_U}{R_U^2} \;\;(5), \qquad v^4 = GM\frac{2c^2}{\Theta} = GMcH_0 \;\;(6) $$

Modified inertia from the strong-Newtonian expansion of Eq. 1 (Eqs. 7–8, p. 56):
$$ m_i\left(1 - \frac{a_0}{|a|}\right)\mathbf a = \mathbf F \;\;(7), \qquad m_{i,m} = m_i\left(1 - \frac{a_0}{|a|}\right) = m_g\left(1 - \frac{a_0}{|a|}\right) \;\;(8) $$

Disc galaxies and the universe (Eqs. 9–13, pp. 57–58):
$$ a \sim \frac{GM}{r^2} + cH_0 = \frac{GM}{r^2} + \frac{c^2}{R_U} \;\;(9), \qquad M_{dark} = \frac{c^2r^2}{GR_U} \;\;(11), \qquad M_{U,dark} = \frac{c^2R_U}{G} = 2\times10^{53}\ \text{kg} \;\;(12), \qquad \frac{GM_U}{c^2R_U} = 1 \;\;(13) $$

Editorial checks:
- ½βπ² = 0.987 with β = 0.2 ✓ (Eq. 3).
- With c²/R_U = cH₀ = 6.9 × 10⁻¹⁰ m/s² and r = 500 pc, Eq. 11 gives 2.46 × 10³⁹ kg (1.24 × 10⁹ M☉), against the printed 2.3 × 10³⁹ kg. The printed value implies a slightly smaller cH₀, ≈ 6.4 × 10⁻¹⁰ m/s². 2.3 × 10³⁹ kg = 1.16 × 10⁹ M☉ ✓.
- c²R_U/G = c³/(GH₀) = 1.75 × 10⁵³ kg for H₀ = 2.3 × 10⁻¹⁸ s⁻¹, consistent with the printed "2 × 10⁵³ kg" at the one-figure level ✓.
- R_U = c/H₀ here, the 2007–2013 convention (Θ = 2R_U ≈ 2.6 × 10²⁶ m).

## Data used
- No new data. Values quoted:
  - Pioneer anomaly 8.74 ± 1.33 × 10⁻¹⁰ m/s², from Anderson et al. (p. 55)
  - cH₀ = 6.9 × 10⁻¹⁰ m/s², and empirical galactic a₀ "typically 2 × 10⁻¹⁰ m/s²" (p. 55)
  - the minimum disc-galaxy mass of 10⁹ M☉ within the central 500 pc, from McGaugh et al. 2010 (p. 57)

## Results & predictions
- No new predictions. The paper reproduces McCulloch's MiHsC results through a MOND-type interpolation:
  - a = GM/r² + 0.99 cH₀ for Pioneer;
  - flyby and Tajmar-effect formulas giving "the same results that those obtained by McCulloch" (p. 56), with the flyby corrections correct "with some exceptions" (p. 57);
  - M_dark ≈ 1.1 × 10⁹ M☉ within 500 pc.
- Editorial reading: the equivalence rests on choosing a₀ ~ cH₀ ≈ 6.9 × 10⁻¹⁰ m/s². The paper itself notes this is about 3.5× the galactic a₀ it quotes (2 × 10⁻¹⁰), so a formula fitted to the Pioneer anomaly and one fitted to galaxies do not share the same constant without further argument.

## Criticisms & responses
- **Pioneer anomaly:** the paper cites [Turyshev et al. (2012)](../../03-criticism/2012-turyshev-pioneer-thermal/README.md), "Support for the thermal origin of the Pioneer anomaly", only among "conventional, non-gravitational explanations" (Ref. 87, p. 56), alongside the Bertolami et al. and Rievers et al. thermal studies. It does not discuss how these affect the MiHsC/MOND explanation it still presents as promising. See [pioneer-anomaly](../../02-evidence/pioneer-anomaly.md).
- **Acknowledged in the paper:**
  - equivalence-principle violation (p. 56);
  - the unexplained difference between bound and unbound orbits (p. 56);
  - the gap between cH₀ and the galactic a₀ (p. 55);
  - the lack of experimental proof (p. 56).
- **On the MiHsC side:** [Renda (2019)](../../03-criticism/2019-renda-sceptical/README.md) criticises the Unruh-spectrum factor behind the 0.99 cH₀ term. For the flyby and galaxy claims, see the evidence notes below.
