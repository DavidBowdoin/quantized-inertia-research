# Newton's Gravity from Heisenberg's Uncertainty Principle. An In-Depth Study of the McCulloch Derivation

<!-- local-only-note -->
> 📁 **Local only:** the stored PDF and its rendered figures are kept out of the public repository because this copy's licence (`unknown`) does not permit redistribution. Read the paper at [source](https://vixra.org/abs/1803.0045).

**Citation:** Haug, E. G. (2018, March 3). *Newton's Gravity from Heisenberg's Uncertainty Principle. An In-Depth Study of the McCulloch Derivation*. viXra:1803.0045 (v1). https://vixra.org/abs/1803.0045

**TL;DR:** A 4-page viXra preprint (not peer-reviewed). It re-reads McCulloch's 2014/2016 derivation of Newtonian gravity from the uncertainty principle through Haug's own "Planck-mass particle" framework. Haug argues that the step E = pc only holds for Planck-mass particles, that the uncertainty principle "collapses" into a certainty principle at the Planck scale, and that G = ħc/m_p² (equivalently G = l_p²c³/ħ) is a composite constant. He calls McCulloch's derivation important but says McCulloch "might have a non-optimal interpretation of his own derivations".

## Key claims
- McCulloch's step "E = pc" can only hold for the Planck-mass momentum $m_p c$, that is, for a "Planck particle" (an idea Haug attributes to Lloyd Motz, 1962).
- The relation $p_p = m_p c / \sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2} = m_p c$ requires v = 0. Haug reads this as a breakdown of Lorentz symmetry at the Planck scale.
- At the Planck scale the Heisenberg principle becomes a "certainty principle": $m_p c \, l_p = \hbar$.
- With Δx = l_p, McCulloch's energy expression becomes an exact identity. Generalising Δx to r recovers $F = \hbar c\, mM / (m_p^2 r^2)$.
- Numerically $\hbar c / m_p^2 \approx 6.67384 \times 10^{-11}$, which Haug calls "well inside the experimentally observed value" of G.
- In McCulloch's energy-conservation expression (his 2016 paper, Eq. 6), the ½ "should not be there", there should be a minus sign, and the constant should be zero.

## Key equations
$$ \Delta p\,\Delta x \ge \hbar, \qquad E\,\Delta x \ge \hbar c $$
$$ m_p = \frac{\hbar}{l_p}\frac{1}{c}, \qquad m_p c\, l_p = \hbar $$
$$ E = \frac{\hbar c\, mM}{m_p^2\,\Delta x} $$
$$ F = \frac{\hbar c}{m_p^2}\frac{mM}{r^2}, \qquad \frac{\hbar c}{m_p^2} \approx 6.67384\times10^{-11} $$
$$ G = \frac{l_p^2 c^3}{\hbar} $$
$$ nN m_p c^2 - \frac{\hbar c}{m_p^2}\frac{mM}{l_p} = 0 $$
Planck acceleration: $a_p = c^2 / l_p \approx 5.56092 \times 10^{51}$ m/s².

## Data used
- None. A theoretical re-interpretation using Planck units, citing Haug's earlier papers (Physics Essays 2016, IJAA 2016, Applied Physics Research 2017, and viXra 1803.0038 and 1802.0169).

## Results & predictions
- No new quantitative predictions. The paper argues that McCulloch's derivation supports Haug's view that G is a composite constant and that "all gravitational phenomena depend on Planck-scale physics".
- It refers to Haug's claim that the Planck length can be measured with a Cavendish apparatus "without any knowledge of the Newton gravitational constant".

## Criticisms & responses
- **Venue:** viXra is an unmoderated preprint server with no peer review.
- **Circularity (factual note):** the Planck mass is conventionally defined as $m_p = \sqrt{\hbar c / G}$, so $\hbar c/m_p^2 = G$ holds by definition. The numerical agreement in Eq. 12 does not test anything independently unless m_p (or l_p) is measured without G. Haug anticipates this objection ("Many physicists will likely protest here …") and points to his claim that l_p can be found independently with a Cavendish apparatus.
