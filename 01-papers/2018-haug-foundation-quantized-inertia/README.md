# A Close Look at the Foundation of Quantized Inertia

<!-- local-only-note -->
> 📁 **Local only:** the stored PDF and its rendered figures are kept out of the public repository because this copy's licence (`unknown`) does not permit redistribution. Read the paper at [source](https://vixra.org/abs/1803.0052).

**Citation:** Haug, E. G. (2018). *A Close Look at the Foundation of Quantized Inertia*. viXra:1803.0052. https://vixra.org/abs/1803.0052. There are three versions: v1 (4 March 2018), v2 (5 March 2018) and v3 (2 September 2018). The stored PDF is v3, 5 pages, dated September 2, 2018. Its printed title is shortened to *A Look at the Foundation of Quantized Inertia*, while the viXra abstract page keeps the original title. Haug's affiliation is given as the Norwegian University of Life Sciences.

**TL;DR:** This is a companion to Haug's [viXra:1803.0045](../2018-haug-mcculloch-derivation/README.md), which dealt with McCulloch's derivation of *gravity*. Here Haug looks at McCulloch's 2016 EPL derivation of *Quantized Inertia* itself from the uncertainty principle. He argues that the step E = pc (momentum of the form mc) is valid only for a Planck-mass particle. He reports that McCulloch confirmed in correspondence a sign typo in the 2016 energy-conservation equation, though the result McCulloch used is unaffected. He then speculates that QI's mass-change formula has a "structural similarity" to Haug's own maximum-kinetic-energy formula from "mathematical atomism". As a Big Bang sceptic, he also questions whether the radius of the observable universe is a necessary input. He calls the theory "an interesting theory that deserves some attention until proven or disproven" and does not reach a verdict. The paper is a viXra preprint and has not been peer-reviewed.

## Key claims
- **E = pc applies only to a Planck mass (Sect. 1).** McCulloch's "Now E = pc so" step uses a momentum of the form mc. Haug claims this "can only hold for a Planck mass particle" (an idea he attributes to Lloyd Motz, 1962). He adds that "the Planck mass is unique and this means that the McCulloch derivation likely holds". The same argument appears in his gravity note 1803.0045.
- **The uncertainty principle becomes a "certainty principle" at the Planck scale:** m_p c l_p = ħ (Eq. 4). Haug's proposed maximum velocity for matter, v_max = c√(1 − l_p²/λ̄²), means every non-Planck particle has a maximum momentum just below m_p c.
- **A typo in McCulloch 2016.** McCulloch's conservation statement m₁c² + ħc/Δx₁ = m₂c² + ħc/Δx₂ should carry minus signs. Haug says McCulloch "confirmed that he had made a small typo". The final line, dm = (ħ/c)(1/Δx₂ − 1/Δx₁), is unchanged, so "his typo does not affect the many calculations he has completed". Haug argues that the corrected relation should no longer be read as conservation of "mass-energy plus the energy uncertainty".
- **A suggested link to Haug's atomism.** Haug sets McCulloch's dm formula beside his own relativistic-mass and maximum-kinetic-energy expressions (Eqs. 9–12). He asks whether QI is "connected to the newly-introduced maximum kinetic energy of non-Planck mass particles". At Haug's maximum acceleration, the Rindler horizon d = c²/a shrinks to about the Planck length (Eqs. 13–15). He says this "could at least lead to some boundary conditions on McCulloch's theory".
- **Questioning the cosmic radius.** Haug writes "as Big Bang skeptics, we question his use of the radius of the observable universe". He cites high-z quasar time-dilation work (printed as "Hawkings" 2001, ApJ 553, 97) and asks whether some other combination of Δx₁ and Δx₂ could replace the cosmic radius.

## Key equations
McCulloch's step, as quoted (Eqs. 1–2):
$$ \Delta p\,\Delta x \gtrsim \hbar, \qquad E\,\Delta x \gtrsim \hbar c $$
Planck "certainty principle" (Eq. 4):
$$ m_p c\, l_p = \hbar $$
McCulloch 2016 conservation equation, corrected for the sign typo as Haug reports (Eq. 8):
$$ m_1c^2 - \frac{\hbar c}{\Delta x_1} = m_2c^2 - \frac{\hbar c}{\Delta x_2} \;\Rightarrow\; dm = \frac{\hbar}{c}\left(\frac{1}{\Delta x_2}-\frac{1}{\Delta x_1}\right) $$
Haug's maximum velocity and maximum kinetic energy (Eqs. 10, 12):
$$ v_{max} = c\sqrt{1-\frac{l_p^2}{\bar\lambda^2}}, \qquad \frac{K_{e,max}}{c^2} = \frac{\hbar}{c}\left(\frac{1}{l_p}-\frac{1}{\bar\lambda}\right) $$

- None. The paper is a theoretical reinterpretation that relies mostly on Haug's own earlier papers (Physics Essays 2016, Applied Physics Research 2017, Acta Astronautica 2017, and several viXra and preprints.org notes).

## Criticisms & responses
- **Venue:** viXra is an unmoderated preprint server with no peer review.
- **Stance:** this archive tags the paper `neutral`. It is sympathetic in tone ("excellent insights", "something great") but neither endorses QI's predictions nor tests them. Its two substantive points are a reinterpretation (Planck-mass momentum) and a doubt about QI's cosmic-radius input. The companion note [1803.0045](../2018-haug-mcculloch-derivation/README.md) is tagged `supports` in this archive.
- **Not independent support or critique of the data fits.** Haug's later [2021 JHEPGC paper](../2021-haug-minimum-acceleration/README.md) proposes a different minimum acceleration, GM_u/r_u², and plots QI's curve only for comparison.
- **The typo claim** rests on private correspondence reported by Haug.
  > [!UNVERIFIED] No erratum to McCulloch 2016 (EPL 115, 69001) was looked for or found for this entry.
- **Editorial check (algebra):** Haug's correction is algebraically right. From m₁c² − ħc/Δx₁ = m₂c² − ħc/Δx₂ one gets dm = m₂ − m₁ = (ħ/c)(1/Δx₂ − 1/Δx₁), which is the result McCulloch uses. The "+" form, as printed and as transcribed in this archive's [equations](../../00-overview/equations.md) §2016, gives the opposite sign.
- **Editorial check:** Haug's suggestion to replace the cosmic radius conflicts with the role Θ plays in every QI prediction. a_min = 2c²/Θ sets the galaxy-rotation, wide-binary and Pioneer numbers ([equations](../../00-overview/equations.md)). Haug does not say what would replace it numerically.
- The broader critiques of the Unruh-spectrum derivations ([Renda 2019](../../03-criticism/2019-renda-sceptical/README.md), [Junghanns 2026](../../03-criticism/2026-junghanns-causal-restriction-nogo/README.md)) do not discuss Haug.
