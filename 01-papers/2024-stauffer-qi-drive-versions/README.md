**TL;DR:** A speculative engineering note, "purely speculation and internet research", with no drives built or tested. It proposes a reactionless "QI Thruster": a sealed gas-filled cylinder heated at the leading end (by a Peltier device or heater) and cooled at the trailing end. The author's reasoning is that hot molecules bounce off the hot plate with higher accelerations, and so "should carry lots of inertia", while cold molecules at the rear carry less. The difference would give net forward thrust "if theories of quantized inertia are correct". Variants include stacked cells with valves, and gas compressors or expanders (porous filters in a flowing gas) that "double up as QI thrusters". There is no mathematics.

## Key claims
- Both the EmDrive and IVO's quantum drive "attempt to violate Newton's laws of action and reaction (as conventionally understood)". Detailed testing "seems to say" that the EmDrive does not work. The author was awaiting results from IVO's in-orbit test (as of February 2024) and recommends a NextBigFuture article as background.
- His reading of QI: "at higher rates of acceleration, matter has higher inertia, and at lower rates of acceleration, matter has lower inertia".
- Simple design (section "Simple Basic Design Here", Fig. 1): two Peltier devices set up a temperature gradient in a sealed gas cavity. The gas presses equally on the side walls, and the hot end gets more "QI thrust" than the cold end. The suggested gas is xenon or krypton.
- Variants (sections "Basic Design, With Some Implausible Variations or Features" and "More-Plausible Variations or Features", Figs. 2–4): insulation and an infrared photon-thrust bonus (judged not worth the mass), smooth inner walls with no baffles, and stacked cells that can be switched to pump fluid by "quasi-peristalsis" (which "will negate any QI thrust effects").
- Section "Gas Compressors and Expanders Which Double Up as QI Thrusters": a gas expander, such as a permeable filter with a thermal gradient along the flow, "should give us a tiny bit of thrust" if QI is correct. Layered nano- or micro-scale structures might do better.
- Conclusion: "I, for one, am sure hoping that QI theories (of Quantum Inertia) can be exploited!" The paper has no references ("References (none for now)").

## Results & predictions
- No quantitative prediction. The claimed effect is conditional on QI being true and is not sized.
- **Editorial check (our reading of QI's published formula):** QI's correction to inertia is the factor (1 − 2c²/aΘ). It matters only when the acceleration a is near 2c²/Θ ≈ 2 × 10⁻¹⁰ m s⁻² (see [equations](../../00-overview/equations.md)). A gas molecule hitting a wall reverses about 300 m s⁻¹ in very roughly 10⁻¹³ s, an acceleration of order 10¹⁵ m s⁻². That makes the QI correction about 10⁻²⁵ for both hot and cold molecules. So QI's published formula gives no usable temperature-dependent inertia difference here, and the device's premise is not one McCulloch's papers make. McCulloch's thrust proposals instead rely on asymmetric damping of Unruh waves by nearby conductors or cavities ([propulsion](../../02-evidence/propulsion.md)).
- The author's own follow-up ([2025-stauffer-qi-experiments](../2025-stauffer-qi-experiments/README.md), "Last-Paper Errata") concludes that molecules bouncing off the side walls would take the thrust back: "I will bet that it all balances out, with or without QI effects."

## Criticisms & responses
- No published critique of this paper was found. The main correction comes from the author himself in the 2025 follow-up (see above).
- **Momentum conservation:** reactionless thrust from a closed system conflicts with momentum conservation ([Provatidis 2024 review](../2024-provatidis-inertial-propulsion-review/README.md); [propulsion](../../02-evidence/propulsion.md)). The author acknowledges that such drives try to violate Newton's third law "as conventionally understood".
- **Status (factual):** a self-published, non-peer-reviewed hobbyist note by a non-specialist, listed by its own author on the QI advocacy site. It is not evidence for or against QI.
