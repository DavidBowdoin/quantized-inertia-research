# MOND as a regime of quantum gravity

Read the paper at [arXiv:1704.00780](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.00780) · [doi:10.1103/PhysRevD.96.083523](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.96.083523).

**Citation:** Smolin, L. (2017). *MOND as a regime of quantum gravity*. Physical Review D 96, 083523. https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.96.083523. Preprint: [arXiv:1704.00780](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.00780) (`paper.pdf` is arXiv v2, 25 May 2017).

**TL;DR:** Smolin proposes that, for a small positive cosmological constant, there is a quantum-gravity regime for accelerations below a_Λ = c²√Λ (the paper's notation), or lengths beyond the de Sitter horizon. In this regime the equivalence principle is weakened: m_I/m_g = Z can depend on environment. Two routes give Z = a/a_Λ and hence MOND dynamics. One is Milgrom's scale invariance. The other is a Verlinde-style entropic derivation of inertia using the Deser–Levin temperature minus the de Sitter temperature. He then writes a static bi-metric ("thermal metric") effective theory that predicts a new damping of *radial* motion. McCulloch is cited (ref. [29]) only as one of several earlier authors linking MOND to quantum gravity or horizons.

## Key claims
- With Λ > 0, the de Sitter horizon R and the acceleration a_Λ split physics into two regimes. In the "equivalence principle dominated" regime (a > a_Λ) general relativity holds. In the "cosmological constant dominated" regime (a < a_Λ, Unruh peak wavelength ~R) it may not.
- Weakened equivalence principle: m_I/m_g = Z(environment), independent of the masses.
- Thermal equivalence principle: a static detector's temperature follows the Deser–Levin formula, T_DL = √(T_dS² + (ħa/2πc)²).
- Scale invariance (Milgrom) forces Z = a/a_Λ, which gives the MOND relation.
- Entropic route: inertial mass is defined as m_I ≡ ħ/(λ_C c). Subtracting T_dS from T_DL gives F ∝ a²/2a_Λ, hence a² = 2a_N a_Λ.
- Effective theory: a bi-metric "thermal metric" g̃_rr = Z g_rr for static spacetimes. It predicts a new term that damps radial motion in the MOND regime. This term does not affect circular orbits but may matter for elliptical galaxies.
- Smolin is openly cautious: "It is painfully clear that the ideas discussed here are tentative and unlikely. Surely dark matter is a far simpler and less challenging hypothesis".
- Relation to QI: McCulloch appears in a list of authors (with Milgrom, van Putten, Verlinde, Hossenfelder and others) who connected MOND to quantum gravity. Smolin cites McCulloch's arXiv:1610.06787 and Galaxies 2014. He says his own argument is "on the whole, novel, but in places it overlaps some of these discussions". He does not evaluate QI.

## Key equations
Baryonic Tully–Fisher relation and the observed a₀ (Eqs. 1–3):
$$v^4 = G a_0 M_b, \qquad a_0 = 1.2\times10^{-8}\ \text{cm/s}^2, \qquad a_0 \approx \frac{a_\Lambda}{8.3}$$

Weakened equivalence principle (Eq. 10):
$$\frac{m_I}{m_g} = Z(\text{universal function of environment, independent of masses})$$

Deser–Levin and de Sitter temperatures (Eqs. 15–16):
$$T_{DL} = \sqrt{T_{dS}^2 + \left(\frac{\hbar a}{2\pi c}\right)^2}, \qquad T_{dS} = \frac{\hbar c}{2\pi}\sqrt{\frac{\Lambda}{3}}$$

Scale invariance requires (Eq. 23):
$$Z\!\left(\frac{a}{a_\Lambda}\right) = \frac{a}{a_\Lambda}$$

Entropic derivation (Eqs. 25, 28, 31–32):
$$S_{loc} = 2\pi\frac{L}{\lambda_C}, \quad m_I \equiv \frac{\hbar}{\lambda_C c}, \quad F = (T_{DL} - T_{dS})\frac{\Delta S}{\Delta L} = \frac{a^2}{2a_\Lambda}\frac{\hbar}{\lambda_C c} \;\Rightarrow\; a^2 = 2a_N a_\Lambda$$

Modified radial geodesic equation with the new radial-motion term (Eq. 47):
$$\ddot{x}^r = -\frac{1}{Z}g^{rr}\partial_r\phi - \frac{\dot r^2}{c^2}\partial_r\phi - \frac{2\pi G\rho\,\dot r^2}{\partial_r\phi}\,\Theta[Z-1]$$

## Data used
- No new data. Fig. 1 reproduces the SPARC radial acceleration relation (McGaugh, Lelli & Schombert 2016; 153 galaxies, 2693 points) with permission.

## Results & predictions
- Recovers deep-MOND dynamics, a² = 2a_N a_Λ, from thermodynamic and scaling arguments. The transition is assumed to be sharp (Z = 1 for a > a₀), with no interpolating function.
- New prediction: an extra radial-motion (damping) term in the MOND regime. It is absent for circular orbits and does not depend on a₀. The paper suggests it could affect the radial motions of stars in elliptical galaxies and disk formation.
- The paper gives no quantitative fit to data and no numerical prediction.

## Criticisms & responses
- The paper lists its own limitations (Sec. 6). It is restricted to static spacetimes. It says nothing about clusters or large-scale structure, where "dark matter hypothesis appears to work well". It treats disk galaxies as spherical. It assumes a sharp MOND transition.
- > [!UNVERIFIED] No specific published critique of this paper was located in a brief search.
- **Relevance to QI.** This is an independent theory, not an endorsement or test of QI. Like QI it links low-acceleration dynamics to horizon/Unruh physics and a weakened equivalence principle, but the derivation and the resulting interpolation (MOND scaling with Z = a/a_Λ) are different.
