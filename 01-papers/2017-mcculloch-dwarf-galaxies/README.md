# Low-acceleration dwarf galaxies as tests of quantised inertia

<!-- local-only-note -->
> 📁 **Local only:** the stored PDF and its rendered figures are kept out of the public repository because this copy's licence (`arXiv-nonexclusive`) does not permit redistribution. Read the paper at [arXiv:1703.01179](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.01179) · [doi:10.1007/s10509-017-3039-6](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10509-017-3039-6).

**Citation:** McCulloch, M. E. (2017). *Low-acceleration dwarf galaxies as tests of quantised inertia*. Astrophysics and Space Science 362(3), 57. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10509-017-3039-6. Preprint: [arXiv:1703.01179](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.01179) (`paper.pdf` is the arXiv version).

**TL;DR:** McCulloch applies the QI/MiHsC outer-edge formula v = (2GMc²/Θ)^¼ to 11 ultra-faint Milky Way dwarf satellites, using visible masses only. He compares the predicted speeds with observed velocity dispersions and with Newtonian and MoND (a₀ = 1.2×10⁻¹⁰ m/s²) predictions. RMS differences from the observations: Newton 6.5, MoND 3.6, QI 3.2 km/s. He concludes QI does "slightly better" than MoND with no adjustable parameter.

## Key claims
- The dwarf satellites need hundreds to thousands of times more dark than visible matter to be bound. The paper cites up to 2600× for Segue 1 and 3600× for Triangulum II, and calls this "scientifically unsatisfactory".
- In QI, the minimum acceleration 2c²/Θ ≈ 2×10⁻¹⁰ m/s² is predicted rather than fitted, so the model cannot be "tuned".
- The external field of the Milky Way (the External Field Effect) is deliberately not included. The paper argues that the horizons it produces affect the dwarf's collective motion, not its internal dynamics.
- Random thermal accelerations of atoms inside stars cancel, so the relevant *a* is that of the whole star.
- The formula used (Eq. 11) is the same as in McCulloch (2012) for galaxies and clusters.
- The paper states: "It is not known if quantised inertia is consistent with Solar system data or not."

## Key equations
MiHsC (Eq. 1), with Θ = 8.8×10²⁶ m (co-moving diameter of the observable universe):
$$m_i = m\left(1 - \frac{2c^2}{|a|\Theta}\right)$$

Force balance with an inhomogeneity term a′ (Eq. 6), setting |a′| = 2c²/Θ:
$$\left(1 - \frac{2c^2}{(|a| + |a'|)\Theta}\right)a = \frac{GM}{r^2}$$

Outer-edge orbital speed (Eqs. 10–11):
$$v^4 = \frac{2GMc^2}{\Theta}, \qquad v = \left(\frac{2GMc^2}{\Theta}\right)^{1/4}$$

Compared with MoND's $v = (GMa_0)^{1/4}$.

## Data used
- 11 Milky Way dwarf satellites: Triangulum II, Segue 1, Ursa Major II, Coma Berenices, Boötes II, Boötes I, Canes Venatici II, Leo IV, Canes Venatici I, Ursa Major I and Hercules.
- Sources: Laevens et al. 2015, Kirby et al. 2015, Simon et al. 2010, Simon & Geha 2007/2009, Martin et al. 2008, Koposov et al. 2011, Aden et al. 2009, Ibata et al. 2006 and Kleyna et al. 2005.
- Visible mass comes from luminosity, assuming K0 stars with M/L = 1.95. The stated uncertainty is about a factor of 2.

## Criticisms & responses
- **Equivalent to deep MOND.** Eq. 11 has exactly the deep-MOND form with an effective a₀ = 2c²/Θ ≈ 2.0×10⁻¹⁰ m/s². The QI predictions are therefore the MoND ones scaled by (2.0/1.2)^¼ ≈ 1.14, which matches Table 2. The comparison shows that a larger a₀ fits these 11 dwarfs slightly better, not that the two theories differ in form. (Editorial observation from the paper's own equations.)
- **Speed versus dispersion.** The paper compares a circular-orbit speed with observed line-of-sight velocity dispersions. MOND treatments of dispersion-supported systems use a different numerical prefactor, and they include the external field effect, which this paper omits by argument. See [dwarf-galaxies](../../02-evidence/dwarf-galaxies.md).
- **Revised data: Triangulum II.** The observed 5.1 km/s (Kirby et al. 2015, six stars) was later revised to an upper limit of **< 3.4 km/s** (90% confidence). The earlier value had been inflated by a binary star. Source: Kirby et al. 2017, *Triangulum II: Not Especially Dense After All*, ApJ 838, 83, [arXiv:1703.02978](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.02978).
- **Revised data: Boötes II.** Ji et al. (2016, ApJ 817, 41, [arXiv:1510.07632](https://arxiv.org/abs/1510.07632)) found a radial-velocity binary that likely inflated the 10.5 ± 7.4 km/s value. The paper itself notes (citing McConnachie & Côté 2010) that binaries might explain the excess dispersions in Segue 1, Boötes II, Leo IV and Hercules.
- **Missing comparisons.** The paper does not compare QI with dark-matter halo fits.
- **General QI critique.** [2019-renda-sceptical](../../03-criticism/2019-renda-sceptical/README.md).
