# Is it possible to implement a holographic equivalence principle?

**Citation:** Giné, J. (2022). *Is it possible to implement a holographic equivalence principle?* Europhysics Letters (EPL), 137(1), 19002. Received 20 Nov 2020, accepted 1 Apr 2021, published online 7 Mar 2022 (dates from the Crossref record). https://doi.org/10.1209/0295-5075/137/19002

**Authorship:** sole author Jaume Giné (Departament de Matemàtica, Universitat de Lleida). McCulloch is not a co-author. Giné is a regular McCulloch co-author, so this is not independent work.

**TL;DR:** Giné (2017) proposed a "holographic equivalence principle": a test particle attracted by a central mass is equivalent to one accelerating towards an event horizon ([2017-gine-inertial-mass-holographic](../2017-gine-inertial-mass-holographic/README.md)). This paper asks whether that principle can be implemented through the imbalance of quantum fluctuations around a particle, "in a similar way to the Casimir effect". That is the mechanism McCulloch's QI uses for inertia (McCulloch 2013 is Ref. 5). According to the abstract, the answer is **no**. The fluctuation imbalance alone cannot do it, and the interactions of the particle's constituents with their surroundings must be included. So gravity cannot be explained by spontaneous quantum fluctuations around a particle alone.

## Key claims
From the abstract (paraphrased):
- The holographic equivalence principle states that having a central mass act on a test particle is equivalent to having an event horizon act on it.
- The principle cannot be implemented on the basis of the imbalance of quantum fluctuations alone, in the manner of the Casimir effect.
- The constituents of the particle, and their interactions with their surroundings, must be taken into account. Gravitation therefore cannot be explained only by spontaneous quantum fluctuations around a particle.

## Results & predictions
- A negative result: according to the abstract, the fluctuation-imbalance mechanism alone cannot produce gravitational attraction.
- Editorial reading: QI explains *inertia* by an imbalance of horizon-limited Unruh radiation, a "Rindler-scale Casimir" picture, and McCulloch has also proposed an uncertainty-principle account of *gravity* ([2014-mcculloch-gravity-uncertainty](../2014-mcculloch-gravity-uncertainty/README.md)). This paper, by a QI co-author, concludes that a Casimir-like fluctuation imbalance alone is not sufficient for gravity without extra physics about the particle's constituents. Whether the paper says anything about the QI inertia mechanism itself cannot be checked without the full text.
