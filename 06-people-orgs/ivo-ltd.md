# IVO Ltd

Private US company developing the **"IVO Quantum Drive"**, a propellantless, purely electric thruster based on Quantised Inertia. Founder and CEO: **Richard Mansell**.

## Facts from sources

| Item | Detail | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Location | Described as based in Virginia (Plymouth, 2022); The Debrief (2023) reports a North Dakota headquarters with a Virginia facility called "IVO East" | Plymouth 2022; The Debrief 2023 |
| People | Richard Mansell (owner/founder; CEO); Daniel Telehey, COO (2022) | Plymouth 2022; The Debrief 2023; NextBigFuture 2024 |
| Origin | Mansell contacted McCulloch in 2019 after encountering his work; IVO built on capacitor experiments, developed stacked-capacitor hardware, ran pendulum and high-vacuum tests | NextBigFuture 2026 (relaying McCulloch) |
| Relationship with Plymouth/McCulloch | McCulloch "collaborated with Richard Mansell and IVO Ltd", which took QI-based work to patent status | Plymouth press release, 27 Apr 2022 |
| Funding | Reported as privately funded, with no venture capital or DARPA money | NextBigFuture, Jan 2024 |
| Claimed performance | ~45–52 mN/W bench thrust (company claim) | Satnews 2022 ([07-media](../07-media/2022-satnews-ivo/README.md)) |

For scale: 50 mN/W is about **15 million times** (~$10^7$) the photon-rocket limit of $1/c \approx 3.3$ nN/W (= 3.3 µN/kW) (see [conservation-of-momentum.md](../05-context/conservation-of-momentum.md)), and orders of magnitude above the nN-level null limits from TU Dresden's tests of QI-motivated devices ([Neunzig et al. 2022](../03-criticism/2022-neunzig-laser-resonators/README.md)).

> [!UNVERIFIED]
> IVO's bench-thrust figures, the "1,000-hour stress test" and "100 hours of vacuum chamber testing" are company statements reported in the press. No independent, peer-reviewed measurement of IVO hardware was found. Patent numbers were not located.

## In-orbit tests

1. **Barry-1 (Rogue Space Systems), SpaceX Transporter-9, launched 11 Nov 2023.** Plan: baseline orbit data, then fire the drives to raise and lower the orbit ([The Debrief, launch](../07-media/2023-debrief-ivo-launch/README.md); [Universe Today](../07-media/2023-universetoday-ivo/README.md); [Inverse](../07-media/2023-inverse-ivo/README.md)). The host satellite suffered power/communications failure (Feb 2024) **before the drives were ever switched on**: no result ([The Debrief, failure](../07-media/2024-debrief-ivo-failure/README.md); [Futurism](../07-media/2024-futurism-ivo-contact-lost/README.md)).
2. **OTP-2 (Rogue), SpaceX Transporter-13, March 2025.** According to McCulloch's account relayed by NextBigFuture (Aug 2026), a hardware/command-and-control problem limited drive operation to under about one minute per day; comparing with a reportedly similar satellite, he calculated ~600 m less orbital decay over three months, but called this "not conclusive", possibly with a conventional explanation "including a possible Lorentz force" ([NextBigFuture 2026](../07-media/2026-nextbigfuture-ivo-review/README.md)). No independent analysis of the orbital data was found.

**Status (Oct 2026):** no conclusive in-space demonstration of QI thrust. Mainstream physicists quoted in coverage regard the device as unlikely to work; note also that a satellite carrying powered electronics in Earth's magnetic field can experience small Lorentz and drag-related perturbations that are hard to separate from a µN–mN-scale signal.
