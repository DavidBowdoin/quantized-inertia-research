# Other researchers

One paragraph per person: who they are (affiliations stated only where verified from the paper itself) and how their work bears on QI. "Stance" describes the work, not the person's views.

### Michele Renda — critic
Particle physicist at IFIN-HH (Măgurele, Romania), per the affiliation on his paper. His *A sceptical analysis of Quantized Inertia* (MNRAS 489, 881, 2019) re-examines McCulloch's 2007/2013 derivation, identifies "two major flaws", and rederives the result using a discrete black-body Unruh spectrum, obtaining a different $F(a)$ that does not reproduce the original prediction. It is the most-cited peer-reviewed theoretical critique of QI; the manifest notes that no published McCulloch reply was found. Folder: [../03-criticism/2019-renda-sceptical](../03-criticism/2019-renda-sceptical/README.md). Stance: **critical**.

### Slava G. Turyshev — Pioneer anomaly
Physicist at NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory (Caltech), affiliation as given on the review. With Viktor Toth he wrote the *Living Reviews* review of the Pioneer anomaly (2010), which lists McCulloch's modified-inertia proposal among candidate new-physics explanations ([source](../01-papers/2010-turyshev-pioneer-review/README.md)). In 2012, with Toth, Kinsella and others, he showed that the anomaly is accounted for by **anisotropic thermal recoil** from the spacecraft's own heat sources ([PRL 2012](../03-criticism/2012-turyshev-pioneer-thermal/README.md)). This removed the anomaly that QI's founding paper ([2007](../01-papers/2007-mcculloch-pioneer-anomaly/README.md)) was built to explain. Stance: **neutral review, then effectively critical** (QI not discussed in the 2012 paper).

### Xavier Hernandez — wide binaries
Astronomer at the Instituto de Astronomía, UNAM (Mexico), per his papers. He has argued since 2012 that wide binary stars show a flattening of relative velocities at accelerations below $a_0$ ([2012 EPJC](../01-papers/2012-hernandez-wide-binaries/README.md), [2019 Gaia DR2](../01-papers/2019-hernandez-gaia-wide-binaries/README.md)), proposed a curvature criterion linking $a_0$ and dark energy ([2019 MNRAS](../01-papers/2018-hernandez-bounding-curvature/README.md)), and with Chae and Aguayo-Ortiz criticised Newtonian-favouring Gaia analyses ([2024 review](../01-papers/2024-hernandez-wide-binary-review/README.md)). McCulloch & Lucio used his data to argue for QI ([2019](../01-papers/2019-mcculloch-wide-binaries/README.md)). Hernandez's framing is MOND-like gravity, not QI. Stance: **indirectly supportive of low-acceleration anomalies**.

### Indranil Banik — wide binaries
Astrophysicist (University of St Andrews at the time of the paper), a researcher who works on MOND. With Pittordis, Sutherland, Famaey, Ibata, Mieske and Zhao he analysed 8,611 Gaia DR3 wide binaries and found Newtonian gravity preferred over MOND at 19σ (MNRAS 527, 4573, 2024). A Newtonian result also contradicts the QI wide-binary anomaly claimed by McCulloch & Lucio, although QI is not tested explicitly. Folder: [../03-criticism/2023-banik-gaia-dr3-wide-binaries](../03-criticism/2023-banik-gaia-dr3-wide-binaries/README.md). Stance: **critical (indirect)**.

### Kyu-Hyun Chae — wide binaries
Astrophysicist at Sejong University (Seoul), per his papers. His 2023 *ApJ* paper reports a ~1.4× gravitational boost in Gaia DR3 wide binaries below ~$10^{-9}\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$, consistent with MOND plus the external-field effect ([source](../01-papers/2023-chae-wide-binaries/README.md)); he has continued the analysis in later papers and co-authored the 2024 Hernandez review. The Chae/Hernandez vs Banik et al. dispute is unresolved and is central to QI's low-acceleration claims. QI is not addressed. Stance: **indirectly supportive**.

### Keith A. Pickering — independent extension
Author of *The Universe as a Resonant Cavity: a Small Step Towards Unification of MoND and MiHsC* (*Advances in Astrophysics* 2(1), 2017), which models the cosmos as a resonant cavity with a refined boundary treatment, linking MiHsC to MOND phenomenology. Not co-authored with McCulloch; McCulloch lists it among QI papers. Folder: [../01-papers/2017-pickering-resonant-cavity](../01-papers/2017-pickering-resonant-cavity/README.md). Stance: **supportive extension**.

### Travis S. Taylor — laser-resonator prediction
Author of *Propulsive Forces using High-Q Asymmetric High Energy Laser Resonators* (*JBIS* 70, 238, 2017), applying QI to predict very large forces from asymmetric Fabry–Pérot laser resonators. Vice/Motherboard (2018) describes him as a US Army physicist and reports that the DARPA-funded German experiment was based on his design. TU Dresden's tests of such set-ups found no thrust at the nN level ([Neunzig et al. 2022](../03-criticism/2022-neunzig-laser-resonators/README.md)). Folder: [../01-papers/2017-taylor-laser-resonators](../01-papers/2017-taylor-laser-resonators/README.md). Stance: **supportive prediction, later tested negative**.

### Espen Gaarder Haug — Planck-mass reinterpretations
Researcher who writes extensively on Planck-scale physics. He reinterpreted McCulloch's uncertainty-principle derivation of Newtonian gravity in terms of Planck masses (viXra:1803.0045, 2018; [source](../01-papers/2018-haug-mcculloch-derivation/README.md)) and asked whether standard physics yields a minimum acceleration like QI's $2c^2/\Theta$ that could remove dark matter (*J. High Energy Phys. Grav. Cosmol.* 7(2), 2021; [source](../01-papers/2021-haug-minimum-acceleration/README.md)). Both venues are low-impact (viXra; an SCIRP journal). Stance: **sympathetic**.

> [!UNVERIFIED]
> Haug's institutional affiliation (often given as the Norwegian University of Life Sciences) was not confirmed from a primary source in this session.

### Giuseppe Gaetano Luciano — extended uncertainty principles
Theoretical physicist and frequent co-author of Jaume Giné. Together they derived QI-like modified inertia from extended uncertainty principles tied to a minimum cosmic acceleration and fixed the MOND transition constant (*EPJC* 80, 1039, 2020; [source](../01-papers/2020-gine-luciano-eup-modified-inertia/README.md)) and proposed two models of inertia from quantum fluctuations / asymmetric Casimir-like damping (*Results in Physics*, 2021; [source](../01-papers/2021-gine-luciano-inertia-fluctuations/README.md)). Stance: **supportive theoretical development**.

> [!UNVERIFIED]
> Luciano's current affiliation was not verified.

### E. E. Kholupenko — anisotropy of Unruh radiation
Astrophysicist at the Ioffe Institute (St Petersburg), per his earlier papers. His three-part series *On the possible anisotropy of the Unruh radiation* (*Gravitation and Cosmology*, 2019, 2022, 2025) shows that with finite integration volume, finite information-propagation speed, a definite start of acceleration, or a screened directional detector, Unruh radiation can be anisotropic and non-thermal, with brightness temperatures exceeding $T_U$. QI relies on such anisotropy, and Part I cites McCulloch, but the papers are not tests of QI. Folders: [part 1](../01-papers/2019-kholupenko-unruh-anisotropy-1/README.md), [part 2](../01-papers/2022-kholupenko-unruh-anisotropy-2/README.md), [part 3](../01-papers/2025-kholupenko-unruh-anisotropy-3/README.md). Stance: **relevant/neutral**.

### Titus Stauffer: hobbyist QI drive and test proposals
Self-published two ResearchGate notes, mirrored on his personal site rocketslinger.com: a thermal-gradient gas-cavity "QI Thruster" concept (2024; [source](../01-papers/2024-stauffer-qi-drive-versions/README.md)), and proposed Newton's-cradle and centrifuge tests with an informal null home test (2025; [source](../01-papers/2025-stauffer-qi-experiments/README.md)). He describes them as "hobby writings". He added both notes to the quantizedinertia.com papers list (2025). Not a McCulloch co-author. Stance: **sympathetic but explicitly unsure** ("would simply like to know, one way or the other").
