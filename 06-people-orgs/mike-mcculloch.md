# Mike McCulloch (M. E. McCulloch)

Originator of **MiHsC** ("Modified inertia by a Hubble-scale Casimir effect", 2007), later renamed **Quantised Inertia (QI)**.

## Career (verified from his own published bios and University of Plymouth pages)

| Period | Role | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1991 | BSc Physics, University of York | Lifeboat Foundation bio; AVC/CATIE bio; Foresight bio |
| 1995 | PhD in physical oceanography ("ocean physics"), University of Liverpool | same |
| 1995–1998 | Postdoctoral research, Universities of Liverpool and Strathclyde | Lifeboat bio |
| 1998–2008 | Scientist at the UK Met Office (ocean and wave modelling) | Lifeboat bio; Foresight bio |
| 2008–2023 | Lecturer in Geomatics, University of Plymouth (School of Marine Science and Engineering, per Lifeboat bio) | Plymouth press releases 2018, 2022; Foresight bio |
| current | "Visiting Specialist", School of Engineering, Computing and Mathematics, University of Plymouth | Plymouth staff page |

These bios are author-supplied (not independently audited CVs) but are mutually consistent. Press articles variously call him "professor" (e.g. UPI, The Debrief); his University of Plymouth title was **Lecturer**, not professor.

> [!UNVERIFIED]
> A Foresight Institute event summary (Space Workshop 2023) quotes him as "now unemployed for the first time in 35 years", implying his lectureship ended in 2023. This is self-reported and the reason/terms are not public.

**Oceanography background.** Before QI, his publications were in physical oceanography and remote sensing; his bio lists e.g. *Air–sea fluxes inferred from an upper ocean heat budget northeast of the Azores*, *Seasonal heat and freshwater budgets of the upper ocean in the Northeast Atlantic*, and *Ocean salinity retrieval approaches for the SMOS satellite*. QI (cosmology, quantum vacuum physics, propulsion) is therefore outside his original research specialism; his first QI paper appeared in 2007, while he was still at the Met Office.

## QI work

- Founding paper: [2007 MNRAS, Pioneer anomaly](../01-papers/2007-mcculloch-pioneer-anomaly/README.md). Mechanism: [2013 EPL asymmetric Casimir](../01-papers/2013-mcculloch-asymmetric-casimir/README.md). Applications to galaxies, wide binaries, flybys, EmDrive, propulsion: see `01-papers/` entries with `mcculloch_author: true`.
- He reports having published roughly 28 QI papers at "1 or 2 per year" (blog, 2026). Main venues: *MNRAS*, *EPL*, *Astrophysics and Space Science*, *Progress in Physics*, *Advances in Astrophysics*; several recent items are preprints only.
- Principal collaborators: [Jaume Giné](jaume-gine.md) (theory), J. H. Lucio (wide binaries); experimental collaborators via the [DARPA award](darpa-2018-award.md): [Martin Tajmar](martin-tajmar.md) (TU Dresden) and José Luis Pérez-Díaz (Universidad de Alcalá).

## Funding and industry links

- **DARPA (2018):** US$1.3 million, four-year award under the Nascent Light-Matter Interactions programme, with TU Dresden and Universidad de Alcalá as collaborators (University of Plymouth press release, 17 Sep 2018). Details: [darpa-2018-award.md](darpa-2018-award.md).
- **IVO Ltd:** Plymouth's 27 April 2022 release states McCulloch collaborated with Richard Mansell and IVO Ltd, which took QI-based work to patent status and developed the "Quantum Drive" thruster. He has publicly reported and interpreted IVO's in-orbit tests. He is not described as an employee or officer of IVO in the sources found. See [ivo-ltd.md](ivo-ltd.md).
- **Other experimental collaborations** he describes on his blog ("Five Experiments", July 2020): Zbigniew Komala (Poland), Ryan Weed (Positron Dynamics) with David Barnhart (USC), Jamie Ciomperlik (Atlanta), and a bid won from CATIE. He is listed as a research associate on the CATIE pages hosted by Antelope Valley College. He also describes his research as crowdfunded.

## Public stance

McCulloch argues QI explains inertia "for the first time", removes the need for dark matter with no adjustable parameters, and enables propellantless propulsion; he has said he has "never been able to understand the objections to QI" (Vice/Motherboard, 2018). He has also written that some in-orbit results are "not conclusive" (2026, as relayed by NextBigFuture). For the case against, see `03-criticism/` and [../05-context/conservation-of-momentum.md](../05-context/conservation-of-momentum.md).
