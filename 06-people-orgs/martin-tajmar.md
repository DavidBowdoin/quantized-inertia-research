# Martin Tajmar

Austrian aerospace engineer; Professor and Chair of Space Systems, **TU Dresden**, and Director of its Institute of Aerospace Engineering. Leads one of the most careful experimental programmes testing "breakthrough propulsion" claims (EmDrive, Mach-effect thrusters, QI devices).

## Career (from his TU Dresden CV)

- MSc/BSc Engineering Physics, Vienna University of Technology (1997); MSc Space Studies, International Space University (1998); PhD (electrical engineering, numerical plasma physics), TU Wien (1999).
- 1998: internship, Advanced Propulsion Group, NASA JPL. 1999–2000: ESA ESTEC electric propulsion young graduate trainee.
- 2000–2010: Austrian Institute of Technology (formerly Austrian Research Centers, Seibersdorf); head of the Space Propulsion & Advanced Concepts business unit 2005–2010.
- 2010–2012: Associate Professor, KAIST (South Korea); 2011–2012 head of aerospace engineering, University of Applied Sciences Wiener Neustadt.
- TU Dresden: Chair of Space Systems (CV gives March 2012), Institute Director (from November 2014).
- Author of *Advanced Space Propulsion Systems* (Springer, 2003), https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-7091-0547-4.

## Relevance to QI

1. **The "Tajmar effect" (2006–2008).** At Seibersdorf his group reported anomalous accelerometer/gyroscope signals near spinning, cryogenically cooled rings (frame-dragging-like, far above GR's prediction). McCulloch modelled this with MiHsC ([2010](../01-papers/2010-mcculloch-tajmar-effect-explained/README.md), [2011](../01-papers/2011-mcculloch-tajmar-effect/README.md)). An independent ring-laser test at the University of Canterbury (Graham et al. 2008) found a result consistent with null, and in later work with improved equipment Tajmar's group measured signals about two orders of magnitude smaller and attributed the earlier effects to noise (as summarised in the review by Gallerati, Modanese & Ummarino 2022). So the anomaly QI was fitted to is no longer regarded as established, including by its discoverer.
2. **DARPA collaborator.** McCulloch writes that he "persuaded Tajmar to take part in my DARPA-funded project in 2017" and that an early Dresden result (~140 nN from 0.35 W) helped the project into phase II, while noting that "later tests though have shown less thrust" and that Tajmar "does not wish me to give details" (blog, July 2020). In November 2021 McCulloch wrote that "the results from the Dresden lab were negative".
3. **Published null results.** The Dresden group's peer-reviewed tests of QI-motivated asymmetric laser resonators, cavities and fibre loops found no thrust at the nN level, constraining the QI predictions for those set-ups by at least four orders of magnitude in the authors' words ([Neunzig, Weikert & Tajmar 2022](../03-criticism/2022-neunzig-laser-resonators/README.md)). Against descriptive bounds max(m, 0) + 2σ, the largest-run ratios are 6× to 3,700× across groups under Dresden’s adaptation, not calibrated exclusion confidences (editorial: [A3](../09-analysis/a3-thrust-ledger/README.md)). Related Dresden work: [SpaceDrive overview 2018](../04-experiments/2018-tajmar-spacedrive/README.md), [Kößling et al. 2019](../04-experiments/2019-kossling-spacedrive-balance/README.md), [EmDrive false positives 2021](../03-criticism/2021-tajmar-emdrive-false-positive/README.md), [EmDrive/laser thrust 2022](../03-criticism/2022-neunzig-emdrive-thrust/README.md), [capacitor thrust 2020](../03-criticism/2020-tajmar-capacitor-thrust/README.md).

Tajmar's approach is notable for being open-minded about testing unconventional claims while publishing negative results rigorously; his group is therefore cited both by QI proponents (as a collaborator) and by critics (as the source of the strongest null results).
