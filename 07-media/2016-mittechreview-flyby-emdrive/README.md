# The curious link between the fly-by anomaly and the 'impossible' EmDrive thruster

**Citation:** Emerging Technology from the arXiv (2016-04-20). *The curious link between the fly-by anomaly and the 'impossible' EmDrive thruster*. MIT Technology Review. <https://www.technologyreview.com/2016/04/20/8558/the-curious-link-between-the-fly-by-anomaly-and-the-impossible-emdrive-thruster/> (Accessed 2026-10-18.)

**TL;DR:** MIT Technology Review's arXiv column describes McCulloch's EmDrive paper: inertia from Unruh radiation, photons with inertial mass that changes along the tapered cavity, and two testable predictions (a dielectric should increase thrust; changing dimensions or frequency could reverse it).

## What the article says
- Background: Roger Shawyer's EmDrive, critics calling it a momentum-conservation violation, and reports of thrust from several experiments including a 2015 NASA vacuum test.
- Describes the flyby anomaly (unexplained velocity jumps during Earth flybys) as something McCulloch's theory is said to predict.
- Explains MiHsC/QI: inertia arises from Unruh radiation; at very low accelerations the relevant wavelengths exceed the observable universe so inertia takes discrete values.
- Applies it to the EmDrive: allowed Unruh wavelengths differ between wide and narrow ends, so photon inertia changes as they bounce; momentum conservation then requires a net force.
- States McCulloch's approximate calculation matches the order of magnitude of past experiments; a full 3D calculation is complex.

## Who is quoted
- **Mike McCulloch**: "This is what the EmDrive may be doing." and "This thrust reversal may have been seen in recent NASA experiments."
- No critics are quoted directly.

## Claims & caveats
- Prediction 1: a dielectric in the cavity should increase thrust.
- Prediction 2: changing cavity dimensions or photon frequency could reverse thrust direction.
- The article flags two assumptions many theorists will find hard to accept: photons have inertial mass, and the speed of light changes within the cavity; it calls the theory "hardly a mainstream idea".

## Tone
- Curious and measured; presents the idea as intriguing but speculative.
