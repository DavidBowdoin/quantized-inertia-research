# The Problems of Quantized Inertia

**Citation:** Urueña Palomo, M. ("Manuel") (2025, 18 August; page modified 13 September 2026). *The Problems of Quantized Inertia*. Independent Physics (blog). <https://independentphysics.com/the-problems-of-quantized-inertia/> (accessed 2026-10-19).

**Authorship:** the page byline and metadata give only "Manuel". The identification rests on three pieces of evidence. (1) The site's About page says Independent Physics was "founded by Manuel Uruena Palomo". (2) The post calls the theory it promotes "my Machian MOND" and links it to arXiv:[2410.19007](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.19007). (3) That paper is by Manuel Urueña Palomo and was published as Int. J. Theor. Phys. 63, 271 (2024), doi:[10.1007/s10773-024-05808-3](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10773-024-05808-3), verified on arXiv and Crossref.

**TL;DR:** A short opinion piece by an independent researcher who promotes a rival, Machian explanation of MOND. He gives three problems with QI. (1) McCulloch does not derive MOND's formula or interpolating function from the vacuum mechanism. A quantum-vacuum origin should, he expects, involve Planck's constant. (2) QI's acceleration scale should change as the universe expands. He reports that "Stacy McGaugh has measured" that the Tully–Fisher law and a₀ are unchanged out to z ≈ 2.5, and that "Stacy claims that Quantized inertia is ruled out". (3) QI has no external-field effect and imposes a minimum acceleration, whereas MOND is said to be tested down to ~0.1 a₀. The tone is respectful: "the intention behind Quantized Inertia is good".

## What the article says
- **Description of QI:** QI "attempts to phenomenologically explain the success of" MOND. The two interpolating functions "are not exactly the same but behave very similarly". In QI, a₀ "is a varying parameter with the radius of the comoving horizon". McCulloch treats that radius as a horizon that limits Unruh radiation.
- **No derivation of MOND:** "As far as I know, McCulloch does not derive MOND's expression or its function from his proposed quantum vacuum mechanism". The author adds that this is "not a problem, since no one has been able to derive MOND from some fundamental principle".
- **Missing ħ:** he "would expect Planck's constant to appear in the formulation if MOND had an origin in the quantum vacuum".
- **Redshift evolution:** if a₀ depended on the size of the universe, high-z galaxies would show larger mass discrepancies and "overall faster spins". He notes that McCulloch claims this is seen in a few high-z galaxies, citing McCulloch's DemystiCon '25 talk on the DemystifySci podcast ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dN0Keg6faTY)). He expects the "disagreement between McGaugh and McCulloch" to be settled by better high-z rotation curves "in the next years".
- **External-field effect and minimum acceleration:** QI is claimed to have no external-field effect, which MOND needs for apparently dark-matter-free systems. QI also has a minimum acceleration, while MOND "is claimed to have been tested deep into low accelerations, as low as 0.1*a0".
- **The author's alternative:** in his Machian MOND, a₀ is the gravitational field intensity of the observable universe, GM_u/R_u². This need not evolve with expansion, though he lists uncertainties (the Hubble tension, relativistic contributions, missing mass in clusters).
- **Milgrom:** the author notes that Milgrom also favours a quantum-vacuum, modified-inertia origin for MOND, and links another post explaining why he disagrees with both.

## Claims & caveats
- **Attribution check (editorial):** the linked McGaugh post does **not** mention Quantised Inertia or McCulloch. It says that the absence of BTFR evolution to z ≈ 2.5 excludes a₀ evolving as a₀ ~ cH. So "Stacy claims that Quantized inertia is ruled out" is this author's inference, not something McGaugh wrote in that post. The inference is reasonable for QI's 2017 Θ₀/(1+z) prescription (see the editorial check in [2025-mcgaugh-kinematics-high-redshift](../2025-mcgaugh-kinematics-high-redshift/README.md)), but it is not a quotation.
- "Measured" is also loose. The BTFR comparison uses data from Nestor Shachar et al. (2023), plotted and discussed in McGaugh et al. (2024).
- **The external-field claim is consistent with QI's own literature.** McCulloch & Lucio (2019) explicitly reject a MOND-like external-field effect for wide binaries ([2019-mcculloch-wide-binaries](../../01-papers/2019-mcculloch-wide-binaries/README.md); also noted by [Junghanns 2026](../../03-criticism/2026-junghanns-causal-restriction-nogo/README.md)).
- **Editorial check on "no ħ":** ħ does appear in McCulloch's uncertainty-principle derivations, for example ΔE = (ħa/2πc)(1 − 2c²/aΘ) in the 2020 horizon-width version. It is absent from the final results m_i = m(1 − 2c²/aΘ) and a_min = 2c²/Θ (see [equations](../../00-overview/equations.md)). The author's point therefore applies to QI's observable formulae, not to every step of its derivations.
- The "0.1 a₀" figure is given without a source.
