# Gyroscope's unexplained acceleration may be due to modified inertia

**Citation:** Lisa Zyga (2011-07-26). *Gyroscope's unexplained acceleration may be due to modified inertia*. Phys.org (PhysOrg.com). <https://phys.org/news/2011-07-gyroscope-unexplained-due-inertia.html> (Accessed 2026-10-19 via Wayback Machine snapshot; phys.org itself returned a bot-check page.)

**TL;DR:** Phys.org feature on McCulloch's 2011 EPL paper "The Tajmar effect from quantised inertia". MiHsC is said to reproduce the small acceleration of laser gyroscopes near spinning, supercooled rings, which Tajmar's group reported at Seibersdorf. The article states that the effect had been seen in only that one laboratory.

## What the article says
- The Tajmar effect: found in 2007 by Martin Tajmar's group (Austrian Institute of Technology, Seibersdorf). A laser gyroscope near a ring cooled to 5 K accelerates slightly in the ring's direction of rotation. "So far, the effect has only been observed in this one laboratory."
- McCulloch's explanation: the gyroscope's inertial mass is set by Unruh radiation, limited by a Hubble-scale Casimir effect. Cooling makes it sensitive to the tiny accelerations of the fixed stars, which lowers its inertial mass. A spinning ring raises it again, and conserving momentum makes the gyro follow the ring.
- Numbers: predicted gyro/ring acceleration ratio about 2.67 × 10⁻⁸ clockwise and about half that anticlockwise. Observed: about 3 × 10⁻⁸ clockwise and half that anticlockwise. The article says MiHsC has no adjustable parameters.
- Predictions: in the southern hemisphere the anticlockwise effect should be the larger. Reducing the ring mass by a factor of 10,000 should make the effect fall off with distance. McCulloch hopes Tajmar's group will try lighter rings.
- Links the Tajmar effect to the flyby and Pioneer anomalies, which McCulloch had addressed in earlier papers.

## Who is quoted
- **Mike McCulloch** (to PhysOrg.com): on how laser gyros work; that "If MiHsC is correct, then the EP is only an approximation", with implications for general relativity and galaxy-edge orbits; and that knowing the cause may make inertia controllable, raising the possibility of moving objects by generating Unruh radiation (citing his EPL 90, 29001 paper).
- No outside scientist (including Tajmar) is quoted.

## Claims & caveats
- The single-laboratory status of the effect is the article's main caveat. Otherwise it reports McCulloch's claims without critical comment.

## Tone
- Explanatory and favourable to the model. Little scepticism.

## Notes
- Editorial check: the archive records that McCulloch's 2010 Tajmar paper predicted a ratio of −1.78 ± 0.25 × 10⁻⁸ in a different reference frame. The 2011 revision, reported here, replaced its southern-hemisphere prediction ([lab-tests](../../02-evidence/lab-tests.md)). The article does not mention this.

## Criticisms & responses
- The archive treats the Tajmar effect as not established. It has not been replicated by an independent laboratory, and a Canterbury ring-laser search (Graham et al. 2008) found no signal (see [lab-tests](../../02-evidence/lab-tests.md)).
- Later Dresden experiments by Tajmar's group on QI-motivated thrust found null results: [2020-tajmar-capacitor-thrust](../../03-criticism/2020-tajmar-capacitor-thrust/README.md), [2022-neunzig-laser-resonators](../../03-criticism/2022-neunzig-laser-resonators/README.md).
