# A new way to launch rockets without fuel?

**Citation:** Paul Scott Anderson (2018-09-21). *A new way to launch rockets without fuel?*. EarthSky. <https://earthsky.org/space/rocket-thrust-quantized-inertia-qi-darpa-funding> (Accessed 2026-10-18.)

**TL;DR:** EarthSky report on the US$1.3 million, four-year DARPA (Nascent Light-Matter Interactions) award to McCulloch to model and then test QI-based thrust with TU Dresden and the University of Alcalá.

## What the article says
- QI described as a "controversial but fascinating" theory, first proposed by McCulloch in 2007, in which thrust could come from Unruh-radiation asymmetries rather than propellant.
- Funding: $1.3M over four years from DARPA's Nascent Light-Matter Interactions programme; first step a predictive theoretical model, then experiments with TU Dresden (Germany) and University of Alcalá (Spain).
- Mentions QI's claimed galaxy-rotation prediction without dark matter and McCulloch's 2016 proposal to test QI on the EmDrive, noting many scientists consider the EmDrive impossible.

## Who is quoted
- **Mike McCulloch** (from the university release): "Ultimately, what this could mean is you would need no propellant to launch a satellite."; "I believe QI could be a real game changer for space science."

## Claims & caveats
- No experimental results; early-stage research. Closes with "if it works".

## Tone
- Enthusiastic and forward-looking, with brief caveats.

## Notes
- The batch title ("DARPA funding for quantized inertia rocket thrust research") reflects the URL slug; the published headline is "A new way to launch rockets without fuel?". The article misspells McCulloch's name in places.

## Criticisms & responses
- Laser-resonator and capacitor experiments at TU Dresden (the experimental partner named in the DARPA project) reported no thrust attributable to QI: [2022-neunzig-laser-resonators](../../03-criticism/2022-neunzig-laser-resonators/README.md); see also [2020-tajmar-capacitor-thrust](../../03-criticism/2020-tajmar-capacitor-thrust/README.md).
