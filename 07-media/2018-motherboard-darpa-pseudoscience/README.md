# DARPA Is Researching Quantized Inertia, a Theory of Physics Many Think Is Pseudoscience

**Citation:** Daniel Oberhaus (2018-10-02). *DARPA Is Researching Quantized Inertia, a Theory of Physics Many Think Is Pseudoscience*. Motherboard (Vice). <https://www.vice.com/en/article/7x3ed9/darpa-is-researching-quantized-inertia-a-theory-of-physics-many-think-is-pseudoscience> (Accessed 2026-10-18.)

**TL;DR:** The most substantive press treatment of the DARPA award: explains QI, the planned Dresden and Alcalá experiments, and gives space to McCulloch and to critics (Koberlein, James Woodward, an anonymous physicist) who regard QI as fringe or pseudoscience.

## What the article says
- DARPA's $1.3M contract funds the first lab tests of QI over about four years (roughly 1.5 years theory, then experiments).
- Explains QI: inertia from Unruh radiation and horizons; explains galaxy rotation without dark matter; decouples inertial from gravitational mass (abandoning the equivalence principle).
- Planned experiments as described: a German setup bouncing laser light between asymmetric mirrors (10 W input, target of order 1 N peak thrust as reported) and a Spanish fibre-optic loop on a superconducting track (2 W laser, predicted thrust about 0.001 N).
- Notes physicists disagree on whether the Unruh effect has been observed, and that attempts to replicate NASA EmDrive results had failed.

## Who is quoted
- **Mike McCulloch**: "No one has ever shown any evidence against the theory."; says dark-matter proponents "have been on the slide into pseudoscience for decades"; argues QI is "a quantum mechanical effect", not electromagnetic.
- **Brian Koberlein** (RIT, via his Forbes article): QI's EmDrive version violates Newton's third law and relativity.
- **James Woodward** (Cal State Fullerton, Mach-effect thrusters): "The prospect that his scheme will lead to a breakthrough in propulsion is quite bleak" ... "But DARPA is in the business of funding highly improbable schemes."
- **Anonymous physicist** (on background): QI is "a concatenation of buzz words and bullshit".

## Claims & caveats
- McCulloch's response to Koberlein: the critic "has not understood my papers" (from his blog rebuttal).

## Tone
- Sceptical but balanced; gives both sides with an irreverent edge (section header "Some Experiments for the Haters").
