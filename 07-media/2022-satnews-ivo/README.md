# IVO Ltd. introduces a propulsion first: a pure electric thruster for satellites

**Citation:** SatNews (2022-04-12). *IVO Ltd. introduces a propulsion first: a pure electric thruster for satellites*. SatNews. <https://satnews.com/2022/04/12/ivo-ltd-introduces-a-propulsion-first-a-pure-electric-thruster-for-satellites/> (Accessed 2026-10-18.)

**TL;DR:** Company announcement (relayed by SatNews) of IVO Ltd.'s "Quantum Drive", a propellant-free electric thruster based on quantized inertia, claiming 45 mN of thrust from 1 W in thermal-vacuum testing with E-Labs.

## What the article says
- Claimed figures: 45 mN thrust at 1 W input (i.e. ~45 mN/W), zero propellant; thermal-vacuum testing at 9×10⁻⁶ Torr and -100 °C to 100 °C with E-Labs (Fredericksburg, Virginia).
- Drive described as modular, scalable along multiple axes, mountable inside a spacecraft; customer development programmes to begin Q2 2022.
- QI named as the basis (also called "Quantum Inertia"); McCulloch not named.

## Who is quoted
- **Daniel Telehey** (COO): the drive "makes it possible to measure a spacecraft's lifespan in decades instead of a few years".
- **Richard Mansell** (CEO/inventor): says he found in 2021 that pure electric thrust is viable from math and test data.
- **Paul Cejas** (Technology Integration Manager): lower power needs could shrink solar arrays and spacecraft.

## Claims & caveats
- All numbers are company claims; no measurement method, error bars or independent verification are given. Later IVO material (2023) cites up to 52 mN/W.

## Tone
- Promotional, close to a press release; no sceptical voices.

## Notes
- Batch URL (news.satnews.com) redirects to satnews.com. No author byline.

## Criticisms & responses
- No independent, peer-reviewed measurement of IVO Quantum Drive thrust has been published (as of the sources reviewed). Lab tests of related capacitor-based QI thrust claims at TU Dresden found no thrust beyond artefacts: [2020-tajmar-capacitor-thrust](../../03-criticism/2020-tajmar-capacitor-thrust/README.md).
- Andrew Higgins (McGill) argues any propellantless electromagnetic drive exceeding about 3.33 µN/kW could be turned into a perpetual-motion machine (quoted in [2023-universetoday-ivo](../2023-universetoday-ivo/README.md)).
