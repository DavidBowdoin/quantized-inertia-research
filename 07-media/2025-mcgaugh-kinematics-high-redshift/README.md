# Kinematics suggest large masses for high redshift galaxies

**Citation:** McGaugh, S. (2025, 10 February). *Kinematics suggest large masses for high redshift galaxies*. Triton Station (blog). <https://tritonstation.com/2025/02/10/kinematics-suggest-large-masses-for-high-redshift-galaxies/> (accessed 2026-10-19).

**TL;DR:** McGaugh (Case Western Reserve University; a SPARC co-author) presents this as the last of a blog series on his peer-reviewed paper McGaugh, Schombert, Lelli & Franck (2024). He argues that the rotation speeds and scaling relations of high-z galaxies show that a lot of *mass*, not just light, was in place early, as MOND predicted. From the RC100 sample (Nestor Shachar et al. 2023) he sees no perceptible evolution in the baryonic Tully–Fisher relation (BTFR) out to z ≈ 2.5. If MOND is the physics, he says, this implies that a₀ is constant, and it "already excludes" a₀ evolving with the expansion rate as a₀ ~ cH. **The post does not mention Quantised Inertia, MiHsC or McCulloch** (checked against the post body and comments, 2026-10-19). Its bearing on QI is indirect (see the editorial check below). Others have read it as ruling out QI, for example [2025-uruena-problems-quantized-inertia](../2025-uruena-problems-quantized-inertia/README.md), and McCulloch replied to that reading.

## What the article says
- **Individual cases:** high-z galaxies have flat rotation curves like local ones. DLA0817g (Neeleman et al. 2020) has V_flat = 272 km/s at z = 4.26. Other massive, dynamically cold discs are reported at z ≈ 5–6. McGaugh says these contradict ΛCDM expectations of small, dynamically hot early discs.
- **Scaling relations:** Fig. 9 of McGaugh et al. (2024) shows the BTFR and the dark-matter-fraction–surface-brightness relation. It uses local data (Lelli et al. 2019; SPARC) and RC100 data in three redshift bins (0.6–1.22, 1.22–2.14, 2.14–2.53). Both relations are "coincident with those at higher redshift … within any sober assessment of the uncertainties". McGaugh adds that "exactly what we measure and how matters at this level". He declines to judge consistency with ΛCDM or MOND because "the data simply aren't good enough for that yet".
- **His own residual check (blog-only):** McGaugh fits a line to the RC100 BTFR and plots the residuals against redshift. "The data scatter around a constant value as function of redshift: there is no perceptible evolution." No numerical bound or uncertainty on the evolution is given in the post.
- **On a₀:** if MOND is right, the lack of evolution implies a constant a₀. There is "some wiggle room in that given all the uncertainties", but the result "already excludes the picture in which a0 evolves with the expansion rate through the coincidence a0 ~ cH0". A link to the cosmological constant (a₀ ~ c²Λ^½) "remains interesting since the cosmological constant is constant".
- **A dissenting hint:** Amvrosiadis et al. (2025, MNRAS 536, 3757) find a hint of an offset in the BTFR normalisation that is "less than 2σ". McGaugh calls this "intriguing" and notes that it needs careful checking of the assumptions.
- **Clusters:** candidate protoclusters (Franck & McGaugh 2016) have velocity dispersions about twice those of matched mock structures in the Millennium simulation.

## Underlying peer-reviewed sources (identifiers verified on Crossref and arXiv, 2026-10-19)
- **McGaugh, S. S., Schombert, J. M., Lelli, F. & Franck, J. (2024).** *Accelerated Structure Formation: The Early Emergence of Massive Galaxies and Clusters of Galaxies*. ApJ 976(1), 13. https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-4357/ad834d, arXiv:[2406.17930](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17930), CC BY 4.0. Sect. 5 ("Galaxy Kinematics") and its Fig. 9 are the basis for the BTFR claim. The paper says there is "little if any indication of evolution in either relation up to z ≈ 2.5". A text search of the arXiv v2 PDF finds no mention of Quantised Inertia or McCulloch, and no explicit discussion of a time-varying a₀; its MOND structure-formation model assumes a constant a₀ = 1.2 × 10⁻¹⁰ m s⁻². **The statement that a₀ ∝ H(z) is excluded appears in the blog post, not in the paper.**
- **Nestor Shachar, A. et al. (2023).** *RC100: Rotation Curves of 100 Massive Star-forming Galaxies at z = 0.6–2.5 Reveal Little Dark Matter on Galactic Scales*. ApJ 944(1), 78. https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-4357/aca9cf, CC BY 4.0. This is the data set. It is a ΛCDM-framed dynamical analysis of dark-matter fractions and does not test a₀ evolution.
- **Amvrosiadis, A. et al. (2025).** *The kinematics of massive high-redshift dusty star-forming galaxies*. MNRAS 536(4), 3757–3783. https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/stae2760. This paper reports the < 2σ BTFR offset hint.

## Claims & caveats
- The post's own caveats: "it remains early days" for high-z kinematics; differences in velocity definitions and stellar-population models between studies matter; and the < 2σ Amvrosiadis offset is still to be assessed.

## Tone
- An advocate of MOND writing on his own blog. He is sharply critical of ΛCDM's account of early galaxy formation, careful about the data's limitations, and says nothing about QI.

## Notes
- **Editorial check (our arithmetic):**
  - In [McCulloch 2017](../../01-papers/2017-mcculloch-galaxy-rotations-sparc/README.md), QI uses Θ(z) = Θ₀/(1+z), so v⁴ = 2GMc²(1+z)/Θ₀. At fixed rotation speed the baryonic mass should then be lower by log₁₀(1+z) dex. That is −0.52 dex at z = 2.3, the figure McCulloch himself gives, and −0.54 dex at z = 2.5.
  - The a₀ ∝ H(z) case that McGaugh calls excluded gives, for flat ΛCDM with Ω_m = 0.3, H(2.5)/H₀ ≈ 3.7, or about −0.57 dex.
  - So QI's 2017 prescription predicts a BTFR shift of about the same size as the scenario McGaugh says is "readily perceptible" and absent. On McGaugh's reading of RC100, QI's redshift prediction is disfavoured. Its fate depends on the same caveats he lists.
  - Junghanns (2026) points out that other Θ(t) definitions used in the QI literature would predict different histories, and a fixed-Θ version would predict no evolution at all ([2026-junghanns-causal-restriction-nogo](../../03-criticism/2026-junghanns-causal-restriction-nogo/README.md)).
- **Conflicting data already in this archive:** McCulloch 2017 claimed support from Übler et al. (2017, KMOS), who found the Tully–Fisher zero point *evolving* by −0.2 to −0.47 dex at z ≈ 2.3 (see [galaxy-rotation](../../02-evidence/galaxy-rotation.md)). McGaugh's post does not discuss Übler et al. Which result holds appears to depend on how mass and velocity are defined, which is the point McGaugh's caveat raises.
- **McCulloch's reply (blog, 30 Aug 2026), under "Changing Universe Problem":** "Apparently Prof Stacy McGaugh has said that if the QI minimum acceleration was true then galaxy rotation speeds would vary in time and they don't. Well, this was the point of my 2017 paper on galaxy rotation." He says QI "indeed predicts that early galaxies should spin faster for the same mass and there is now evidence that that does happen". He cites his 2017 paper and JWST's "Little Red Dots" ([blog](https://physicsfromtheedge.blogspot.com/2026/08/responding-to-criticisms.html)). He does not address the RC100 / McGaugh et al. 2024 BTFR result specifically.
