# Dark matter effect might be explained by modified way to calculate inertial mass

**Citation:** Lisa Zyga (2012-09-18). *Dark matter effect might be explained by modified way to calculate inertial mass*. Phys.org. <https://phys.org/news/2012-09-dark-effect-inertial-mass.html> (Accessed 2026-10-19 via Wayback Machine snapshot; phys.org itself returned a bot-check page.)

**TL;DR:** Phys.org report on McCulloch's 2012 *Astrophysics and Space Science* paper "Testing Quantised Inertia on Galactic Scales". It explains gravitational and inertial mass, MOND, and how MiHsC lowers the inertial mass of low-acceleration stars instead of adding dark matter. It is built mostly on an interview with McCulloch.

## What the article says
- Background: Zwicky's 1933 cluster observation, flat galaxy rotation curves (Rubin & Ford), and dark matter as the most popular explanation.
- Introduces McCulloch (Plymouth University, described as a geomatics specialist) and says his model accounts for fast outer rotation even though it violates the equivalence principle.
- Explains gravitational vs inertial mass, the equivalence principle, and MOND. It says MOND needs an adjustable parameter, while MiHsC does not.
- Describes MiHsC: Unruh radiation from acceleration, with wavelengths limited by a Hubble-scale Casimir effect, so inertial mass falls at low acceleration. It notes that whether Unruh radiation exists is disputed.
- From this McCulloch derives a baryonic Tully–Fisher relation. The article says the predictions for dwarf galaxies, spirals and clusters overestimate the observed velocities by one-third to one-half, but stay within error bars (attributed to Hubble-constant and mass-to-light uncertainties).
- Prediction: accelerations at galaxy edges should stay above a minimum value.

## Who is quoted
- **Mike McCulloch** (interview with Phys.org, several long quotes). For example: "you can either (1) increase the GM of the galaxy to hold its stars in with more force (dark matter), or (2) you can decrease the IM of the stars ... MiHsC/quantized inertia does the latter."
- He argues torsion-balance tests of the equivalence principle cannot detect MiHsC, because the anomaly does not depend on mass.
- On testing, he says a controlled lab experiment would be the best proof. He suggests weighing an object cooled to about 5 K, and possibly spacecraft tests.
- No outside scientist is quoted.

## Claims & caveats
- The article says "Whether or not MiHsC turns out to be true remains to be seen", and it flags the equivalence-principle violation and the dispute over Unruh radiation.
- It reports the one-third to one-half velocity overestimate, which the paper also states.

## Tone
- Explanatory and broadly sympathetic. Light caveats, no critical voice.

## Notes
- Editorial check: one sentence says some scientists violate the equivalence principle to explain galaxy rotation "without invoking dark energy". Dark matter is meant.
- The lead figure (credit M. E. McCulloch) compares observed rotation speeds with MOND and MiHsC predictions against baryonic mass. It is not stored here.
- Its "Explore further" link points to the 2011 Phys.org Tajmar-effect article ([2011-physorg-gyroscope-tajmar](../2011-physorg-gyroscope-tajmar/README.md)).

## Criticisms & responses
- The paper itself overpredicts velocities, and its constant 2c²/Θ is about 3–6 times MOND's fitted a₀ (see the paper's README, [2012-mcculloch-galactic-scales](../../01-papers/2012-mcculloch-galactic-scales/README.md)).
