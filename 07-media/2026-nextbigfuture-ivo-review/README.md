# Reviewing the IVO Quantum Drive

**Citation:** Brian Wang (2026-08-24). *Reviewing the IVO Quantum Drive*. NextBigFuture. <https://www.nextbigfuture.com/2026/08/reviewing-the-ivo-quantum-drive.html> (Accessed 2026-10-18.)

**TL;DR:** Blog post relaying McCulloch's account of the second IVO flight (Rogue OTP-2, Transporter-13, March 2025): drive operation limited to under about one minute per day, and about 600 m less orbital decay than a comparison satellite over three months, which McCulloch himself calls inconclusive.

## What the article says
- History: Mansell contacted McCulloch in 2019; IVO built stacked-capacitor hardware, did pendulum and high-vacuum tests, partnered with Rogue.
- Barry-1 (Transporter-9, Nov 2023): host power/communications problems; drives never activated.
- OTP-2 (Transporter-13, Mar 2025): per McCulloch, an apparent thrust response, but a hardware / command-and-control problem limited drive operation to under about one minute per day, preventing the planned clear manoeuvre.
- McCulloch compares OTP-2 with a reportedly similar satellite: about 600 m less orbital decay over three months, which he argues is in the range expected for intermittent operation.

## Who is quoted
- **Mike McCulloch** (relayed): the result "is not conclusive and could have a conventional explanation, including a possible Lorentz force."
- Commenters: Brett Bellmore says the satellite "lost altitude, at a rate within a normal range of rates"; others argue the prediction is not yet falsified.

## Claims & caveats
- No independent verification; the comparison satellite, error budget and drag modelling are not documented in the post.

## Tone
- Measured relay of McCulloch's claims with uncertainty flagged; comment section largely sceptical.

## Notes
> [!UNVERIFIED] No primary IVO/Rogue data release or peer-reviewed analysis of OTP-2 was located for this entry.

## Criticisms & responses
- No independent, peer-reviewed measurement of IVO Quantum Drive thrust has been published (as of the sources reviewed). Lab tests of related capacitor-based QI thrust claims at TU Dresden found no thrust beyond artefacts: [2020-tajmar-capacitor-thrust](../../03-criticism/2020-tajmar-capacitor-thrust/README.md).
- Andrew Higgins (McGill) argues any propellantless electromagnetic drive exceeding about 3.33 µN/kW could be turned into a perpetual-motion machine (quoted in [2023-universetoday-ivo](../2023-universetoday-ivo/README.md)).
