# Contact Lost With Spacecraft Carrying Experimental Quantum Drive

**Citation:** Frank Landymore (2024-02-14). *Contact Lost With Spacecraft Carrying Experimental Quantum Drive*. Futurism (The Byte). <https://futurism.com/the-byte/contact-lost-spacecraft-quantum-drive> (Accessed 2026-10-18.)

**TL;DR:** Short, wry Futurism item (subtitle "Dud on arrival") reporting that Barry-1 went silent on 9 Feb 2024 before IVO's drive was ever tested.

## What the article says
- Barry-1 (Rogue Space Systems, SpaceX Falcon 9) had ongoing power problems; contact lost 9 Feb 2024 after more than two months; the drive test never began.
- Relays IVO's claimed 52 mN/W, citing Forbes's comparison of about twelve times the efficiency of existing ion drives.
- Calls it a "reactionless drive" and notes sceptics expected it to do nothing, and that no similar claims have been confirmed at scale or in space. Does not mention QI or McCulloch.

## Who is quoted
- **Richard Mansell** (via The Debrief): "Sadly, we never even got to turn on the Drives!"

## Claims & caveats
- None beyond the reported failure.

## Tone
- Sceptical and wry; remarks on the convenient timing of the failure.

## Criticisms & responses
- No independent, peer-reviewed measurement of IVO Quantum Drive thrust has been published (as of the sources reviewed). Lab tests of related capacitor-based QI thrust claims at TU Dresden found no thrust beyond artefacts: [2020-tajmar-capacitor-thrust](../../03-criticism/2020-tajmar-capacitor-thrust/README.md).
- Andrew Higgins (McGill) argues any propellantless electromagnetic drive exceeding about 3.33 µN/kW could be turned into a perpetual-motion machine (quoted in [2023-universetoday-ivo](../2023-universetoday-ivo/README.md)).
