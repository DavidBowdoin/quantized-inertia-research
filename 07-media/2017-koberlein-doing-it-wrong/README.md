# Doing It Wrong

**Citation:** Brian Koberlein (2017-02-21). *Doing It Wrong*. Brian Koberlein (blog). <https://briankoberlein.com/blog/doing-it-wrong> (Accessed 2026-10-18.)

**TL;DR:** Koberlein's broader critique of MiHsC/QI: Unruh radiation is far too weak at ordinary accelerations and is isotropic, so it cannot by itself produce inertia; the EmDrive extension conflicts with Newton's third law, relativity and Noether's theorem; and claims of superiority over MOND ignore these conflicts.

## What the article says
- Summarises McCulloch's premise that inertia comes from Unruh radiation rather than being intrinsic to matter.
- Argues the Unruh effect is negligible at everyday accelerations and isotropic, so it cannot generate inertia alone; the added Hubble-scale cutoff, cosmic Casimir effect and information-theory arguments are, in his view, misapplied.
- Says the EmDrive version requires time-varying photon mass, violating Newton's third law, special and general relativity and Noether's theorem.
- On galaxy rotation: MiHsC does about as well as MOND for dwarf galaxies (uncertainties around 30%), but claiming superiority because MOND has a fitted parameter overlooks that MiHsC contradicts established physics.
- Criticises uncritical popular-press repetition of blog claims (dark energy, Pioneer/flyby anomalies, free energy).

## Who is quoted
- No interviewees. Key lines: "Thus inertia is an effect of acceleration rather than an inherent property of matter."; "Nevermind the fact that MiHsC violates established physics, while MoND is simply descriptive."; "Because once you allow yourself to ignore basic physics in your theory, all sorts of phenomena can be explained."

## Claims & caveats
- A viable speculative model must address its conflicts with established theory rather than only accumulating explained phenomena.

## Tone
- Critical but measured; accepts that speculative "what if" papers are normal science.
