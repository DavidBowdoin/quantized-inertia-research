# Scientists receive $1.3 million to study new propulsion idea for spacecraft

**Citation:** University of Plymouth (2018-09-17). *Scientists receive $1.3 million to study new propulsion idea for spacecraft*. University of Plymouth news. <https://www.plymouth.ac.uk/news/scientists-receive-13-million-to-study-new-propulsion-idea-for-spacecraft> (Accessed 2026-10-18.)

**TL;DR:** Primary source for the DARPA award: $1.3M over four years from DARPA's Nascent Light-Matter Interactions programme for McCulloch to build a predictive QI model (first 18 months) and then test QI thrust with experimentalists at TU Dresden and the University of Alcalá.

## What the article says
- QI (proposed 2007) suggests objects can be pushed by differences in Unruh-radiation intensity; a shield above an accelerating object could produce thrust; only electrical power would be needed.
- Plan: Plymouth team develops a fully predictive theoretical model over the first 18 months; experiments in Germany and Spain then test whether any thrust is due to QI and whether it can be enhanced.
- No individual collaborator names are given.

## Who is quoted
- **Mike McCulloch** (Lecturer in Geomatics): "I believe QI could be a real game changer for space science."; "Ultimately, what this could mean is you would need no propellant to launch a satellite".

## Claims & caveats
- Benefits claimed (cheaper, safer than chemical rockets) are the institution's and researcher's framing.

## Tone
- Promotional university press release; no independent assessment.

## Criticisms & responses
- Laser-resonator and capacitor experiments at TU Dresden (the experimental partner named in the DARPA project) reported no thrust attributable to QI: [2022-neunzig-laser-resonators](../../03-criticism/2022-neunzig-laser-resonators/README.md); see also [2020-tajmar-capacitor-thrust](../../03-criticism/2020-tajmar-capacitor-thrust/README.md).
