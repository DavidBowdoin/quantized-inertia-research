# Theoretical Dream EmDrive

**Citation:** Brian Koberlein (2016-04-27). *Theoretical Dream EmDrive*. Brian Koberlein (blog). <https://briankoberlein.com/blog/theoretical-dream-emdrive/> (Accessed 2026-10-18.)

**TL;DR:** Astrophysicist Brian Koberlein reviews McCulloch's 2015 paper *Testing quantised inertia on the emdrive*. He credits it with testable predictions but argues it rests on Unruh radiation being real and the source of inertia, needs a varying speed of light in the cavity, and "still violates well tested laws of physics".

## What the article says
- Notes reported small EmDrive thrusts were not yet peer reviewed and are hard to separate from mundane effects.
- Explains the model: assumes Unruh radiation is real and is the source of inertia; extends this to photons, giving them an inertial mass that depends on the cavity geometry; photons changing inertia as they bounce in the tapered cavity implies a reaction force by momentum conservation.
- Gives credit for testable predictions: thrust tied to cavity geometry and a possible thrust reversal for certain dimensions.
- Criticises: model does not closely match reported results; requires the speed of light to change inside the cavity (conflict with special relativity); effectively hides the Newton's-third-law violation.

## Who is quoted
- No interviewees; author's own analysis. Key lines: "the author of the model argues that Unruh radiation is the source of inertia"; "In short, this model buries Newton's third law in quantum speculation."; "it still violates well tested laws of physics".

## Claims & caveats
- QI's EmDrive explanation depends on unverified premises (Unruh radiation real and causally responsible for inertia).
- The predictions are a point in its favour, but the model conflicts with special relativity and momentum conservation.

## Tone
- Sceptical but fair and conversational; says he would "love to see" a working drive.
