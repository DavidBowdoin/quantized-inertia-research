# How The 'Impossible' Space Drive Engine May Work

**Citation:** Jason Torchinsky (2016-04-25). *How The 'Impossible' Space Drive Engine May Work*. Jalopnik. <https://jalopnik.com/how-the-impossible-space-drive-engine-may-work-1772482176> (Accessed 2026-10-18.)

**TL;DR:** Jalopnik follow-up on the EmDrive explaining McCulloch's quantised-inertia account through a long commentary from physicist Stephen Granade, who is "extremely skeptical": the idea would make inertial and gravitational mass differ and needs photons with non-zero inertial mass.

## What the article says
- Recaps the EmDrive (microwave cavity said to produce thrust without propellant) and notes thrust had not yet been confirmed in peer-reviewed work; mentions a forum post by NASA Eagleworks' Paul Marsh that an updated peer-reviewed paper was coming.
- Summarises McCulloch's idea (via Granade): an accelerating object sees Unruh radiation; modes are limited by the Rindler horizon behind and the Hubble horizon ahead; more modes fit on one side, which McCulloch identifies as the origin of inertia; at very low accelerations inertial mass falls, which McCulloch applies to the EmDrive.
- Points to MIT Technology Review's link between the theory and spacecraft anomalies, while noting the Pioneer anomaly was ultimately attributed to thermal effects.
- Repeats popular claims about what a working EmDrive could do (e.g. Mars in about 10 weeks vs about 260 days), explicitly as speculation.

## Who is quoted
- **Stephen Granade** (physicist, the author's "tame physicist"): main source, quoted at length — "McCulloch's theory claims that gravitational mass and inertial mass can be different."; "But without more solid proof, I'm not ready to accept either of them just yet."
- **M. E. McCulloch**: not interviewed; paper summarised.

## Claims & caveats
- Granade: the theory conflicts with the equivalence of inertial and gravitational mass, which experiments confirm to high precision.
- Granade: the theory requires photons to have a small inertial mass, while measurements constrain photon mass to be extremely close to zero.
- Granade finds the Rindler-horizon reasoning unclear.

## Tone
- Humorous and curious but cautiously sceptical; ends by saying the EmDrive is either an interesting dead end, something genuinely new, or a mystery.
