# An Introduction to MiHsC / quantised inertia

**Citation:** McCulloch, M. (2014-01-08). *An Introduction to MiHsC / quantised inertia* (URL slug "mihsc-101"). Blog post, physicsfromtheedge.blogspot.com. <https://physicsfromtheedge.blogspot.com/2014/01/mihsc-101.html> (Accessed 2026-10-19.)

**TL;DR:** McCulloch's own short, non-mathematical introduction to MiHsC. It covers inertia, Rindler horizons and Unruh radiation, the claim that horizon-disallowed Unruh waves produce inertia (a seawall-and-boat analogy), the Hubble-horizon cutoff at low acceleration, and the claimed explanations of galaxy rotation and other anomalies.

## What the post says
- Inertia, he says, has always been assumed but never explained.
- An accelerating object has a Rindler horizon behind it, and sees Unruh radiation. McCulloch proposes that Unruh waves must fit exactly between the object and the horizon. Non-fitting waves are disallowed by logical argument (a partial wave would reveal what lies beyond the horizon) rather than a Casimir calculation, so the radiation pressure becomes unbalanced and resists the acceleration. He says the effect is the right size to explain inertia, linking his 2013 paper, and notes "there's a factor of 2 error in the paper, when corrected the result is within 29% of the Planck mass".
- Analogy: a boat near a seawall, where fewer wavelengths fit between boat and wall.
- The Hubble horizon also disallows very long Unruh waves, so objects at very low acceleration lose inertial mass. This is the new prediction.
- Galaxy rotation: edge stars have low accelerations, so their reduced inertia removes the need for dark matter, which he calls a non-predictive patch. He says MiHsC is better than dark matter and MOND "because there is only one way to apply MiHsC, and that way works" (citing McCulloch 2012).
- Lists other claimed successes: cosmic acceleration, the low-l CMB anomaly, the Podkletnov and Tajmar lab experiments (which he calls controversial), the flyby anomaly and the EmDrive.
- Outlook: MiHsC is not yet fully developed, and a controllable, repeatable lab test is in progress. He predicts propulsion from sudden acceleration (e.g. a fast-spun disc) and a minimum acceleration that modifies special relativity's speed limit and matches the cosmic acceleration.

## Who is quoted
- Author's own post. It cites Smolyaninov (2008, arXiv cond-mat/0510743) as some evidence for Unruh radiation.

## Claims & caveats
- All claims are the author's. The only caveats are self-stated: the factor-of-2 error in the 2013 paper, and that the theory is not fully developed.
