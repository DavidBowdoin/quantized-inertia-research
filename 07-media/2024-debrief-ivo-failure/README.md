# BREAKING: Satellite failure scuttles first-of-its-kind in-space test of physics-defying Quantum Drive

**Citation:** Christopher Plain (2024-02-12). *BREAKING: Satellite failure scuttles first-of-its-kind in-space test of physics-defying Quantum Drive*. The Debrief. <https://thedebrief.org/breaking-satellite-failure-scuttles-first-of-its-kind-in-space-test-of-physics-defying-quantum-drive/> (Accessed 2026-10-18.)

**TL;DR:** Rogue Space Systems lost all contact with Barry-1 on 9 Feb 2024 after power-system problems throughout LEOP; IVO's Quantum Drives were never switched on, so the first in-space test produced no result.

## What the article says
- Barry-1 launched 11 Nov 2023; it completed some of its own objectives (Rogue's Scalable Compute Platform) but had bus power problems from launch through LEOP.
- All contact lost 9 Feb 2024 after about two months; Rogue investigating.
- IVO says the drive did not fail but went untested; configuration will not change for the next flight; Rogue and IVO discussing a second attempt on a 2025 Rogue mission, IVO also seeking other launches.

## Who is quoted
- **Richard Mansell** (IVO): "Sadly, we never even got to turn on the Drives!"; "The overall configuration of the Drives will not change."
- **Jon Beam** (Rogue CEO): called IVO "a great customer".
- **Tim Ventura** (Alternative Propulsion Engineering Conference): likened the setback to early Starship test failures.

## Claims & caveats
- No data on drive performance; the test simply did not occur.

## Tone
- Sympathetic to the developers; notes mainstream physicists dispute propellantless thrust.

## Criticisms & responses
- No independent, peer-reviewed measurement of IVO Quantum Drive thrust has been published (as of the sources reviewed). Lab tests of related capacitor-based QI thrust claims at TU Dresden found no thrust beyond artefacts: [2020-tajmar-capacitor-thrust](../../03-criticism/2020-tajmar-capacitor-thrust/README.md).
- Andrew Higgins (McGill) argues any propellantless electromagnetic drive exceeding about 3.33 µN/kW could be turned into a perpetual-motion machine (quoted in [2023-universetoday-ivo](../2023-universetoday-ivo/README.md)).
