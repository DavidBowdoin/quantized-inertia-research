# A Controversial Rocket Technology Could Challenge a Basic Law of Physics

**Citation:** Matthew Williams (2023-03-26). *A Controversial Rocket Technology Could Challenge a Basic Law of Physics*. Inverse (syndicated from Universe Today). <https://www.inverse.com/science/alternative-inertia-theory-space> (Accessed 2026-10-18.)

**TL;DR:** Syndicated republication on Inverse of Matt Williams's Universe Today preview of the IVO Quantum Drive flight test; content matches [2023-universetoday-ivo](../2023-universetoday-ivo/README.md).

## What the article says
- IVO Ltd. (North Dakota) to fly its all-electric Quantum Drive on a SpaceX Falcon 9 Transporter-8 rideshare hosted by Rogue Space Systems, then scheduled for 10 June 2023.
- IVO claims up to 52 mN of thrust per watt; drive units of 186.6 g (external) and 103.5 g (internal); vacuum testing down to 4×10⁻⁶ Torr at E-Labs.
- Compares with Hall-effect thrusters (25–250 mN, 1–7 kW, typically >200 kg system).
- Background on QI: proposed by McCulloch in 2007 as an alternative to ΛCDM, to explain galaxy rotation without dark matter and the Pioneer anomaly; notes the 2012 thermal explanation of the Pioneer anomaly and the DARPA grant; calls QI "a controversial idea that many physicists view as a fringe theory".

## Who is quoted
- **Daniel Telehey** (IVO COO): "It is the first pure electric propulsion device, meaning it requires only electricity to run."
- **Richard Mansell** (IVO): ground tests showed thrust "consistent with predicted Quantized Inertial calculations"; the drive is not reactionless; "The next and definitive test will be in LEO."
- **Andrew Higgins** (McGill University): "It is trivial to show such a device can be converted into a perpetual motion machine of the first kind." He gives a bound of 3.33 µN/kW for propellantless electromagnetic drives.

## Criticisms & responses
- No independent, peer-reviewed measurement of IVO Quantum Drive thrust has been published (as of the sources reviewed). Lab tests of related capacitor-based QI thrust claims at TU Dresden found no thrust beyond artefacts: [2020-tajmar-capacitor-thrust](../../03-criticism/2020-tajmar-capacitor-thrust/README.md).
- Andrew Higgins (McGill) argues any propellantless electromagnetic drive exceeding about 3.33 µN/kW could be turned into a perpetual-motion machine (quoted in [2023-universetoday-ivo](../2023-universetoday-ivo/README.md)).
