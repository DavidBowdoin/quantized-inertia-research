# The SpaceDrive project – Developing revolutionary propulsion at TU Dresden

**Citation:** Tajmar, M., Kößling, M., Weikert, M. & Monette, M. (2018). *The SpaceDrive project – Developing revolutionary propulsion at TU Dresden*. Acta Astronautica, 153, 1–5. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.actaastro.2018.10.028.

The journal version is paywalled, so no PDF is stored. This summary uses the published abstract and the conference version presented at the 68th International Astronautical Congress, Adelaide, 25–29 Sep 2017 (IAC-17,C4,7-C3.5,10,x38595; 8 pp.), hosted by TU Dresden ([PDF](https://tu-dresden.de/ing/maschinenwesen/ilr/rfs/ressourcen/dateien/forschung/folder-2007-08-21-5231434330/ag_raumfahrtantriebe/IAC-The-SpaceDrive-Project-Developing-Revolutionary-Propulsion-at-TU-Dresden.pdf)). That version was read but not stored. Its abstract matches the journal abstract.

**TL;DR:** A programme overview, not a results paper. In 2012 TU Dresden set up a breakthrough-propulsion group to test, with high-confidence instrumentation, concepts that "claim to be at least an order of magnitude more efficient in producing thrust compared to photon rockets". SpaceDrive focuses on the **EMDrive** and the **Mach-Effect Thruster**, plus complementary experiments. It describes a new thrust balance under development: up to 25 kg payload, nN resolution, a laser interferometer, and the ability to reverse thruster direction to separate thermal centre-of-gravity drift from thrust. **QI is only cited in passing**, as one of the "alternative theories" for the EMDrive (McCulloch 2017, EPL). This paper is the starting point of the Dresden test programme that later carried out the DARPA-funded QI laser-cavity tests.

## Key claims
- "Most of these schemes rely on modifying the inertial mass, which in turn could lead to a new propellantless propulsion method."
- Aim: "to develop an excellent research infrastructure to test new ideas and measure thrusts and/or artefacts with high confidence to determine if a concept works and if it does how to scale it up."
- **On the EMDrive (IAC version, Sect. 2.1):**
  - Shawyer's radiation-pressure explanation "was highly criticized as not being compatible with electromagnetism and conservation laws".
  - "Alternative theories have appeared [23],[24]". [24] is McCulloch, "Testing Quantised Inertia on Emdrives with Dielectrics" (EPL 2017); [23] is Grahn et al. (2016).
  - "however, the community remains highly sceptical on the theoretical grounds of this concept".
  - Experimentally, recent high-vacuum tests, "especially NASA's latest test results by White et al", "revealed that air is not an issue". However, "thermal drifts were especially significant in the latest reported test [29] and possible magnetic interaction with feeding cables has yet to be assessed."
- **On the Mach-Effect Thruster:** "theoretically much better understood". Woodward's linearised-GR/Sciama analysis predicts mass fluctuations. The experiments show thrust of order 1 µN or less, and vibration and thermal effects are critical.
- **Balance requirements:** nN resolution, blocking of electromagnetic interactions, and limited balance–chamber-wall interaction. "Vibration and thermal expansion/drifts are the two most important artefacts".
- **Conclusion:** "Each thruster concept has its supporters and critics and each of them can be attacked on theoretical grounds and possible experimental artefacts."

## Results & predictions
- No thrust results. The paper sets out plans: several EMDrive models of different sizes to study scaling and interaction with the environment, multiple Mach-Effect Thruster builds, and side experiments, including a high-temperature mass–temperature study.

## Criticisms & responses
- **McCulloch and the Dresden programme:**
  - 25 Jul 2020: "I persuaded Tajmar to take part in my DARPA-funded project in 2017. The idea was that if I could get even the famous 'Dr Zero' to say 'Hmm..' then the world would listen." He said an early asymmetric copper cavity "immediately, as expected" gave the QI-predicted thrust (140 nN from 0.35 W), but "Later tests though have shown less thrust" ([blog](https://physicsfromtheedge.blogspot.com/2020/07/five-experiments.html)).
  - Oct 2018: he blogged about visiting TU Dresden to talk on QI ([blog](https://physicsfromtheedge.blogspot.com/2018/10/bozeman-montana-or-tu-dresden.html)).
  - The published Dresden laser-cavity results were null; see [2022-neunzig-laser-resonators](../../03-criticism/2022-neunzig-laser-resonators/README.md).
- **Media reaction to the first SpaceDrive EMDrive results (2018):** McCulloch argued the media were "attempting to debunk the emdrive based on Tajmar's new result". In his view Tajmar did "not yet even have a working emdrive": QI expected 0.19 mN for the Dresden set-up against 0.004 mN observed ([blog, 24 May 2018](https://physicsfromtheedge.blogspot.com/2018/05/emdrive-trial-by-media.html)). Those results are in the Space Propulsion 2018 paper (Tajmar, Weikert & Monette, SP2018_016), not in this overview.
