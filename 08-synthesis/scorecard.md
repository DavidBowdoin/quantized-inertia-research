## 3. Bottom line

1. **QI's best evidence is a numerical coincidence it shares with MOND.** The acceleration scale 2c²/Θ falls near MOND's a₀ ≈ 1.2 × 10⁻¹⁰ m s⁻² and near cH₀. That coincidence is genuine and widely noticed (Milgrom pointed it out in 1983). It does not single out QI over other horizon-based or entropic MOND-like models (see [mond](../05-context/mond.md)).
2. **The fixed cosmological normalization needs a more precise test.** Θ has taken values from 2.6 to 8.8 × 10²⁶ m across papers (a factor of 3.4). With the early value, galaxy rotation speeds were over-predicted by 30–54% (2012). Our editorial [A1 refit](../09-analysis/a1-sparc-rar-refit/README.md) finds that the 2017 value over-predicts low-acceleration bin means by 17–32% **in acceleration**, equivalent to about 8–15% in velocity at fixed radius. A1 and the [per-galaxy A1b fits](../09-analysis/a1b-sparc-per-galaxy/README.md) favor Θ ≈ 1.2–1.4 × 10²⁷ m under the tested assumptions. A1 uses a point-level likelihood approximation; A1b reports prior-penalized fit loss, velocity-error and prior-width refits, and galaxy-influence diagnostics. These are conditional normalization tensions, not model-independent exclusion or a calibrated evidence strength. McCulloch compared binned data with RMS scatter. A1's public-bin reconstruction places his published curve within one tabulated scatter of all 14 bin means (largest offset/scatter = 0.741), consistent with that visual claim; the exact privately supplied bins remain unverified. His papers give cosmological justifications for their different Θ values ([equations.md](../00-overview/equations.md#7-discrepancies-and-inconsistencies-between-papers)); the change alone does not establish that he fitted Θ to galaxy data. Any new scale we select using these fits must be labelled as calibrated and tested separately from his published prediction. Our binned redshift test, [A6](../09-analysis/a6-rar-redshift-evolution/README.md), finds the same pattern at z ≈ 0.5–1.3 in MUSE-DARK data: with freely fitted amplitude, the predicted (1+z) shape fits the four bins well if their bars are read as 1σ; A1's calibrated scale also transfers without refitting. The published normalization is 28–45% too high under the assumed conversion and mass scale. These are favorable shape and calibration checks, not confirmation of the published prediction. The reconstructed-galaxy results depend on mass budget and radial sampling, with broad SED-mass ranges compatible with both no evolution and (1+z) ([A6b](../09-analysis/a6b-musedark-per-galaxy/README.md)). RC100 at z = 0.6–2.5 fits the published law and its free-amplitude (1+z) shape worse than a constant scale, including after adding radius errors and testing error correlations ([A6c](../09-analysis/a6c-rc100-qi/README.md)). Its likelihood differences are conditional on the decomposition and error model, not calibrated exclusion significances. Follow-ups: [A6d](../09-analysis/a6d-mcculloch-2017-highz/README.md) finds a Genzel tension conditional on halo-derived baryons and an arithmetic discrepancy between Übler's peak-velocity relation and the deep-limit Eq. 13; that approximation's applicability is unverified. Rotation below the Fig. 2 reference alone does not violate McCulloch's split-acceleration construction. [A6e](../09-analysis/a6e-sharma-qi/README.md) uses halo-independent masses; a −0.31 dex common baryonic shift improves the published law, illustrating calibration sensitivity rather than establishing an allowed correction. Shared systematic errors remain unmodelled. [A6f](../09-analysis/a6f-rizzo-z45-qi/README.md) implies about a third of the predicted scale conditional on Rizzo's NFW decompositions of six z ≈ 4.5 discs. It has no measured local baseline and does not independently measure slower evolution. MUSE-DARK's halo-free MOND refit, approximately converted to QI's convention, gives the (1+z) shape at two-thirds of the normalization ([A6](../09-analysis/a6-rar-redshift-evolution/README.md)); a QI refit remains open.
3. **The controlled thrust tests reviewed here report null anomalous forces.** They cover laser cavities, fibre loops, capacitors and EmDrives; their constraints depend on the formula and setup being tested. QI is unusual among MOND-like ideas in predicting effects in the lab, which makes it more falsifiable, and so far those tests have gone against it. *Editorial ([A3](../09-analysis/a3-thrust-ledger/README.md)):* Dresden's PS/c or PQ/c adaptations exceed our descriptive bounds max(m, 0) + 2σ by 6–3,700× in each tabulated group's strongest run. These ratios are not calibrated confidence levels or independent device counts. McCulloch's revised silver-cavity number is near resolution. His 40 nN loop number exceeds the vacuum bounds by 45–67× if transferred there, but its Q/winding mismatch and chamber objection remain unresolved; the in-air test cannot resolve it. Our application of 2022 Eq. 5 to T20's capacitor gives 74× its reported 3σ limit, conditional on electron transit, speed, area and Q assumptions not established by that experiment. The 2021 formula substitutes a rate for an electron count and is excluded from force ratios. Positive comparisons are retrospective, not independent confirmation; this alone does not establish parameter fitting or refute each claim. Moddel's electrical-output result is not a thrust measurement and is excluded from the thrust chart.
4. **The theoretical foundation is disputed.** Renda (2019) and Dickmann & Dickmann (2023) challenge the early spectrum and radiation-pressure derivations. In his [2026 response](https://physicsfromtheedge.blogspot.com/2026/08/responding-to-criticisms.html), McCulloch acknowledges the peak-spectrum limitation and points to later uncertainty-principle and information-erasure derivations of the same formula. Those routes need a separate audit of assumptions such as Δx = half the horizon circumference and log₂; their validity is not decided by substituting Renda's reconstruction. *Editorial ([A2b](../09-analysis/a2b-derivation-trace/README.md)):* the printed final expressions share a linear bracket, but the 2016 conservation equation and mass change have opposite signs (a typo first reported by Haug 2018b), and the 2017 linearisation has a restricted domain. The tested choices change P at fixed κ; P still affects galaxy predictions at fixed baryonic masses and G unless an explicit calibration removes it. The 2016 route retains a Wien-peak substitution. [Junghanns 2026](../03-criticism/2026-junghanns-causal-restriction-nogo/README.md), §10 (unreviewed), assesses its extra assumptions without excluding that formulation. Cosmic radius, orbit law and Θ(z) require further choices. The P = 1 orbit laws compared in A2b fit SPARC differently under A1's assumptions. See also A2 in [ideas-and-tests](ideas-and-tests.md).
5. **The mainstream challenges to any no-dark-matter theory still apply**: Bullet Cluster lensing, CMB acoustic peaks, structure formation. We found **no QI calculation** of any of these.

**Overall verdict:** an interesting, falsifiable, MOND-like phenomenology. Its published astrophysical support comes almost entirely from its author, its laboratory predictions have failed independent tests, and its derivation is contested. The high-redshift results are conditional: MUSE-DARK's converted MOND fits favour the approximate (1+z) shape, while RC100 and KMOS3D alone prefer weaker evolution under their adopted models and errors. The Genzel and ALMA normalization tensions retain halo-fitted baryonic inputs; they are not direct no-dark-matter QI fits. The Tully–Fisher comparisons require a check that the deep-limit formula applies to the measured velocities, and ALMA's ratio to QI's theoretical present-day scale does not measure evolution. These unresolved assumptions limit both favourable and unfavourable interpretations. The evidence reviewed here does not establish a robust preference for QI over MOND or ΛCDM. These conditional tests do not rule out QI as a description of low-acceleration astrophysics.
