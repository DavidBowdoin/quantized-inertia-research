# Casimir effect

## Plain-language explanation

Two uncharged, parallel conducting plates in vacuum attract each other. In the usual textbook picture, the plates restrict which electromagnetic vacuum modes can exist between them (only wavelengths that "fit" are allowed), so there are fewer modes inside than outside and the plates are pushed together. Casimir predicted this in 1948. The force is measurable only at sub-micron separations.

An important subtlety: Jaffe (2005) showed that Casimir forces can be computed **without reference to zero-point energy**, as relativistic, retarded van der Waals forces between the charges in the plates; the force vanishes as the fine-structure constant $\alpha\to0$. So the effect does not by itself prove that vacuum energy is "real" in the sense of gravitating.

## Key equations

Energy per unit area and pressure between ideal parallel plates separated by $d$:

$$
\frac{E}{A} = -\frac{\pi^2 \hbar c}{720\, d^3}, \qquad
\frac{F}{A} = -\frac{\pi^2 \hbar c}{240\, d^4}
$$

At $d = 1\ \mu\mathrm{m}$ the pressure is ~1.3 mPa.

## Mainstream status

Experimentally confirmed. Lamoreaux (1997, sphere–plate, torsion pendulum, ~5% agreement), Mohideen & Roy (1998, AFM, ~1%), and Bressi et al. (2002, true parallel plates, ~15%). The dynamical Casimir effect has also been demonstrated in superconducting circuits (Wilson et al. 2011). Casimir physics is mainstream; extrapolating it to cosmological scales is not.

## How it relates to QI

QI's original name, **MiHsC**, stands for "Modified inertia by a Hubble-scale Casimir effect". McCulloch's analogy:

- The cosmic horizon (diameter $\Theta$) plays the role of the plates for the whole universe: Unruh wavelengths longer than $\sim\Theta$ cannot "fit" and are disallowed. This lowers inertial mass at tiny accelerations ([2007](../01-papers/2007-mcculloch-pioneer-anomaly/README.md)).
- A body's own Rindler horizon acts as a nearby "plate" on one side, giving an **asymmetric Casimir effect** that McCulloch identifies with inertia itself ([2013](../01-papers/2013-mcculloch-asymmetric-casimir/README.md)); see also the [2022 preprint on QI thrust as an asymmetric Casimir effect](../01-papers/2022-mcculloch-qi-thrust-casimir/README.md) and Giné's [uncertainty-principle derivation of the Casimir force](../01-papers/2018-gine-casimir-uncertainty/README.md).

Mainstream objections: the Casimir effect requires **material boundaries that interact with the field**. A horizon is a causal boundary, not a conductor, and it is observer-dependent; the analogy is not derived from QED. Dickmann & Dickmann (2023) argue that a Hubble-scale Casimir modification of inertia is inconsistent with classical inertia ([criticism folder](../03-criticism/2023-dickmann-casimir-contradicts/README.md)).
