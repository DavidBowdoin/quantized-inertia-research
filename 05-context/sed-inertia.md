# SED inertia (Haisch–Rueda–Puthoff)

## Plain-language explanation

**Stochastic electrodynamics (SED)** is a classical theory in which empty space is filled with a random, Lorentz-invariant electromagnetic **zero-point field (ZPF)** whose spectral energy density grows as $\omega^3$. SED reproduces some quantum results (e.g. the Casimir and van der Waals forces, the harmonic-oscillator ground state) but fails for others (notably nonlinear systems such as the hydrogen atom over long times).

In 1994 Bernard Haisch, Alfonso Rueda and Harold Puthoff (HRP) proposed that **inertia is an electromagnetic drag**: a charged "parton" accelerating through the ZPF feels a magnetic Lorentz force from the field that is proportional to, and opposes, its acceleration, i.e. $F = -m_i a$ with $m_i$ fixed by the ZPF spectrum and a high-frequency cutoff. Rueda & Haisch (1998) later recast it as the momentum flux of the ZPF seen by an accelerated object, and argued it could also explain gravitational mass. Puthoff (1989) had earlier proposed gravity as a ZPF effect (after Sakharov).

## Key equations

ZPF spectral energy density (Lorentz-invariant):

$$
\rho(\omega)\,d\omega = \frac{\hbar\,\omega^3}{2\pi^2 c^3}\,d\omega
$$

HRP's result (schematic), with damping constant $\Gamma_z$ and cutoff $\omega_c$:

$$
\mathbf F = -\left(\frac{\Gamma_z \hbar\, \omega_c^2}{2\pi c^2}\right)\mathbf a \;\equiv\; -m_i\,\mathbf a
$$

## Critiques

- **Ad hoc modelling and cutoff.** The result depends on a free cutoff frequency and an unspecified parton–field coupling; the mass is not predicted, only re-expressed. Rueda and co-workers themselves noted the earlier treatment was "ad hoc" and that the electric Lorentz-force contribution had been approximated to zero.
- **Where the mass is.** Woodward (2001) argues that most hadronic mass resides in electrically neutral gluon fields, so an electromagnetic ZPF mechanism cannot be the origin of inertia; Woodward & Mahood (1999) criticised the approach, and Dobyns, Rueda & Haisch (2000) replied.
- **Gravity version.** Carlip (1993) found errors in Puthoff's 1989 ZPF-gravity derivation.
- **Neutral particles and relativity.** Inertia of neutral particles (neutrinos, neutrons), and full consistency with the [equivalence principle](equivalence-principle.md), are not naturally explained.
- The programme has not been adopted by mainstream physics, where mass arises from the Higgs mechanism (for elementary fermions and W/Z) and QCD binding energy (for most hadronic mass).

## How it relates to QI

HRP is QI's closest intellectual predecessor: both claim inertia is a reaction force from the quantum vacuum seen by an accelerated body. Differences:

| | HRP / SED | QI / MiHsC |
|---|---|---|
| Vacuum field | Classical EM ZPF | Unruh radiation (any field) |
| Key asymmetry | Doppler/Lorentz force in the accelerated frame | Rindler horizon on one side vs cosmic horizon |
| Free parameters | Cutoff frequency | Claimed none ($c$, $\Theta$) |
| Low-acceleration prediction | None in particular | Inertia drops below $a\sim 2c^2/\Theta$ |

McCulloch's [2013 paper](../01-papers/2013-mcculloch-asymmetric-casimir/README.md) and [2014 book](../07-media/2014-mcculloch-physics-from-edge/README.md) discuss this lineage. Critics apply similar objections to both (e.g. that isotropic vacuum radiation should exert no net force, and that the energy budget is far too small).
